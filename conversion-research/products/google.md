# Google Merchant Center and AI shopping

Research snapshot: 29 September 2026. Native benchmark. The companion [google.json](google.json) contains ten complete idea records, experiment designs, unvalidated prices and forty primary sources.

Google is the baseline a purchase-focused product has to improve on. It already supplies product ingestion, diagnostics, AI visibility reports, checkout validation and transaction analytics. Its position as the operator of these shopping experiences makes it a useful benchmark; that position does not establish merchant profitability or prove the effect of any proposed intervention.

Merchant Center accounts and organic listings are free. Google Ads is a separate paid channel. This research did not establish UCP integration fees or payment-processing economics, and it accessed no merchant accounts or private performance data. [Merchant Center](https://business.google.com/us/merchant-center/)

The current offering is broader than a feed manager. Optional conversational attributes cover questions and answers, documents, product relationships, family titles, variant dimensions and popularity. They can be submitted through Merchant API. Their existence makes "add AI attributes" a weak standalone proposition; accurate relationships and purchase outcomes still require work. [Conversational attributes](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/17085370)

Merchant Center's AI performance report measures organic shopping visibility in AI Mode and AI Overviews. The documented markets are Australia, Canada, India, New Zealand and the US, for English-language queries. It reports share of voice, shopping stages, terms, attributes and products showing, with daily updates and a lag. Insufficient data can yield zero, while a missing competitor set can produce 100% share of voice. These are visibility observations, not purchase probabilities. [AI performance insights](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/17200695?hl=en)

Search Console also has a dedicated generative AI impression report, with worldwide rollout recorded on 31 August 2026. Describing Google AI visibility as wholly inseparable from ordinary Search is now outdated. On 24 September, Google announced multimodal reporting filters covering experiences such as Lens and image uploads. Neither source establishes an order-level attribution service. [Generative AI report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139?hl=en), [multimodal reporting](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/09/web-multimodal-in-sc)

Business Agent answers questions from merchant website and catalog information. Its help page still describes deeper training, performance insights and agentic checkout as forthcoming, so those should not be presented as universally live. Direct Offers belongs to Google Ads and is described as a pilot in the announcement reviewed here. Organic visibility gains and sponsored offer performance need separate evidence. [Business Agent](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/16410382?hl=en), [Direct Offers](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/google-marketing-live-search-ads/)

UCP supports a transaction path, not a ranking shortcut. Google's FAQ explicitly says native integration does not affect offer ranking. Its native flow includes a user completing checkout in Google UI; calling every UCP purchase fully autonomous would overstate the documentation. Program access remains gated. Eligibility help mentions US, Canadian and Australian products, while the integration-hub guide describes a US rollout with Canada and Australia following early next year. Actual account access is the deployment gate. [UCP FAQ](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp/faq), [native flow](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp/guides/overview/native-checkout), [eligibility](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/16837055?hl=en), [integration hub](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp/guides/tools/merchant-center/integration)

| Evidence layer | What is documented | What it can establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Controlled simulation | UCP sandbox requests and production validation | Endpoint behavior and protocol compatibility. No exact consumer ranking replay was found. |
| Live monitoring | Native AI impression reports, product status events and UCP funnel metrics | Observed exposure, failures and transactions under the platform's definitions. |
| Randomized experiments | Google Ads experiments, including Shopping versus Performance Max | Effects for supported paid-campaign comparisons, not an organic AI-ranking test service. |
| Purchases | UCP Buy clicks, purchases, purchase rate, cancellations, linking outcomes and AOV | Transaction outcomes. Incrementality still requires a suitable comparison. |

The UCP analytics page explicitly says its data is not available through Merchant API yet. Google already offers a Python/BigQuery logging reference, so "log checkout events" is existing functionality. Merchant API product reports include attributed free-traffic conversions, but that is broader than AI-origin sales. The `srsltid` parameter also identifies a free-listing tracking path, not specifically AI Mode. [UCP analytics](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp/guides/tools/merchant-center/analytics), [logging reference](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp/guides/tools/bq-storage), [product metrics](https://developers.google.com/merchant/api/reference/rpc/google.shopping.merchant.reports.v1/Types/ProductPerformanceView), [auto-tagging](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/15191080?hl=en), [paid experiments](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12997711?hl=en)

Google's alpha Merchant API MCP service exposes authorized catalog, report and diagnostic operations. That is useful integration infrastructure, but its `list_products` tool is not documented access to the consumer ranking stack. Google's AI-search guidance explicitly rejects claims that third parties can access its internal ranking or AI systems. A custom search proxy can test sensitivity to supplied evidence; it cannot estimate current Google recommendation rate merely by resembling a tool interface. [Merchant MCP](https://developers.google.com/merchant/api/guides/agentic-tools/merchant-data-mcp), [AI-search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

These ten hypotheses concentrate on purchases. They are distinct interventions, not ten names for catalog enrichment.

1. **Allocate repair work by incremental retained orders.** Join merchant order outcomes to checkout failures and deployed repairs, then test whether a purchase-based queue beats the existing backlog under equal engineering hours. The causal dependency is shipping different, better fixes; a dashboard creates no orders on its own. Google already supplies diagnostics and status events, so this must earn its price through allocation quality. Randomize independently managed store clusters and keep urgent fixes available to both arms. [Product diagnostics](https://developers.google.com/merchant/api/guides/products/list-products-data-issues), [status events](https://developers.google.com/merchant/api/guides/notifications/product-status-changes)

2. **Preserve the cart through handoff.** Verify that a merchant cart contains the intended items, variants and quantities after transfer, redirects and expiry. Return a secure continuation URL and disclose unavoidable changes before purchase. Google's Cart API already performs the transfer; the opportunity is resolving merchant-specific failures. Randomize eligible sessions between validated adapters and measure retained orders per transfer start. Duplicate charges, silent substitutions and latency are guardrails. [Cart API](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp/implementation/2026-04-08/cart-api)

3. **Recover correctable checkout failures.** Map legacy address, shipping and discount errors to the correct UCP response so the shopper can fix the problem without starting again. Google already supports structured errors and recovery; the merchant adapter is the proposed product. Randomize at session creation and retain all assigned sessions in the denominator. If retries rise without additional retained orders, the idea has failed. [Error codes](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp/guides/overview/error-codes)

4. **Keep stock and price valid until commit.** Coordinate feed, page and checkout updates with a short merchant-side reservation or quote window where supported. Test by product-family and fulfillment-pool clusters, since shared stock makes session-level randomization misleading. Measure retained orders and lost sales elsewhere. Google automations already repair some mismatches, but its guide says they cannot replace frequent authoritative updates. Holds that starve other channels can make this intervention harmful. [Automatic updates](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/12157888?hl=en)

5. **Carry earned loyalty benefits into checkout with consent.** Connect actual entitlements to UCP identity linking while retaining a guest path. The experiment compares compliant linking implementations; it must not withhold an earned benefit from the control. Higher linking success matters only if retained purchases increase after reward costs and channel switching. Google's OAuth and streamlined-linking capabilities already provide the foundation. [Identity linking](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp/guides/overview/identity-linking), [account consent](https://developers.google.com/identity/account-linking)

6. **Prove that a basket contains compatible required parts.** Build an evidence-backed graph for categories where missing batteries, fittings or adapters prevent a working purchase. Publish supported relationships, distinguish necessities from optional accessories and validate the smallest complete basket against budget. Native `related_product` support means the differentiation is verified correctness. Randomize product families and measure retained orders plus compatibility-related returns; AOV alone would reward unnecessary attachments. [Related products](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/17085213?hl=en)

7. **Bind the exact variant to the purchasable item.** Check that family grouping, custom dimensions, landing selection and checkout ID describe the same sellable configuration. This targets width, memory or finish confusion before purchase, separately from transferring an already selected cart. Google already supports custom variant dimensions. Test whole families together and measure retained orders and wrong-variant returns. More complete metadata without transaction improvement is insufficient. [Variant options](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/17085214?hl=en)

8. **Show the return promise that applies to this item.** Resolve product, country, condition and seasonal exceptions into accurate machine-readable data and a concise customer explanation. Fix misleading policies in both arms first, then test clarity. Google already has exception labels and offer-level overrides. More visible restrictions could reduce gross orders; success requires a useful retained-order effect after refunds, not a prettier policy card. [Return policies](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/14011730?hl=en), [offer-level API updates](https://developers.google.com/merchant/api/latest-updates)

9. **Back delivery dates with fulfillment evidence.** Use warehouse cutoffs, capacity and carrier history to present feasible UCP shipping choices with a recorded basis. Compare specific supported dates against existing accurate broad estimates, holding services and prices constant. Measure retained orders delivered within the selected promise, alongside total orders. The FTC requires a reasonable basis for US shipping representations and addresses delay consent/refunds; this workflow is not legal certification. [Checkout fulfillment](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp/implementation/2026-04-08/native-checkout-api), [FTC rule](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule)

10. **Reduce false declines caused by missing checkout context.** Map documented UCP signals correctly into the merchant's existing risk system and distinguish technical failures from risk decisions. Google already provides those signals; excess false declines remain an unproven merchant-specific hypothesis. Start in shadow mode, then test approved low-risk paths while preserving mandatory controls. Measure legitimate settled orders after chargebacks mature. Approval-rate gains that create greater fraud losses do not count. [Risk signals](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp/implementation/2026-04-08/risk-signals)

None has a supportable conversion percentage from this research. The JSON therefore uses break-even thresholds: for example, an unvalidated USD 500 monthly fee at USD 25 contribution per additional retained order needs twenty such orders before setup cost. Zero or negative impact remains possible.

For checkout experiments, assign before the intervention and reconcile orders through the returns window. For discovery changes, randomize families or independent clusters and use fixed preperiod opportunity weights; conditioning on treatment-generated clicks can hide the total effect. Check merchant-wide purchases so channel migration is not sold as growth. Restrict AI-origin claims to verified transaction/session evidence. Plan duration from power and outcome lag; fifty identical synthetic shoppers are neither fifty independent human intentions nor a purchase trial.

The proxy proposed in the shared conversation remains useful for rejecting broken variants and diagnosing evidence sensitivity. Rank manipulation tests an assumed advantage, not an achievable ranking intervention. Its example percentages are invented. The deployable sequence is controlled evidence tests, native integration validation, live observations and then randomized merchant changes with actual orders.

New integrations should use Merchant API. Content API reached sunset in August, with staged degradation documented from September rather than an immediate universal shutdown. Platform updates will keep absorbing basic features; the durable test for this product is whether it produces more retained purchases per merchant resource budget. [API sunset details](https://developers.google.com/shopping-content/guides/deprecation-and-sunset)
