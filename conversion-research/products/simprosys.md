# Simprosys Google Shopping Feed

Research snapshot: 29 September 2026. Commercial benchmark. The companion [JSON](simprosys.json) contains ten complete intervention specifications, experiments, falsifiers and 28 source records.

Simprosys is a demanding incumbent for a small entrant. The Shopify listing showed 4.9 stars from 4,894 reviews and a 2017 launch. Its product page claims more than 90,000 Shopify merchants, an unverified vendor figure. These support studying its distribution and operations; they do not establish profitability or causal sales uplift. A recent merchant review describes support resolving conflicting Merchant Center feeds and price mismatches. An entrant must compete with that service as well as the software. [Shopify listing](https://apps.shopify.com/google-shopping-feed), [product page](https://simprosys.com/product-feed-app-for-shopify)

Vendor-curated testimonials mention faster syncing, time savings and rising sales, but do not provide controlled comparisons, purchase denominators or refund-adjusted outcomes. No controlled Simprosys study establishing incremental AI-origin purchases was found in the reviewed official material. [Merchant testimonials](https://simprosys.com/customer-reviews)

The observed monthly prices were USD 4.99 for up to 500 published products, 8.99 for 1,000, 13.99 for 5,000 and 17.99 for 10,000. Variants do not increase the count. The first-install trial lasts 21 days. AI enrichment costs USD 1 per 25 products one-time, with separate XML and additional-market charges. Match product count, destinations, support and add-ons before comparing vendors. A more expensive tool is not inherently worse, and a cheaper one is not inherently incomplete. [Pricing](https://support.simprosys.com/faq/how-my-store-will-be-billed)

Existing depth matters. Simprosys documents reviewed or automatic AI enrichment; its current guide restricts eligibility to English stores, named categories and descriptions over 200 characters. It explicitly says later product updates do not automatically rerun optimization. That is a concrete lead for investigation, unlike an assumed absence of approval controls. [AI guide](https://support.simprosys.com/faq/simprosys-ai-powered-feed-optimisation)

It also documents order-data-based delivery estimates, direct-checkout setup, Google automated discounts, server-side GA4 and a dedicated PMAX workflow for low-activity products. The last workflow selects items using impressions, clicks or conversions. None is evidence that simulated AI recommendations become incremental purchases. [Delivery](https://support.simprosys.com/faq/setup-google-calculated-delivery-speed-estimates-from-simprosys-google-shopping-feed-app-for-shopify), [checkout](https://support.simprosys.com/faq/how-to-enable-the-direct-checkout-feature-for-google-free-listings), [discounts](https://support.simprosys.com/faq/google-automated-discount-requirements), [GA4](https://support.simprosys.com/faq/enable-server-side-ga4-tracking-using-gtm), [PMAX](https://support.simprosys.com/faq/setup-google-ads-zombie-skus-campaign)

The destination is a material distinction. Shopify says Google AI Mode/Gemini discovery uses Merchant Center, including third-party providers. Its ChatGPT channel uses Shopify Catalog and sends shoppers to the merchant checkout. A feed-only change therefore cannot be presumed to alter ChatGPT. Native Catalog mapping already supports preferred sources and variant grouping. An entrant should prove that a repair reached the intended surface before measuring its effect. [Google channel](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/agentic-storefronts/google), [ChatGPT channel](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/agentic-storefronts/chatgpt), [Catalog mapping](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-catalog/mapping)

The reviewed Simprosys sources did not reveal an offline shopper simulation, consumer-AI output monitor or native randomized AI-shopping experiment. This is a bounded source-search finding, not proof of absence. Its advice already recommends A/B tests, and purchase tracking exists. Adjacent competitor DataFeedWatch documents title A/B allocation across item IDs with URL tracking, so “test product titles” is occupied territory. Its described alternating item allocation is also different from randomizing shoppers to two versions of the same SKU. [Simprosys advice](https://simprosys.com/simprotips/product-feed-optimisation-checklist), [DataFeedWatch implementation](https://www.datafeedwatch.com/blog/ab-testing-product-titles)

The shared search-proxy proposal is useful for diagnosing sensitivity. Freeze candidate products, change one supported fact, and inspect whether a model notices or uses it. Fifty identical reruns measure model variation; they do not represent fifty independent customers. Moving a product from fourth to second tests an oracle assumption unless a deployable intervention causes that move. The illustrative recommendation ladders in the shared conversation are invented, not results. Public documentation describes multiple data and context inputs, not a reproducible production selection algorithm. [OpenAI shopping documentation](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search)

Ten purchase-oriented improvements follow. All effects are hypotheses; none has a supportable conversion percentage. The JSON specifies denominators, proposed prices and failure conditions in detail.

1. **Expire enriched facts after source changes.** Track the specification behind each generated attribute and invalidate only facts affected by a genuine revision. This targets stale meaning despite successful transport. Compare dependency-triggered refresh with the merchant's normal reviewed refresh using product-family randomization and kept AI-origin orders per family-week. Immediately fix material false claims in both arms. The opening disappears if relevant drift is rare or the engine ignores the changed source. An unvalidated USD 29/month pilot should begin with reproduced incidents. [Documented refresh limit](https://support.simprosys.com/faq/simprosys-ai-powered-feed-optimisation)

2. **Preserve the chosen variant through redirects and cart creation.** Probe the actual selected controls, price and cart line across locales and devices, then repair a reproduced break. Google already requires variant alignment and checks checkout URLs; another link validator adds little. The proposed advantage is testing the merchant's theme and scripts after updates. Randomize suitable referred visitors between supported routing implementations and measure kept orders per assigned visitor. Wrong-variant returns and latency are guardrails. A single durable theme repair may be the better commercial model. [Variant requirements](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/9773429?hl=en-GB), [existing checkout validation](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13945960?hl=en)

3. **Resolve an expressed delivery deadline.** For “I need it by Friday,” combine live stock location, warehouse calendars and available services into a dated quote. This extends existing estimates only where operational exceptions matter. No false countdowns are needed. Randomize deadline-qualified visitors, keeping input burden equal; measure on-time kept purchases per visitor and total kept purchases separately. Local pickup must use a supported storefront route. Failure is either inaccurate promises or simply moving the same buyers to pricier shipping. [Existing cutoff controls](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/16543665?hl=en)

4. **Maintain compatibility rules with explicit non-matches.** A model-number match can still hide the wrong generation or connector. A narrow merchant-approved graph should express supported models, adapters and exclusions from authoritative evidence, then publish facts and verify the resulting cart. Fitment apps already exist; the opportunity is maintaining trustworthy rules across discovery and purchase. Randomize product families and measure kept compatible orders, not merely fewer returns. This is integration-heavy and unattractive without accessible manufacturer evidence. [Existing fitment competitor](https://apps.shopify.com/product-fit-finder?surface_detail=store-design-search-and-navigation-search-and-filters&surface_inter_position=1&surface_intra_position=5&surface_type=category&surface_version=redesign)

5. **Recover stockout referrals while preserving hard constraints.** Acknowledge the unavailable item, then show in-stock alternatives that satisfy the known size, material, budget or model requirement. Explain differences and let the shopper choose; never silently substitute. Shopify already provides stock-aware recommendations. Test this against the merchant's configured recommendations among randomized stockout arrivals. Count any kept purchase per visitor across the whole store, so SKU switching is not misreported as incremental demand. The proposal fails if the unavailable variant was essential. [Native recommendations](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/storefront-search/search-and-discovery-recommendations)

6. **Connect the displayed offer to a destination-specific payable estimate.** Use exact variant, quantity, country and legitimate discount eligibility to explain expected cost before a late surprise. Shopify already exposes buyer-aware cart estimates, and checkout remains authoritative. The useful intervention is reconciliation and a feasible alternative, not inventing a final-price guarantee. Randomize eligible arrivals and measure kept-order probability, with contribution and estimate error as guardrails. Earlier abandonment without additional purchases is not uplift. An extra quotation step may make matters worse. [Cart mechanics and limitations](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/headless/building-with-the-storefront-api/cart/manage)

7. **Explain the return commitment for this item and market.** Turn a merchant-approved policy table into a short, accurate answer, linked to the full terms. Preserve statutory rights and material exceptions. Simprosys already supports return labels, while Shopify supports policies and AI FAQs. Correct misleading policies universally before testing concise versus existing accurate presentation. The primary outcome is kept purchases per randomized visitor, with disputes and return expense monitored. Start in a bounded jurisdiction; this is not automated legal advice. [Existing attributes](https://support.simprosys.com/faq/optional-attributes-and-specifications-in-google-shopping-feed), [native FAQs](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-catalog/optimizing-products), [UK guidance](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds)

8. **Repair pack and seller identity across destinations.** Establish whether the offer is one item, a manufacturer multipack, a merchant kit or a different condition before comparing prices. Existing identifier rules and Catalog grouping cover the basics. The proposed service investigates conflicts that remain despite valid-looking fields, using supplier evidence and merchant approval. Randomize suitable product families against the existing review workflow and measure kept orders across each family. Do not invent identifiers or maker status. Wrong merges can suppress legitimate variants and harm sales. [Identity rules](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/160161?hl=en), [Simprosys guidance](https://support.simprosys.com/faq/product-identifiers-mpn-gtin-and-brand)

9. **Verify checkout capability against actual product requirements.** Inventory required inputs and app dependencies before enabling a route, test supported carts, and move permissible validation into supported server logic. Some product types and checkout experiences cannot run in Google's embedded route; an app cannot force support. Preserve a valid storefront option where permitted. Randomization may need matched stores because settings operate above visitor level. Measure kept AI-origin orders from the order ledger, plus invalid orders and losses from abandoning a useful direct route. [Channel constraints](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/agentic-storefronts/google)

10. **Allocate repair work using held-out purchases.** Use frozen retrieval replay to nominate a few fixes, then validate them with real merchant experiments. Compare an evidence-based repair queue with the existing queue across randomized merchants, holding implementation hours and paid spend equal. The causal dependency is explicit: measurement creates value only when it changes deployed work and that work produces more kept orders. Require an A/A assignment check, source-quality flags and enough stores for power. A sparse-traffic merchant may be unable to learn enough to justify the fee. [Existing order attribution](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/agentic-storefronts)

A sensible first commercial test is a small add-on for existing Simprosys users with demonstrated variant or fact-drift failures. Retain its feed transport and compare the proposed repair with native settings and vendor support. Proposed USD 29-99 monthly prices are unvalidated. For illustration, USD 49/month requires two additional kept orders at USD 25 contribution each merely to cover the fee; implementation and service costs still matter. That is a break-even threshold, not a forecast.

Use product-family allocation for shared catalogue edits and persistent visitor assignment for merchant-owned landing interventions. Predeclare propagation delays, sample size and return maturation. Track completed orders, then remove cancellations/refunds consistently. Source attribution is incomplete and must stay distinct from incrementality. Keep all-channel orders as a guardrail and avoid adding effects from overlapping interventions. A useful proxy should earn its place by predicting held-out purchase results; otherwise it is an inexpensive diagnostic, not a conversion claim.
