# Smart SEO

Snapshot: 29 September 2026. Commercial benchmark. Public documentation and merchant reviews were inspected; no merchant account was installed or changed. The companion JSON contains ten complete experiment designs, dependencies, economics and source records.

Smart SEO is a useful benchmark because an established Shopify app already sells much of the proposed implementation layer cheaply. Its current listing shows Built for Shopify, a launch date of 6 August 2015 and a 5.0 rating from 956 reviews. Those facts support tenure and marketplace reputation. They do not establish paid adoption, retention, profit or incremental purchases. Sherpas also claims more than 20,000 customers across its Shopify apps; that suite-wide claim cannot become a Smart SEO subscriber count. [App listing](https://apps.shopify.com/smart-seo), [Sherpas app suite](https://sherpas.design/pages/shopify-apps).

Prices checked on the snapshot date are USD 14.99/month for Pro, 24.99 for Business and 49.99 for Premium, with a free tier and seven-day trials. Business adds multilingual work; Premium adds broader automation and own-key AI generation. Credits and usage caps make ongoing costs visible, although a buyer must understand trial limits. These prices are a demanding comparator for a narrow new subscription. [Plans and billing](https://intercom.help/sherpas-design/en/articles/11401987-smart-seo-plans-and-billing-info).

The merchant evidence is useful but selective. The Card Collective's 17 September review describes switching after a USD 99/month proposal elsewhere, then receiving help with duplicate schema. Infano and JOERIAKNITS describe other technical fixes. The Card Collective had used the app for roughly two hours when reviewing. Brand63's May 2025 critical review alleges access and support problems. These are anecdotes, neither incident rates nor independently measured sales outcomes. [Merchant reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/smart-seo/reviews), [critical review record](https://apps.shopify.com/smart-seo/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1).

Its current audit workflow edits real product content as well as metadata, with a generation-cost estimate and a preview before publication. “Test AI Optimize” is an output preview, not a randomized purchase experiment. Product schema already covers reviews, shipping and returns. A new product should account for these capabilities before claiming safer editing or policy enrichment as an invention. [SEO Audit](https://intercom.help/sherpas-design/en/articles/11929118-seo-audit), [JSON-LD documentation](https://intercom.help/sherpas-design/en/articles/10602817-json-ld-structured-data).

The AI-branded features require a closer reading. LLM Catalog publishes a store introduction and Markdown product feed, capped at 100, 200 or 300 products by plan, with weekly or daily regeneration. Its theme-status checks establish whether a managed file exists, not whether a shopping engine used it. The MCP connection gives merchant agents read-only Google Search Console access; it is not documented as a product-search or checkout tool for consumers. [LLM Catalog](https://intercom.help/sherpas-design/en/articles/16257230-help-ai-assistants-discover-your-products-with-llm-catalog), [MCP documentation](https://intercom.help/sherpas-design/en/articles/16257268-connect-ai-agents-to-your-seo-data-with-mcp).

Native competition substantially changes the opportunity. Shopify already supplies default agent-discovery files and Catalog distribution. Its mapping tool supports preferred sources and variant grouping. Google explicitly says special AI text files do not improve its Search visibility. A supplemental catalogue can have a use, but generating one is not sufficient evidence of additional purchases. [Shopify product discovery](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/agentic-storefronts/products), [Catalog Mapping](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-catalog/mapping), [Google's current guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

Shopify also documents per-channel orders, sales, sessions and conversion, plus a raw Catalog search preview whose ranking can differ from the eventual AI channel. SimGym supplies simulated shoppers, while Rollouts supplies live treatment/control experiments for supported resources. Lily Max already markets an enrichment, testing, measurement and approval loop. A new entrant must prove a narrower advantage than “AI shoppers plus A/B testing.” [Agentic storefronts](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/agentic-storefronts/agentic-home), [SimGym](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym), [Rollouts](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/markets/rollouts/rollout-types), [Lily Max](https://www.lily.ai/how-it-works).

In the reviewed Smart SEO sources, I did not locate controlled shopper simulation, repeated external-engine prompt monitoring, randomized AI-shopping deployments or purchase attribution. This is a source-search result, not proof of absence. Its privacy policy says it does not collect customer order histories. Conventional audits, Search Console and 404 monitoring should not be relabelled a complete conversion experiment. [Privacy policy](https://sherpas.design/blogs/news/smart-seo-privacy-policy).

The shared conversation's search proxy is a sensible diagnostic method: freeze retrieved products, vary one truthful field, then inspect where the agent's reasoning changes. OpenAI function calling supports application-controlled outputs. Hosted web search also offers live-access controls, but cache-only search is not a fixed corpus or a consumer-engine replica. The illustrated 12%-67% ladder and 18.4%-31.7% improvement are invented examples. Fifty reruns of one intent measure model variability, not fifty independent customers. Moving a product up the result list tests sensitivity to an oracle change; it does not prove a deployable ranking gain. [Function calling](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/function-calling), [Web search](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-web-search).

Ten purchase mechanisms follow. Each must beat a competent existing implementation. All effects are hypotheses unless an adjacent case is explicitly identified.

1. **Recover the shopping intent behind a dead product link.** Preserve the retired item's identity and hard requirements, then offer current, compatible replacements with clear differences. Smart SEO already supports redirects; the improvement is preserving a shopper's task instead of restarting it at a generic destination. Randomize eligible AI-referred arrivals against the merchant's best existing redirect and measure seven-day paid-order rate. Stop if returns erase the gain. This is feasible now and strongest where failed-link volume is measurable. [Existing link workflow](https://intercom.help/sherpas-design/en/articles/10602788-fixing-broken-links).

2. **Keep the chosen variant and market intact from answer to cart.** Verify that an explicit offer link reaches the intended model, size, currency and live stock; show any price change before cart creation. Correct native grouping is the baseline. Randomize arrivals that actually carry variant context; never invent the hidden shopper prompt. Measure purchasers per assigned visitor and wrong-variant returns. The integration is worthwhile only if an audit finds meaningful handoff errors. [Native mapping](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-catalog/mapping), [OpenAI shopping behavior](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search).

3. **Expose only delivery promises that can still be met.** For a volunteered deadline and destination, combine stock, carrier windows and fulfillment cutoffs before recommending an offer. ShipperHQ already provides delivery calculations; integrate it and compare earlier feasibility checks against the same accurate estimate shown only at checkout. A partner shopping flow is required. Paid orders per assigned deadline-shopping session is the endpoint, with on-time fulfillment mandatory. A speculative delivery promise would make this worse than doing nothing. [Delivery-date configuration](https://docs.shipperhq.com/delivery-datecalendar-configuration).

4. **Make compatibility answers fail closed on unknown facts.** Build source-backed compatibility rules with exclusions, model versions and explicit unknown states for replacement parts or accessories. Publish useful explanations and provide a deterministic checker to willing agents. RevenueHunt already offers compatibility quizzes, so the comparator must include a competently configured quiz. Test product-family clusters for discovery effects and human sessions separately for on-site effects; measure paid orders and wrong-fit returns. The distinction is evidence quality and hard constraints, not more descriptive copy. [Existing compatibility tools](https://revenuehunt.com/solutions/size-finder/).

5. **Answer the actual return-cost question before checkout.** Explain the consequence for this item, destination and declared situation using reviewed policy rules. Separate statutory rights from additional merchant policy and show known costs. Smart SEO already emits return schema; the hypothesis concerns comprehension. In a scoped UK pilot, compare two accurate presentations while providing required information to everyone. Measure purchase rate, understanding, refunds and complaints. Market/category review is a real dependency; the product must not sell an automatic compliance guarantee. [UK returns guidance](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds).

6. **Sell the complete working setup without unnecessary extras.** Turn a declared project into the smallest sufficient set of compatible components, subtracting what the shopper says they already own. Use native bundles where appropriate, with honest total price and live stock. Test purchase incidence against existing bundles and individual products at equal economics. Higher order value alone does not satisfy the objective. Required/optional dependency knowledge and component maintenance create the work; a generic bundle builder is already free. [Shopify Bundles](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/bundles/shopify-bundles).

7. **Prove which seller and product the shopper is choosing.** Separate verified product identity, seller authorization, condition and warranty terms. A GTIN lookup establishes identifier ownership, not the authenticity of an individual unit or a reseller's authority. Test a concise evidence panel against ordinary accurate seller details, measuring paid purchases and comprehension. Upstream publication needs a separate clustered experiment. This is most plausible for legitimate high-value offers that lose sales through confusion; it confers no promised ranking privilege. [Verified by GS1](https://www.gs1.org/services/verified-by-gs1).

8. **Optimize the cold product-page arrival that AI sends.** Fix the actual first landing, including critical content and purchase controls, on representative mobile devices. Smart SEO's navigation preloading cannot warm an external visitor's first page. Test visually equivalent theme versions against paid-order rate for preclassified AI arrivals, with performance and functional guardrails. Vodafone's older paid-traffic experiment is adjacent evidence that speed can affect sales; its reported effect is not a forecast for this channel or merchant. [Smart SEO preloading](https://intercom.help/sherpas-design/en/articles/10602873-page-speed), [Vodafone case](https://web.dev/case-studies/vodafone).

9. **Let shoppers carry their own equipment context between stores and agents.** Offer an optional, editable equipment card with explicit model versions and prior compatible choices, shared through export or revocable access. Swym already supplies shared wishlists; True Fit already supplies preference/fit intelligence through MCP. The narrow hypothesis is cross-brand equipment continuity for later accessory purchases. Randomize the offer against an ordinary saved list and follow verified AI-assisted repeat purchasing for 90 days. Cross-store adoption, privacy controls and repeat-cycle length make this a frontier idea. [Swym](https://apps.shopify.com/swym-relay), [True Fit](https://www.truefit.com/).

10. **Choose the repair that earns the next purchase at a fixed work budget.** Calibrate a queue spanning fulfillment, landing and catalogue problems against real order experiments. Compare it with expert allocation and a no-change holdout across merchant clusters at equal active-arm budgets. Simulations reject weak hypotheses cheaply; they cannot replace the purchase holdout. DataFeedWatch already tests titles, while Lily Max and Shopify cover much of the general workflow. The remaining claim is better cross-stage allocation, which fails if experts already choose as well. [Existing feed tests](https://www.datafeedwatch.com/blog/ab-testing-product-titles).

The first validation sequence should examine dead links, variant arrivals and cold-page performance. These have observable faults and deployable merchant-side comparators. Deadline and compatibility work are promising where operational evidence is available; equipment portability should follow a small adoption pilot.

No supportable Smart SEO conversion percentage emerged. The JSON therefore uses transparent break-even requirements rather than lift forecasts: for example, an unvalidated USD 49 monthly fee at an assumed USD 25 contribution per extra fulfilled order requires 1.96 additional orders before integration cost. Zero or negative effects remain possible. A cheap, broad incumbent makes purchase contribution, repeatable delivery cost and renewal after initial cleanup the commercial tests that matter.
