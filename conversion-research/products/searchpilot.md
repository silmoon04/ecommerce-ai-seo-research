**SearchPilot, researched 29 September 2026. Commercial benchmark.**

SearchPilot is a direct competitor to an AI commerce experimentation product. It already tests live PDPs, PLPs, feeds and schema, measures LLM referrals, and combines SEO experiments with conversion experiments. Its team has also published a conversational shopping simulation. Our defensible proposition would need to solve a specific purchase failure or make a better rollout decision than this existing workflow. Page splitting, AI shopper agents, credible intervals and a generic test-and-deploy loop are established capabilities, not inventions we can claim. [GEO offer](https://www.searchpilot.com/ai), [Merchant Center offer](https://www.searchpilot.com/merchant), [conversational simulation](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/blog/conversations).

The buyer is an enterprise SEO/GEO team working with ecommerce, analytics and engineering. The public Core plan starts at **$37,500 annually**, but explicitly excludes transactional websites; the page does not identify the dollar currency. Commerce and Enterprise require a quote. Commerce includes advanced analytics, Merchant Center and warehouse integrations. This is evidence of an enterprise commercial offer, not of profit, market leadership or a validated price for our product. [Pricing, checked 29 September 2026](https://www.searchpilot.com/pricing).

SearchPilot's useful conversion mechanism is deployment discipline: change merchant-controlled information, expose it to real retrieval systems, measure the outcome against a comparison group, and deploy changes worth keeping. Its product already offers test-readiness analysis, credible intervals, forecast diagnostics, Googlebot recrawl coverage, publishing inspection and infrastructure monitoring. A replacement must compete with those details. The documentation does not establish that a Googlebot visit proves another engine retrieved the same version. [Product walkthrough](https://www.searchpilot.com/what-we-do).

The evidence is unusually relevant, but the endpoints matter. In its Omio case, adding brand USPs produced a vendor-reported **18% increase in LLM sessions**. A separate key-takeaways test helped LLM traffic but had an estimated **6.5% negative effect on Google organic sessions**, so it was not rolled out. These are controlled traffic estimates, not observed incremental purchases. The public page does not give order counts, return-adjusted revenue, complete allocation data or intervals sufficient for independent reproduction. Omio is also a travel business. [Omio customer story](https://www.searchpilot.com/geo-a-b-testing-seo-and-geo-are-not-the-same).

SearchPilot separately reports **$6 million revenue uplift from one test** for an unnamed sports brand. The public summary does not explain the order-level calculation or establish AI-origin revenue. It supports commercial relevance, with substantial verification limits. Its July 2026 article says revenue models may use session values, channel segmentation and finance assumptions. We must distinguish such translations from counted incremental orders. [Sports-brand case](https://www.searchpilot.com/global-sports-brand-customer-success-story), [agentic-commerce measurement discussion](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/blog/agentic-commerce-seo-what-enterprise-ecommerce-teams-should-test-now).

There are two different assignment methods. SEO testing constructs statistically similar page groups and predicts untreated performance using controls and historical relationships. It is inappropriate to describe every such test as randomized shopper assignment. For conversion testing, SearchPilot documents random user assignment. Its methodological update recommends a CRO phase followed by an SEO phase, with combined net-conversion analysis. Older explanations describe the opposite sequence, so implementation order should be checked during diligence. The general capability is clear. [Current mathematical explanation](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/blog/the-math-behind-searchpilot-how-seo-a/b-testing-actually-works), [full-funnel update](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/blog/announcement-weve-improved-how-we-run-full-funnel-tests), [older full-funnel explanation](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/blog/what-is-full-funnel-testing-how-we-combine-seo-and-cro-testing).

The important remaining statistical issue is transport: a conversion effect learned from yesterday's visitors may differ for the audience recruited by tomorrow's discovery change. Matching seasonal traffic cannot automatically remove product substitution, changing intent or unequal engine exposure. This is a research concern, not a demonstrated defect in SearchPilot's private implementation. Its September guardrail article acknowledges that LLM referrals and conversions may be too sparse to decide a test; organic sessions can remain primary while purchase metrics are directional. An inconclusive purchase guardrail is not evidence that commercially meaningful harm is impossible. [Guardrail policy](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/blog/guardrail-metrics).

The September 23 conversational experiment is particularly close to the proposed harness. SearchPilot explicitly calls it a toy model. Repeated agent conversations show recommendation instability, not independent consumer demand. Our controlled search proxy could isolate whether an attribute changes a model's decision under frozen inputs. It cannot reproduce proprietary ranking, personalization or production tool contracts. Moving a product from fourth to second tests sensitivity to rank; it does not demonstrate a deployable ranking improvement. The shared conversation's numerical uplift ladder is illustrative. [SearchPilot's experiment](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/blog/conversations).

Ten ideas follow. These are proposed extensions and comparison tests, not claims that SearchPilot cannot implement them. The companion JSON gives each intervention's data, experiment, falsifier, price test and sensitivity model.

1. **Re-test conversion when discovery changes the audience.** Add a purchase revalidation step after a discovery change recruits a different visitor mix. Compare rollout policies across matched catalog families, using real retained orders and a fixed observation window. The edge would be fewer wrong decisions caused by transporting an earlier pooled conversion result. SearchPilot already combines SEO and CRO; a generic full-funnel dashboard offers no differentiation. This needs enough AI-labelled purchases to make the extra phase worthwhile. [Existing full-funnel method](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/blog/announcement-weve-improved-how-we-run-full-funnel-tests).

2. **Count substitute families before declaring a catalog winner.** Group products shoppers actually substitute for one another, including sibling variants and relevant categories. Randomize whole groups and count merchant orders across the group, so moving a sale from one SKU to another does not masquerade as growth. SearchPilot already measures internal-link donors and recipients. The proposed extension concerns shopping substitution and retained purchases. Marketplace experiments provide adjacent evidence for interference, but their measured bias is not an expected ecommerce uplift. [Existing linking tests](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/case-studies/expanding-your-internal-linking-test-strategy), [primary interference research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12489).

3. **Separate publication time from observed engine exposure.** Track the merchant version, feed acceptance, permitted retrieval observations and eventual landing version independently. A precommitted policy can extend an unexposed trial, retire an obsolete offer, or move scarce testing capacity elsewhere. Preserve intention-to-treat analysis from assignment; conditioning the primary estimate on engines that happened to refresh would introduce selection bias. The edge must beat SearchPilot's existing crawl diagnostics on orders per testing budget. [Crawl analysis](https://www.searchpilot.com/what-we-do), [feed integration](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/guides/get-started).

4. **Recover a missed delivery promise with verified alternatives.** When an arriving shopper explicitly supplies a deadline and the chosen item cannot meet it, offer an equivalent variant, collection option or nearby stock that can. Confirm the altered choice and total before checkout. Randomize eligible shoppers against the existing availability experience and measure purchases delivered on time and retained. This is a completion intervention, not an AI visibility tactic; fulfillment accuracy is the dependency. [Existing agent fulfillment and cart primitives](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents/carts-and-checkout).

5. **Prove compatibility with the shopper's existing equipment.** For accessories, let shoppers supply an exact device or appliance model. Resolve manufacturer-supported compatibility, distinguish unknown from incompatible, and offer a verified alternative when needed. Test completed compatible purchases and compatibility returns. Google already accepts manuals and required-part relationships, while Constructor already interprets intent. The proposed edge is audited compatibility evidence attached to the chosen variant, not another attribute generator. [Conversational attributes](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/17085370), [Constructor agent](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/products-ai-shopping-agents-learn-about-ai-shopping-agent).

6. **Merge agent cart edits without losing an approved choice.** Multiple conversations or devices can submit stale cart state. Build a merchant-controlled reconciliation layer that reads the latest cart, detects conflicting revisions and requests confirmation for changed quantities or substitutions. Shopify already provides persistent carts and checkout handoff, but its documented cart update replaces the full state. Test whether reconciliation reduces dropped items and increases retained completed baskets against a correctly implemented native integration. [Shopify cart semantics](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents/carts-and-checkout).

7. **Keep seller promises attached to the selected offer.** Bind seller, condition, warranty provider and return-policy version to the exact offer through recommendation and checkout. Show a concise comparison if the seller changes; never infer authorized-dealer status. Native OpenAI checkout already resolves authoritative data from item and offer IDs. The edge is catching cross-offer promise confusion before a shopper abandons or buys under a mistaken assumption. Randomize eligible ambiguous-offer journeys and count retained orders, disputes and mismatch incidents. [Checkout contract](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/guides/product-checkout-conversion-spec).

8. **Explain the actual return cost for this item.** Convert the merchant's verified rules into a short scenario: destination, relevant condition, deadline, return postage and refund form. Keep required disclosures in both variants; test comprehension and timing, not concealment. Google already supports policy overrides and several return-cost fields. Count retained orders per assigned AI arrival, since higher initial orders followed by more returns would fail the proposition. This tests whether resolved uncertainty helps people make a suitable purchase. [Return-policy specification](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/return-policy).

9. **Buy evidence only when it can change an order decision.** Prioritize experiments using remaining selling time, stock, plausible retained-order value, uncertainty and the cost of another observation. Compare this policy with normal expert prioritization at the same budget. Do not reject potentially useful work solely because a small site's p-value will be inconclusive. SearchPilot already has readiness and commercial-threshold guidance; the proposed edge is better realized purchases from a finite seasonal budget. Incorrect priors or an expensive model can erase that advantage. [Existing decision analysis](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/blog/the-math-behind-searchpilot-how-seo-a/b-testing-actually-works).

10. **Make the simulator earn its right to choose live tests.** Freeze a library of merchant experiments, hide their live purchase results, and test whether simulated journeys select better interventions than experts or random selection. Then compare prospective testing portfolios with equal budgets. Shopify SimGym already sells shopper simulation and Shopify Rollouts supplies live experiments. The differentiation is measured transfer to purchase decisions across held-out merchants and model versions. Recommendation frequency alone cannot qualify a winner. [SimGym](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym), [Rollouts](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/markets/rollouts/rollout-types).

Every purchase estimate needs a defined denominator. Post-click interventions use shoppers assigned before treatment, including abandoners. Discovery interventions use preassigned catalog groups over fixed time, since referral sessions can themselves be changed by treatment. Reconcile cancellations and returns after the merchant's applicable window. Report identifiable AI referrals and authenticated agent orders separately from unattributed traffic; missing provenance does not justify inventing AI attribution. Native UCP already reports completed purchases and cancellations, so a generic checkout funnel is not a new product. [Google UCP analytics](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp/guides/tools/merchant-center/analytics).

None of these ten ideas has a supported AI-origin purchase-lift percentage. The JSON uses explicit sensitivity assumptions or break-even conditions, including zero and negative outcomes. The best initial tests are narrow failures with observable prevalence, such as lost cart choices or compatibility errors. A simulator becomes commercially useful only if it selects more valuable live interventions for the same budget. Otherwise SearchPilot's existing live experimentation, or native commerce experiments, remain the stronger baseline.
