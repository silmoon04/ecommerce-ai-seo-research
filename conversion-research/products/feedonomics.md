# Feedonomics: managed enterprise catalogue benchmark

Snapshot: 29 September 2026. Feedonomics is a useful commercial benchmark because it combines enterprise data operations, human implementation and access to commerce channels. The reviewed public evidence does not establish its profitability or a causal increase in purchases originating in consumer AI shopping. The practical question is whether a new product can improve decisions and transactions that remain difficult after competent feed management.

Its enterprise price is a custom quote based on SKU count, channel scope and service level; the pricing page supplies no numeric enterprise price and says it takes no revenue percentage. Dedicated managers, mapping, transformation rules, QA, regular reviews and 24/7 support make labor part of the product. A cheaper subscription is not comparable unless the replacement also handles that work. [Pricing](https://feedonomics.com/pricing/), [managed services](https://feedonomics.com/services/)

Feedonomics imports from ecommerce platforms, PIMs and internal systems, transforms records, applies destination mappings, schedules full/delta updates and uses alerts, hard stops and inventory buffers. It also handles order routing and split fulfillment. Basic enrichment, error repair, approvals, fresh inventory and order synchronization are crowded capabilities. The opportunity needs a more specific purchase failure. [Platform workflow](https://feedonomics.com/product/how-it-works/), [order orchestration](https://feedonomics.com/product/order-orchestration/)

Source data is a real dependency. Current FDX documentation distinguishes language generation from filling missing structured attributes or assigning taxonomy, which it places outside that feature today. This does not mean the broader managed service cannot join source data or populate fields. Generation guidance also says the finished configuration persists while its uploaded briefing materials do not. Source-evidence workflows must inspect the actual product contract rather than assume all facts, inputs and transformations share the same provenance guarantees. [Source-data requirements](https://docs.feedonomics.com/product/enrichment/what-makes-good-source-data), [generation inputs](https://docs.feedonomics.com/product/enrichment/inputs)

Commerce's April 2026 Agentic Catalog Exports announcement describes an enterprise distribution service for participating AI destinations, with both crawled and feed-based implementations and further self-service access planned. It names OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Perplexity and other partners. That supports participation capability, not preferential recommendations or checkout everywhere. OpenAI separately requires checkout enablement; a discovery feed flag alone does not complete onboarding. [ACE launch](https://www.commerce.com/en-GB/press/feedonomics-unlocks-agentic-discovery-with-agentic-catalog-exports/), [OpenAI product specification](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/file-upload/products)

The merchant results are useful but narrower than the promotional framing:

- Evo describes a Google Shopping title split test with 19% higher ROAS and 104% more impressions. Public assignment, sample and uncertainty details are incomplete. [Evo case](https://feedonomics.com/success-stories/outdoor-sports-brand/)
- VIETRI reports 80% higher CVR and 37% higher ROAS across two October-to-January periods. Budget rose about 40%, and campaigns changed. The headline CVR is not isolated to one randomized intervention. [VIETRI case](https://feedonomics.com/success-stories/vietri-trellis/)
- Easylife reports roughly 500% YoY shopping-campaign revenue growth with ROAS 4.5, alongside segmentation and spend scaling. Revenue growth is not an experimental effect estimate. [Easylife case](https://feedonomics.com/success-stories/easylife/)
- Euro Car Parts combines earlier Google feed improvements with later enrichment of 25,000 listings. Its compatibility example uses an existing master list. The case does not isolate AI-origin purchases. [Euro Car Parts case](https://feedonomics.com/success-stories/euro-car-parts/)

Feedonomics already has much of the proposed measurement foundation. Its A/B playbook uses stable-hash product assignment, keeps variants together and compares labelled groups in Google Shopping. The merchant owns the analysis; the suggested two-to-four-week period is not a guarantee of statistical power. [A/B testing documentation](https://docs.feedonomics.com/product/enrichment/ab-testing)

The Traffic report provides orders and revenue for BigCommerce storefronts, including an AI/LLM channel. Current paid reporting supports Google Ads. Enrichment comparisons require tags, and Traffic and Paid Media remain separate reports. Attribution windows, cross-device identity and refund treatment are not established by these pages. A new tool cannot claim that Feedonomics lacks purchase reporting. [Traffic report](https://docs.feedonomics.com/product/analytics/traffic-report), [paid reporting](https://docs.feedonomics.com/product/analytics/paid-media), [comparison setup](https://docs.feedonomics.com/product/enrichment/reports)

Its AEO report monitors AI requests, page/image fetches and crawls. Those observations do not prove that a shopper saw a recommendation; a request alone also cannot independently verify an engine's indexing decision. No controlled shopper-agent replay or prompt-level shopping-rank panel was found in the reviewed pages. That is a documentation finding, not proof the vendor lacks either capability. [AEO report](https://docs.feedonomics.com/product/analytics/aeo-report)

The shared search-proxy proposal is useful for mechanism testing. Freeze retrieved records, vary one deployable field, and test whether an agent understands a requirement or selects the correct variant. Custom function tools support this design. They do not replicate proprietary consumer retrieval, ranking or personalization; built-in web search does not establish that equivalence either. The shared 12%-67% ladder and 18.4%-31.7% example are invented figures. Repeated runs of one shopper prompt measure model variability, not independent human demand. Artificially moving a result tests position sensitivity, not an obtainable ranking improvement. [Function calling](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/function-calling), [web search](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-web-search)

Use four separate records: offline replay results, live engine observations, controlled merchant experiments and verified purchases. Pin model/tool versions, sample distinct real intents with permission, and reserve held-out intents. Promote an intervention only when a supported integration can deliver it and a live experiment can test retained purchases. Do not sell a simulated recommendation percentage as current ChatGPT conversion.

The ten ideas below are hypotheses. The companion JSON contains each intervention, overlap, data requirements, experiment, metric, cost model and falsifier.

1. **Measure catalogue experiments against retained orders and substitution.** Build an order/refund ledger joined to exported versions and destination acknowledgement. Group close substitutes so a winning SKU cannot hide losses elsewhere. Feedonomics already supports product-group randomization; the proposed extension evaluates net orders and propagation lag. Randomize category teams to this decision process or existing prioritization with equal labor. Kill the claim if better attribution does not produce more retained purchases. This is an enabling measurement product, not a direct conversion treatment. [Existing testing](https://docs.feedonomics.com/product/enrichment/ab-testing)

2. **Revalidate a short-lived offer before an AI shopper commits.** For volatile stock or promotions, check the exact variant, price and fulfillment immediately before commitment, with an optional genuine inventory hold after buyer intent. Feed sync and UCP checkout expiry already exist; the candidate advantage is coordination across a stale recommendation and the merchant's live system. Randomize eligible sessions against current checkout and measure retained orders, including failed checks. Extra latency and stranded inventory can make the result negative. [UCP checkout](https://ucp.dev/specification/shopping/checkout/)

3. **Carry the selected variant and agreed constraints into checkout.** Pass a short-lived opaque token containing validated choices and only the context the shopper agreed to share. Restore and revalidate it at arrival, then allow edits. Basic variant links and checkout handoffs are native capabilities, so compare against correctly implemented deep links. Richer context needs an approved origin integration; it cannot be recovered from an inaccessible conversation. Test retained purchases per eligible arrival and wrong-item returns. [Variant feed contract](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/file-upload/products), [handoff contract](https://ucp.dev/specification/shopping/checkout/)

4. **Resolve ambiguous fitment before recommending a part.** Ask the smallest necessary question about an engine, trim or year, then use authoritative compatibility records to return compatible, incompatible or unresolved. Feedonomics already joins fitment and expands listings. The proposed step is interactive ambiguity resolution before an external recommendation, tested against the merchant's actual fitment selector. The purchase endpoint is retained compatible orders per assigned session. A fluent explanation cannot override missing qualifiers. [Existing automotive offer](https://feedonomics.com/who-we-serve/auto-parts-feed-management/)

5. **Compile a complete, compatible basket for a stated task.** For DIY or equipment shopping, build a merchant-reviewed bill of materials with quantities, dependencies, included parts and shopper-owned components. Produce the smallest adequate basket, with optional extras unselected. Existing cart and fulfillment systems already move multiple items; they do not establish task completeness. Randomize against current bundles/configurators and measure retained task-complete purchases, not attachment rate or AOV alone. [Existing fulfillment model](https://ucp.dev/specification/shopping/extensions/fulfillment/)

6. **Let shoppers relax one negotiable constraint transparently.** When no product meets the request, identify the conflicting preferences and show a few explicit single-tradeoff choices. Preserve hard requirements and spending caps. Generic clarification already exists; the proposed difference is a deterministic explanation grounded in current merchant facts. Test against the existing zero-result flow. More clicks without retained orders meeting every hard requirement would falsify the purchase claim. This can start in a merchant-controlled assistant. [Source grounding](https://docs.feedonomics.com/product/enrichment/what-makes-good-source-data)

7. **Expose the payable and returnable offer before cross-border selection.** Calculate destination-specific charges, delivery feasibility and merchant return terms before the shopper commits to a shortlist. Shipping fields and fulfillment protocols already overlap. The candidate improvement is agreement between the early offer and checkout. Both trial arms retain required disclosures; measure retained deliverable orders and quote errors. UK guidance supports clear total-price and delivery information, but implementation needs the rules applicable to each market. [UK guidance](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses/online-selling), [fulfillment specification](https://ucp.dev/specification/shopping/extensions/fulfillment/)

8. **Reconcile buyer entitlements across a distributor network.** With explicit account linking, compare genuinely available dealer terms and prepare a permitted requisition across heterogeneous systems. UCP identity linking and BigCommerce's B2B MCP already cover consent, permissions, lists, quotes and checkout. The proposed extension is cross-dealer reconciliation, a frontier integration. Randomize company accounts and count fulfilled orders across the whole network. A share shift between dealers is not a new purchase, and suppliers may resist the product. [Identity linking](https://ucp.dev/specification/common/identity-linking/), [B2B MCP](https://docs.bigcommerce.com/developer/api-reference/mcp/storefront/b2b)

9. **Turn a consented owned-product record into a timely reorder.** Retain the exact model and buyer-chosen review cadence for non-medical consumables, then recheck need, compatibility and current offers during a user-authorized AI journey. Existing subscriptions and repeat-list tools are the comparator. Do not automatically buy or infer private usage. Randomize accounts and measure retained repeat purchases across two consumption cycles; an order merely brought forward does not count as incremental. [Native repeat-order support](https://docs.bigcommerce.com/developer/api-reference/mcp/storefront/b2b)

10. **Buy the missing product facts that block real shopper decisions.** Instead of rewriting thin data, fund the smallest supplier confirmation or physical measurement needed to answer a repeated buying requirement. Feedonomics already integrates supplemental facts and supplies expert labor. The proposed distinction is purchase-based allocation of a fixed evidence budget. Randomize product families against the normal backlog with equal expert hours. Better completeness without more retained purchases kills the claim; a family measurement must not be generalized to incompatible variants. [Source limits](https://docs.feedonomics.com/product/enrichment/what-makes-good-source-data), [managed work](https://feedonomics.com/services/)

Start with a merchant that can demonstrate one repeated failure and enough AI-origin traffic to test it. Favor variant handoff or fitment when the fault is observable; use fact acquisition when the required evidence genuinely does not exist. Dealer reconciliation needs a cooperating network before significant build work. All proposed prices in the JSON are unvalidated USD pilot tests. Its break-even calculations include chosen contribution assumptions and, where relevant, labor or data costs. No cited case justifies an expected uplift percentage for these ideas.
