# Constructor: a conversion benchmark with an important onsite/offsite split

Research snapshot: 29 September 2026. The companion [constructor.json](constructor.json) contains ten complete experiment specifications, source IDs, dependencies, falsifiers and unvalidated price tests.

Constructor is a useful commercial benchmark because it combines shopping assistance with established discovery infrastructure and order instrumentation. It is not evidence that offsite AI optimization reliably produces a particular purchase uplift. Its onsite AI Shopping Agent (ASA), Product Insights Agent (PIA), conventional search improvements and newer Answer Engine Assistants beta need separate evidence.

ASA handles natural-language goals, including multi-item shopping, using merchant catalog/content data and behavioral feedback. The implementation requires catalog preparation, API integration and a merchant-designed experience; conversational continuity uses `thread_id`. These are existing capabilities, so a generic shopping chatbot, goal planner or persistent conversation would offer little differentiation. No public numerical subscription price was verified; onboarding remains sales/CSM-led. [ASA documentation](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/products-ai-shopping-agents-learn-about-ai-shopping-agent), [implementation guide](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/products-ai-shopping-agents-learn-about-ai-shopping-agent-implement-ai-shopping-agent).

The homepage separately lists Answer Engine Assistants as a beta that supplies real-time information for discovery and purchase through engines such as ChatGPT. I did not locate its public engine contracts, freshness guarantees, complete availability criteria or controlled purchase results. The public MCP page concerns Constructor's **documentation**, so it cannot establish that consumer ChatGPT accesses a merchant's live shopping tools. [Constructor homepage](https://constructor.com/), [MCP documentation](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/ai-tooling-mcp-server).

The purchase mechanism is plausible: better intent capture narrows the catalog, evidence resolves uncertainty, and the shopper proceeds to merchant checkout. PIA already answers compatibility, delivery and return questions using product and supporting content. A new product must improve a specific failure within that process, then prove additional purchases. Providing more answers, generating larger baskets or earning more recommendation citations is insufficient. [PIA](https://constructor.com/solutions/product-insights-agent).

The published results require restraint:

- Belk's ASA case says mobile-app shoppers using the agent convert at double or better the rate of standard-search users. That conditions on usage; it does not disclose ASA-specific random assignment, absolute rates, sample size, confidence intervals or a precise conversion definition. The same case's USD 21 million initial uplift and later USD 35 million/7.4% figures concern core search and search optimization, not ASA. [Belk case study](https://constructor.com/customers/belk-constructor).
- A demo page reports ASA increases of 12% conversion, 16% revenue per visitor and 5% average order value without identifying the underlying test. Its separate PIA purchase-rate claim, 197%, compares users with non-users. These are adjacent vendor claims, not expected effects for our interventions. [Agentic-commerce demo](https://info.constructor.com/agentic-commerce-demo).

Constructor does document a randomized methodology: its AABB article assigns users to duplicate control and treatment groups to detect assignment problems. Dashboard tests cover global search rules, global browse rules and recommendation strategies, with traffic allocation and sample-size planning. This supports a general experimentation benchmark; it does not establish how the advertised ASA results were produced or that an offsite answer-engine experiment is available. [AABB method](https://constructor.com/split-test-digest/why-running-classic-ab-tests-could-be-a-big-mistake), [dashboard tests](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/analytics-a-b-testing).

Actual purchase measurement is also documented. The purchase event means checkout completion and order placement; a generic success event can instead mean add-to-cart, wishlist or another action. Offline behavioral ingestion already accepts returns. A proposal to add order tracking or return ingestion alone would overlap. [Purchase tracking](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/integrating-with-constructor-behavioral-tracking-success-event-and-purchase-tracking), [offline actions API](https://docs.constructor.com/reference/v1-offline-behavioral-actions-create-actions).

Two reporting details matter. First, Constructor changed attribution on 1 September 2026 from a session window to 30 days across sessions. The last discovery interaction before add-to-cart receives per-item credit, which the eventual purchase inherits. That can increase attributed revenue without increasing orders. Second, the External Traffic Report already reports AI-referral orders and revenue; its Sources table defines “Purchase Rate” as a platform's share of AI-origin purchases, not purchases divided by that platform's sessions. Missing referrers may fall into Direct or Other. [Attribution](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/analytics-metrics-glossary-attribution), [External Traffic Report](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/analytics-external-traffic-report).

The harness assessment is therefore mixed. Offline shopper replay was not found in the reviewed sources and targeted searches. Live referral monitoring, general randomized testing and completed-order measurement are documented. Repeated prompt monitoring inside external engines and a documented randomized ASA/offsite purchase study were not found. Search omissions establish uncertainty, not absence.

The shared search-proxy proposal can help diagnose sensitivity to merchant-controlled facts in a frozen tool response. It cannot replicate proprietary consumer-engine retrieval, personalization or ranking. Repeated model runs quantify model variability, not independent human shopping intents. Moving a result upward is a sensitivity test unless a real deployable intervention can earn that position. The shared 12%-67% ladder and 18.4%-31.7% example are invented, not observations.

There is also native competition: Shopify SimGym already simulates shopping and add-to-cart, while Rollouts supports live treatment/control changes. Use simulation to reject obvious failures and choose candidates for live testing. Sell verified purchase improvement only after real outcomes support it. [SimGym](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym), [Rollouts](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/markets/rollouts/rollout-types).

The ten opportunities below are hypotheses. Each experiment should assign shoppers before interaction, preserve assignment, count non-users and abandoners, and distinguish paid purchases from later retained purchases. The JSON gives duration, power caveats and guardrails. Onsite AI-shopping cohorts and identifiable offsite AI referrals must be reported separately.

1. **Prove that the whole project basket works.** For DIY or electronics, solve required parts, quantities, compatibility and total budget together. A relevant list can still leave a project unusable. Compare a constraint-checked basket against existing agent recommendations and measure additional retained mission purchasers. ASA already covers multi-item goals; Google supports required-part relationships and Channable advertises forthcoming bundles. The prospective advantage is verified whole-basket feasibility, not another bundle generator. Incomplete compatibility evidence must produce an unresolved requirement rather than reassurance. [Google attributes](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/17085370), [Channable updates](https://www.channable.com/product-releases-2026).

2. **Ask only the question that changes the purchase decision.** A further question helps when its answer changes suitable products; otherwise it may create abandonment. Build a policy that estimates that distinction and exposes a usable shortlist when enough is known. Existing agents already ask follow-ups, so compare against the current follow-up policy using paid purchasers per eligible ambiguous-query shopper. Kill the claim if quicker purchases lead to more unsuitable returns. This is feasible now where the merchant controls the conversation policy. [Current agent API](https://docs.constructor.com/reference/v1-asa-retrieve-agent-insights-chat).

3. **Carry accepted constraints through the AI referral.** A cooperating agent could pass an expiring, signed, shopper-approved record of budget, requirements and selected variants to the merchant. Resume the accepted task and visibly confirm current facts. Compare with a prefilled cart, since Shopify already offers cart context and handoff URLs. The purchase path is less repeated explanation and fewer lost requirements. Universal consumer-engine support is not assumed; insufficient supported traffic would kill the economics. This is a frontier integration, not a basic shared cart. [Shopify carts](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents/carts-and-checkout).

4. **Resume a saved project with only the decisions that changed.** Furniture and renovation decisions often span visits. Store an optional project record, then recheck accepted choices against current facts when the shopper returns. Explain material changes instead of replaying the entire conversation. Thread memory and logged-in identity are already available; the hypothesis is that a concise decision review creates more purchases than a saved chat/cart. Randomize when shoppers save the project and include non-returners in the denominator. Avoid invented urgency. [Identification parameters](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/integrating-with-constructor-behavioral-tracking-identification-parameters).

5. **Keep a stable comparison board while the agent explains trade-offs.** Let shoppers lock a small candidate set, compare it against their stated requirements and replace options deliberately. This reduces the need to remember changing chat recommendations. Hold the underlying recommendation generator constant in the test, so any purchase gain belongs to the interface. Constructor already allows custom agent layouts; stability and lower decision effort are the proposed advantages. Mobile overload, anchoring on poor initial choices and unsupported facts could all reverse the effect. [Agent UI options](https://constructor.com/blog/enhancing-product-discovery-through-ai-agents).

6. **Recommend baskets that can arrive together before the real deadline.** Connect agent selection to warehouses, carrier cutoffs and installation capacity, not just item stock. A purchase becomes useful only if the whole mission arrives in time. Existing checkout integration already resolves authoritative inventory and prices; the new hypothesis concerns joint delivery feasibility. Compare paid, on-time mission purchases, using service-area/time clusters if scarce slots create interference. Reject a gain that merely reallocates a slot from another shopper. [OpenAI checkout contract](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/guides/product-checkout-conversion-spec).

7. **Promote agent policies only on additional retained purchasers.** Build a selection loop with frozen event definitions, persistent eligibility assignment and deduplicated orders/returns. Use causal retained-purchaser estimates to choose policies, rather than chat-user conversion or shifting attribution totals. Existing testing and measurement infrastructure is the baseline. This tool creates value only if it selects better interventions or avoids harmful ones. Test the full sequence of policy decisions against the current selection process with an independent control. It must beat an already competent experimentation team.

8. **Isolate gift intent from the account owner's history.** An explicitly declared gift mission should use the recipient requirements the shopper supplies, without silently inheriting the buyer's tastes or training those choices back into self-shopping preferences. Use temporary task scope and feature provenance, not an identified recipient profile. Compare retained gift purchases and subsequent account purchase outcomes. Constructor already combines context and history; equivalent gift handling may exist. The idea fails if suppressing history removes more useful information than it corrects.

9. **Apply return conditions to the exact variant before recommending it.** For a shopper who needs a change-of-mind option, select offers against merchant-reviewed variant and destination rules. Separate goodwill policies, cancellation exceptions and faulty-goods rights. PIA already answers returns questions; the hypothesis is better candidate selection. Both test arms must retain required disclosures. Incorrect rights statements are a failure even if short-term purchases rise. Start with one jurisdiction and explicit merchant policy ownership, rather than offering automated legal assurances. [UK returns guidance](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds).

10. **Spend reasoning time where it changes the chance of buying.** Route exact-product requests to fast verified retrieval, reserving deeper reasoning for unresolved consequential constraints. Compare against existing routing at the same total serving budget. Additional retained purchasers remain primary; cheaper attributed orders do not establish improvement. Constructor already has streaming and multiple discovery modes, so the advantage must come from better allocation. Keep correctness checks on every route and audit rare intents. Savings matter when they fund additional effective assistance without reducing purchases.

There is no supportable common uplift percentage for these ideas. The JSON instead provides transparent fee-only break-even examples. For instance, 10,000 eligible monthly shoppers, GBP 80 contribution per additional retained order and a proposed GBP 2,000 monthly fee require 25 extra orders, or 0.25 percentage points, before integration and serving costs. Those are chosen assumptions. Baseline conversion and defect prevalence must be measured. Zero or negative effects remain possible for every idea.

The strongest near-term tests are the clarification stopping policy and stable comparison board because the merchant can control them while holding recommendations or data constant. Whole-project feasibility and fulfillment coordination offer a more substantial technical advantage where authoritative data exists. The cross-agent handoff is promising only after a supported partner and enough reachable shopping traffic are secured.
