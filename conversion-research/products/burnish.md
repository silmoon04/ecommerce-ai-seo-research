# Burnish: a workflow benchmark

Snapshot: 29 September 2026. Burnish is worth studying for the breadth of its operational loop. Public evidence does not establish it as a financial winner. Its case-study page explicitly has no published customer results; Threshold Footwear and the associated revenue screens are labelled representative demonstrations. The Shopify listing shows two five-star reviews, written after seven and eight days of use. They support early usefulness, not durable demand, renewal or incremental orders. [Case-study status](https://useburnish.com/case-studies/) · [Shopify listing](https://apps.shopify.com/burnish)

The published monthly prices are USD149 for Presence, USD299 for Authority and USD649 for Omnipresence. Allowances increase from 1,500 to 7,500 analyzed answers and from 300 to 2,000 product optimizations per month. Authority adds per-action lift and cohort ROI; the highest plan adds agency features. These are verified offers, not verified merchant willingness to pay. [Pricing](https://useburnish.com/pricing/)

Burnish already addresses much of the obvious product proposal. Its source-attached fact spine feeds an approved brand narrative; separate judges assess whether an answer concerns the correct store and whether claims match live policy pages. The manifesto includes legal entity, primary domain, namesake distinctions, returns, shipping and warranty. Therefore, fact provenance, brand identity checks and policy grounding cannot be described as newly discovered opportunities. [Workflow](https://useburnish.com/what-it-does/) · [Manifesto contents](https://useburnish.com/help/manifesto/whats-in-your-manifesto/)

Its catalog documentation lists 41 fix types, 29 approval-gated by default and 37 with registered rollback. Six checks cover dependencies such as feeds, collections and existing structured data. Variant consistency and product-type normalization have confidence floors. The page also states rollback limitations, including unsupported strategies and oversized recorded state. This is a substantial execution baseline. The more interesting question is whether its choice of work creates purchases. [Catalog optimizer](https://useburnish.com/features/catalog/)

The measurement deserves close reading. The AI Commerce Index combines 40% readiness with 60% visibility. Readiness lift is a projection within Burnish's rubric, not a traffic or revenue forecast; the representation/presence split is acknowledged as provisional. Sampling repeats over time, but the published methodology explicitly excludes randomized controls. A rising score can establish that the instrument changed its reading without establishing that an extra buyer purchased. [Methodology](https://useburnish.com/methodology/)

Purchase measurement does exist. Burnish documents a first-party pixel, Shopify order corroboration and optional GA4 checks, with a 30-day before/after comparison. The revenue page labels the evidence correlational and the displayed merchant figures illustrative. The help pages describe a 95% interval for per-action lift and a cohort table combining eligible actions by type and month. Neither an interval nor agreement between data sources removes confounding. With multiple changes touching the same products, the public material does not make overlapping effects auditable. That is an evidence limitation, not proof of an implementation error. [Revenue method](https://useburnish.com/features/revenue/) · [Per-action lift](https://useburnish.com/help/revenue-attribution/per-action-lift/) · [Cohort ROI](https://useburnish.com/help/revenue-attribution/cohort-roi/)

| Measurement layer | Public evidence | What it can support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frozen tool-response simulation | Not found in the pages reviewed | No claim about Burnish's private capabilities |
| Live engine monitoring | Documented scheduled answers and named samplers | Observed answer behavior under its sampling conditions |
| Randomized merchant experiment | Methodology explicitly says no control group | Before/after association, without a causal treatment estimate |
| Purchase measurement | Documented pixel and order association | Observed AI-associated orders, subject to coverage and attribution limits |

The [engine page](https://useburnish.com/engines/) and [ChatGPT page](https://useburnish.com/engines/chatgpt/) name the sampling models and schedules. They do not demonstrate reproduction of consumer search routing, shopping personalization or checkout behavior. There are also unresolved documentation mismatches: pricing lists 50/100/500 tracked prompts, while engine documentation lists 50/50/100 discovery questions; the catalog page retains a 7,000-product ceiling that the [July 28 changelog](https://useburnish.com/changelog/) says was removed. Ask for current contractual limits before purchasing or designing a head-to-head comparison.

The proposed controlled search proxy is useful for testing how a model reacts to deployable changes. Current [OpenAI function-calling documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/function-calling) confirms that an application can execute a function and return its output. That does not recreate the separate [hosted web-search tool](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-web-search), much less every consumer shopping system. Freeze retrieval, vary one truthful field, and distinguish discovery, comprehension, shortlist and recommendation. Repeated runs of one intent estimate output variability, not independent human demand. Moving a result upward tests rank sensitivity; it does not show that a merchant can obtain that position. The shared conversation's numerical ladders remain invented illustrations.

The practical extension is to connect this diagnostic instrument to purchase experiments. Use independent real intent samples, separate held-out stores and dates, and calibrate automated judgments against people, consistent with [OpenAI's evaluation guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices). For upstream changes, randomize disjoint product families and measure orders per assigned family-period; conditioning only on arriving visitors can hide a discovery effect. For changes after arrival, randomize eligible visitors before exposure. Keep source classification fixed, leave unattributed traffic unknown, and check all-channel displacement. The following ten ideas have different interventions and failure conditions; complete schemas and trial plans are in [burnish.json](burnish.json).

1. **Prioritize fixes by recoverable paid orders.** Replace readiness-gap ordering with observed demand, specific purchase blockers, inventory and conservative treatment evidence. This is an allocation tool: it creates value only through better deployed repairs. Randomize product-family clusters between queues at equal implementation capacity; compare paid AI-origin orders per family-week. Burnish already has prioritization and cohort ROI, so the test must show better work selection. Stop if readiness ordering performs as well. [Existing prioritization](https://useburnish.com/features/catalog/)

2. **Calibrate simulated winners against purchase holdouts.** Link frozen replay effects to prospectively randomized merchant results, then learn which intervention classes transfer on held-out stores. Test the resulting selection policy against raw simulation ranking under equal deployment budgets. The endpoint is actual paid orders across assigned clusters, not agreement between models. This could become valuable research infrastructure, but poor transfer would kill the proposition. Purchase-based experimentation already exists in adjacent onsite search, as [Constructor's Saatchi Art case](https://constructor.com/customers/saatchi-art-constructor) illustrates; its claimed gains are not forecasts for this tool.

3. **Carry the selected variant and constraints into checkout.** A signed handoff record preserves exact variant, market and buyer-shared constraints, rechecks current terms and explains discrepancies. Randomize qualifying handoffs against the existing native checkout link and count seven-day paid purchases over every assigned handoff, including failures. [Shopify already offers variant checkout links and Cart MCP](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents/carts-and-checkout); the advantage must be verified continuity. The external assistant must cooperate or the buyer must confirm requirements. Hidden conversation access cannot be assumed.

4. **Qualify offers against the buyer's delivery deadline.** Combine a volunteered destination and deadline with stock, warehouse cutoff and carrier estimates before a controlled shopping agent recommends an offer. Recheck at checkout. Randomize deadline-bearing sessions against existing selection and measure paid orders with on-time delivery as a guardrail. [Narvar already sells delivery promises](https://corp.narvar.com/promise). The extension is earlier qualification across the recommendation and checkout journey. It fails if exclusions suppress acceptable choices or estimates become promises the merchant cannot keep.

5. **Recover stale AI links without losing requirements.** When a linked variant is unavailable, ask the buyer to confirm any unknown hard requirements and present only suitable, available substitutes, with differences disclosed. Randomize these arrivals against [native related products](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/storefront-search/search-and-discovery-recommendations). Count any paid order within seven days per assigned visitor; inspect substitution returns. Stock filtering and related recommendations already exist. The hypothesis concerns preserving the reason the buyer selected the original product, and may fail when clarification adds excessive friction.

6. **Attach an executable compatibility witness.** For a narrow parts or equipment category, compile licensed manufacturer facts into versioned compatibility rules. A buyer-supplied model receives supported, unsupported or unknown, plus its evidence, and the result follows variant selection into the cart. Compare paid and retained compatible purchases with current specifications or fitment tools. [Partly already documents OEM-based parts identification](https://www.partly.com/integrations/workshop), so category selection matters. Its direct page did not load during this review; the official indexed description supports only that limited overlap. False-compatible verdicts are a decisive failure.

7. **Quote return eligibility for this basket before purchase.** Evaluate approved product, market, promotion and date rules, show relevant fees and exceptions, then retain the quoted policy version with the order. [ReturnGO already implements eligibility rules](https://support.returngo.ai/eligibility-rules); [Google supports country policies and product overrides](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/return-policy). The improvement is consistent prospective eligibility across the recommendation, purchase and return. Test completed purchases against an accurate existing policy presentation, then mature returns. [EU guidance distinguishes rights and exceptions](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/consumers/shopping/returns/indexamp_en.htm); legal determinations require qualified merchant review, not generated assurances.

8. **Ask one decisive question before showing more products.** Choose at most one clarification when its likely decision value exceeds its friction, allow skipping, and explain the resulting tradeoff. A three-arm visitor experiment compares the existing list, existing quiz and selective clarification on paid orders. [Octane already has branching, exclusions and variant mapping](https://www.octaneai.com/products/quiz-logic). Novelty therefore rests on knowing when to ask nothing or one question after an AI referral. A higher answer rate without more suitable purchases does not validate it.

9. **Let buyers carry an editable shortlist between sessions.** Give the buyer a revocable record of shortlist IDs, explicit non-sensitive preferences and unresolved questions. Refresh prices and stock on return. Compare availability of this option against standard saved carts, using 30-day purchases across everyone assigned before opting in. [Native carts](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents/carts-and-checkout) already preserve commerce state; the new element is the buyer's portable decision context across authorized touchpoints. Low adoption, privacy failures or no gain over saved carts would defeat the idea.

10. **Offer a real short inventory hold during handoff.** At an explicit buyer request, reserve an item using a supported path, show the true expiry and release unused holds. [Shopify already exposes a reservation input for draft orders](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/input-objects/draftorderinput); checkout compatibility still needs validation. Randomize inventory pools or time blocks with washout because visitors share supply. Require additional AI-origin orders without displacement of other purchases. If the stock would sell out anyway, improved channel conversion is redistribution, and this idea fails the incremental-purchase test.

Start with the verified handoff, unavailable-item recovery or one narrow compatibility category where actual failures can be observed. Their primary tests reach purchases without waiting for an external engine to rerank the merchant. Maintain the replay system as a diagnostic aid and build calibration gradually.

No empirical uplift range is defensible here. Every financial scenario in the JSON is an explicitly unvalidated break-even calculation. For example, a USD300 monthly fee at USD40 contribution per additional retained order requires eight such orders before extra operating costs. It says nothing about whether eight are available. Measure eligible traffic, failure prevalence and treatment effects first; treat cost reductions as secondary to creating additional purchases.
