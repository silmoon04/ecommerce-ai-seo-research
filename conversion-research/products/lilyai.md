Lily AI / Lily Max: vertical conversion benchmark. Research snapshot: 29 September 2026.

Lily is a serious benchmark for this project because its current offer already joins product enrichment to controlled optimization. A proposal to find catalog gaps, generate attributes, test variants and deploy winners substantially overlaps Lily Max. The opportunity is to solve a particular lost-purchase problem better, then prove the difference against an already enriched catalog. Lily's public evidence supports investigating that opportunity; it does not establish our expected uplift, its private profitability, or a causal increase in purchases from ChatGPT. [Current platform](https://www.lily.ai/), [operating workflow](https://www.lily.ai/how-it-works).

The buyer is a retailer, brand, performance marketer or feed team, particularly in fashion, footwear, beauty and home. Lily distributes improved records into the existing commerce stack. Its pricing page requires a custom quote based on scope, catalog and markets, with channel/spend considerations also described. No public currency-denominated rate was verified. The homepage advertises a 30-day trial for 500 products; commercial eligibility remains unverified. These are useful buying-process benchmarks, not a price we can copy. [Pricing](https://www.lily.ai/pricing).

The mechanisms need separate treatment:

| Mechanism | What the sources establish | What remains unproven |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consumer-language ontology | A March 2024 account describes more than 20,000 curated attributes, synonyms and trends, with retail specialists involved. | Current audited accuracy, coverage on a new merchant, and the marginal value over modern search models. [Training data](https://www.lily.ai/blog/retail-ai-experts-training-data). |
| Behavior | Lily describes recommendations based on the attributes of products viewed during a session; its 2025 release adds ongoing search-language analysis. | That behavioral personalization adds purchases beyond a strong contextual baseline. [Session intent](https://www.lily.ai/blog/boost-your-beauty-product-attribution-part-one), [winter release](https://www.lily.ai/blog/see-whats-new-lily-ais-winter-2025-release). |
| Demand | A case describes visual-similarity forecasting and a shorter forecasting cycle. | The USD 7-48 million revenue figure is projected; the page does not establish realized incremental purchases. [Forecasting case](https://www.lily.ai/case-studies/demand-forecasting-accuracy-at-scale). |
| Styling | Fine-grained attributes can improve similar-product recommendations inside an existing engine. | An additional order is different from more items in an order. [Certona case](https://www.lily.ai/case-studies/multi-brand-retailer-rpv-recommendations). |
| Attributes | The current offer supplies search, facets, PDPs, recommendations and merchandising inputs. | A style label does not prove dimensions, fabric composition, performance or individual fit. [Onsite offer](https://www.lily.ai/onsite). |

This distinction changes the product design. Consumer-language synonyms help an item become a candidate. Behavior helps choose among candidates. Styling can explain combinations. Demand planning changes whether stock exists when needed. Verified physical facts determine whether a literal requirement is satisfied. These operations can reinforce one another, but their gains cannot simply be added.

The stronger case studies still require qualification. thredUP's dated story describes an early split test with 4-15% sell-through lift, followed by a 15% headline, 2%+ conversion lift for repeat purchasers and 2.8%+ on iOS. It omits sample size, duration, uncertainty and assignment detail. Those cohort percentages are not a sitewide purchase forecast. [thredUP case](https://www.lily.ai/case-studies/thredup-sell-through-15-percent).

The Certona case is particularly instructive: revenue per visit rose 0.65% and average order size 0.6%, while visits, orders and conversion remained constant. Its USD 20-30 million demand figure extrapolates that basket effect. Calling this more purchases would misstate the result. [Recommendations case](https://www.lily.ai/case-studies/multi-brand-retailer-rpv-recommendations).

A newer apparel case reports a 26-day matched Google Shopping test, 7.3% higher revenue per product per day and a distinct 4.7% difference-in-differences estimate. A home-furnishings case reports 5.7% higher revenue per product across 28 days, despite 1.3% fewer clicks. These concern paid-shopping performance. The public pages omit the counts, intervals and pretrend checks needed to audit the causal estimates. [Apparel test](https://www.lily.ai/case-studies/performance-apparel-google-shopping-revenue-lift), [home test](https://www.lily.ai/case-studies/home-furnishings-google-shopping-revenue-lift).

A.L.C.'s 129% net-sales headline combines historical progress with discussion of recent testing; the page lacks enough design detail to identify a causal effect of that size. Likewise, the beauty case describes paid-channel tests separately from stronger AI recommendation visibility. The latter provides no purchase denominator or controlled AI-origin conversion estimate. [A.L.C. case](https://www.lily.ai/case-studies/alc-full-catalog-discovery-on-google), [beauty case](https://www.lily.ai/case-studies/prestige-beauty-cross-channel-ai-visibility).

Lily already documents matched tests, holdouts, fixed measurement windows and DiD. Its guide acknowledges that bundled attribute changes cannot isolate individual mechanisms. Matching and equal spend alone do not establish random assignment or the parallel-trends assumption needed for DiD. The JSON therefore records controlled testing as documented but randomized assignment as unclear, rather than suggesting Lily has no experiment capability. [Testing methodology](https://www.lily.ai/blog/does-feed-optimization-increase-sales).

Live AI recommendation analysis and revenue tracking are advertised; the public prompt coverage, cadence and attribution completeness are unclear. No frozen-tool replay simulator was found in the reviewed material. That is a search result, not proof that one is absent. Lily also disclaims guaranteed placement. [AI discovery offer](https://www.lily.ai/ai-discovery).

Platform details must come from platform documentation. Google's six optional conversational fields are now public, and its measurement specification includes native product length, width and height. These contradict parts of Lily's August attribute guide. Google's Q&A rules also exclude keyword lists and time-related price/date information. A successful language transformation must preserve the underlying fact; photographs can suggest style but cannot establish physical measurements. [Google conversational fields](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/17085370?hl=en-GB), [measurement fields](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/11018531?hl=en), [Q&A requirements](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/17085211?hl=en), [Lily attribute guide](https://www.lily.ai/blog/product-attributes-101).

The following ten proposals have distinct interventions. Their full experiments, dependencies, data requirements, failure modes and economics are in the companion JSON.

1. **Preserve explicit exclusions.** Compile requests such as straight-leg but not tapered into positive, negative and unknown requirements before ranking. Test against the same enriched catalog with ordinary semantic retrieval. The purchase hypothesis is fewer disqualifying recommendations, not simply more results. A persistent shopper split can test retained purchases; excessive zero-results would challenge the approach. Lily already supplies the underlying search inputs. [Existing search capability](https://www.lily.ai/onsite).

2. **Answer fit questions with verified garment evidence.** Store size-specific measurements, units, methods and tolerances; compare with optional shopper-provided reference-garment dimensions. Integrate existing fit tools where useful. True Fit already uses purchasing and returns data, so generic fit recommendations offer little differentiation. The narrower claim is resolving physical constraints with evidence and abstaining when evidence is missing. Measure retained buyers and size bracketing. [True Fit data requirements](https://help.truefit.com/app-data-requirements), [fit consistency report](https://help.truefit.com/fit-consistency-report).

3. **Recover unavailable AI landings.** When the requested variant is sold out, propose alternatives that preserve the shopper's must-haves and are currently purchasable. Revalidate stock and the precise variant at cart creation. Compare with the existing stockout page; count total merchant purchasers, including shoppers who ignore the replacement. The test fails if suggested-item sales only divert other purchases. Available-size filtering already exists in adjacent products. [True Fit](https://www.truefit.com/).

4. **Solve whole-outfit feasibility.** Combine shopper-declared occasion, deadline, budget and optional owned pieces with stock and delivery information. Test whether a feasible set converts someone who otherwise cannot assemble an outfit. Stylitics already has shoppable looks and inventory-aware outfitting; the proposed difference is joint purchase feasibility. Retained purchase incidence and on-time arrival matter more than outfit clicks or AOV. [Stylitics](https://stylitics.com/solutions/outfitting-for-athletic-brands/).

5. **Ask one useful question.** For genuinely ambiguous requests, select an optional question only when its answer changes the feasible shortlist. Show provisional results immediately. Compare with immediate retrieval and a fixed question using all assigned shoppers as the denominator. A higher answer rate is irrelevant if questions add friction without purchases. This extends existing contextual-intent capabilities rather than inventing them. [Lily session intent](https://www.lily.ai/blog/boost-your-beauty-product-attribution-part-one).

6. **Carry approved requirements across the handoff.** An expiring, opt-in token can preserve explicit constraints and product references from a cooperating assistant to a merchant. Show an editable summary; avoid unrelated identity joins and sensitive inference. Compare all invited users with a standard deep link, not only users accepting transfer. Partner access is a real integration dependency. An equally accurate variant link is a useful stronger comparator. [Current AI-commerce scope](https://www.lily.ai/ai-discovery).

7. **Move stock toward unmet intent.** Use qualified failed searches, stock by location and transfer lead times to recommend small inventory transfers before demand expires. Randomize whole donor/recipient pools and count their combined purchases. More sales at the receiving store alone can conceal cannibalization. This requires operational approval and cost accounting; Lily's forecasting case supplies adjacent context, not a forecast for this intervention. [Demand planning](https://www.lily.ai/case-studies/demand-forecasting-accuracy-at-scale).

8. **Acquire the missing fact that blocks a purchase.** Allocate a fixed supplier-verification budget to unanswered questions with observed qualified demand. A measured leg opening may be more valuable than hundreds of decorative tags. Compare with completeness-led verification, using equal budgets and purchase outcomes across independent merchant/category clusters. Fact acquisition is valuable only when deploying the answer changes buying outcomes. Never manufacture an answer to make a record look complete. [Measurement specification](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/11018531?hl=en).

9. **Explain the deciding tradeoff.** For a shopper-selected comparison, show which verified difference matters to the stated requirements, including unknowns. Compare with a complete conventional comparison table containing identical products, prices and facts. This tests decision support rather than extra copy. Stop if explanation errors or returns offset increased checkout completion. Google's factual Q&A rules provide useful publishing boundaries. [Q&A specification](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/17085211?hl=en).

10. **Validate whether simulation selects better live experiments.** Pair frozen-tool diagnostics with independently controlled purchase outcomes, then prospectively test an abstaining selection model on unseen merchants. Compare equal-budget portfolios against expert triage, with live shopper holdouts inside each selected intervention. The feature only creates conversion value if it funds better changes. Application-controlled tool output makes replay possible; it does not recreate consumer ChatGPT. [Function calling](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/function-calling).

Keep four evidence layers separate: offline replay, live engine observations, controlled merchant experiments and actual orders. Fifty reruns of one prompt measure model variation, not fifty independent humans. Moving a result upward is a sensitivity test, not an achieved ranking improvement. The shared conversation's numerical recommendation ladders are invented illustrations.

For every proposed experiment, report observed external-AI handoffs and merchant-owned assistant traffic separately. Unknown origins stay unknown. Landing tests estimate the effect after arrival; they cannot establish an upstream discovery gain. Set power from a minimum worthwhile purchase effect, allow returns to mature, and check total merchant outcomes for substitution.

All ten price points in the JSON are unvalidated value-based trial prices in USD. All traffic, conversion and contribution figures are chosen break-even assumptions. None is a predicted uplift. I would start with exclusion preservation and verified stockout recovery where traffic and evidence exist; fit evidence and outfit feasibility need deeper integrations. Simulation calibration remains a research program until prospective purchase results justify using it to allocate merchant money.
