**Algorithmic ideas for incremental AI-shopping purchases**

Snapshot: 29 September 2026. Ten independent proposals, with full structured records in [algorithmic.json](algorithmic.json). Every proposed purchase effect is a hypothesis. None of the sources establishes a transferable conversion uplift for these products.

The most useful early pilots are a constraint compiler for one compatibility-heavy category, a complete-basket solver for one repeated shopping mission, and stockout recovery with enough follow-up to detect cannibalization. They have concrete failure modes and can be compared with an existing merchant experience. The budget allocator is more ambitious and needs substantially more independent purchase data.

The controlled search proxy in the shared conversation can diagnose which evidence or result order changes an agent's choice. Rank manipulation is an oracle sensitivity test, not a deployable ranking gain. Repeated runs of one artificial shopper estimate model variability, not independent human demand. Shopify already offers synthetic shopper comparisons, and its own documentation says results can differ from actual behavior; it also has live treatment/control rollout experiments. A generic simulator followed by A/B testing is therefore an existing product pattern. [Shopify Help Center | SimGym](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym); [Shopify Help Center | Types of rollouts and changes](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/markets/rollouts/rollout-types)

Most proposals below require control of a merchant assistant, landing page or approved tool integration. They can improve a purchase journey that began in AI search without claiming control of the original answer engine. ChatGPT uses its own product and merchant selection process, while direct feed onboarding remains an approved-partner process. A better custom retrieval endpoint does not automatically change consumer ChatGPT results. [Shopping with ChatGPT Search](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search); [Get Started](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/guides/get-started)

For live tests, determine eligibility before treatment, preserve shopper assignment and include non-engagers in the denominator. Separate verified AI referrals, approved handoffs and merchant-assistant sessions. Unknown origins remain unknown. Measure paid orders and matured cancellations/returns; diagnostic shortlist, click, answer or add-to-cart rates are secondary. If a feed change affects everyone, use a defensible merchant/category cluster design rather than pretending there was a per-user feed split. All sample sizes need the merchant's baseline and minimum worthwhile effect. Calendar durations below are planning windows, not power guarantees.

**1. Compile shopping requests into verified hard constraints**

Camera-accessory, computer-peripheral and specialist equipment retailers with frequent compatibility questions. Semantic retrieval can return plausible items that violate one decisive requirement, while ordinary filters miss requirements expressed in a sentence. Build a constraint compiler with typed units, model/version relations and three states per predicate: supported, contradicted or unknown. Return an evidence-backed candidate set, its smallest conflicting constraint set when empty, and explicit alternatives the shopper can approve. Start with one category and a merchant-controlled AI landing page or retrieval endpoint.

Selection stage: translate an explicit request into testable requirements, eliminate verified mismatches and expose unresolved facts. More shoppers can reach a defensible suitable choice and complete a retained purchase. External discovery improves only if an approved channel actually uses the resulting data or tool. The edge to test is specific: For requests with several hard requirements, verified relational constraints and an explicit unknown state outperform a well-configured incumbent semantic search plus facets on retained purchases without excessive false exclusions.

Constructor ASA already handles natural-language filtering and refinement; constraint-based knowledge-graph recommenders exist. Constructor also advertises Answer Engine Assistants beta. A natural-language filter or generic compatibility badge is not the edge. Hypothesized purchase effect. constraint-paper supports technical feasibility; ecom-bench supports a requirement-level offline test design only; constructor-asa and constructor-home establish overlap. No direct conversion percentage is supported. [A Constraint-based Recommender System via RDF Knowledge Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10702); [EComAgentBench: Benchmarking Shopping Agents on Long-Horizon Tasks with Distributed Hidden Intent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17698); [Learn about AI Shopping Agent](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/products-ai-shopping-agents-learn-about-ai-shopping-agent); [AI Ecommerce Search and Product Discovery | Constructor](https://constructor.com/); [Shopping with ChatGPT Search](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search)

Test: Persistently randomize eligible shoppers before results between the compiler and the merchant's strongest configured search/facet experience. Enroll all requests identified as constraint-bearing before assignment. Primary endpoint is a paid purchase within 14 days retained through day 45; use 6-8 weeks of enrollment plus maturation, with sample size set from the merchant baseline and minimum worthwhile effect. Guardrails: incompatible recommendations, false exclusions, latency and contribution per shopper. ITT difference in retained-purchaser probability: unique assigned shoppers with at least one retained order divided by every eligible assigned shopper. Count all merchant purchases in the category, not just purchases of the promoted candidate. Report AI-referral and merchant-assistant cohorts separately.

The main failure is concrete: Bad mappings reject good products, missing specifications create empty results, or an extra verification step adds more friction than it removes. Reject the claim if a powered test rules out the minimum worthwhile retained-purchase increase, or verified incompatibility/false-exclusion guardrails worsen. Better simulated constraint satisfaction alone does not rescue it.

Economics: Unvalidated value-based test price: GBP500/month plus separately quoted integration. Require a paid pilot; no published demand estimate is asserted. No observed traffic or conversion baseline. Chosen sensitivity: GBP40 contribution per additional retained purchaser; GBP600 recurring monthly fee plus operation cost. Break-even requires 600/40 = 15 additional retained purchasers monthly, or an absolute rate change of 15/N for N eligible monthly shoppers. Add amortized integration costs to the numerator. Zero lift loses GBP600; false exclusions can make the purchase effect negative. Cost control: Use deterministic predicates after one parse, cache stable compatibility edges and reserve model calls for unresolved language. Never reuse cached stock or price without a valid freshness policy.


**2. Ask only the question that can change the purchase decision**

Retailers of considered purchases whose AI shoppers frequently arrive with incomplete requirements. A chatbot can ask many reasonable questions that do not change the candidate set, while failing to ask the one question that prevents a wrong or abandoned purchase. Build a question-selection policy that compares expected decision regret with the predicted cost of an extra turn. Candidate actions include asking one bounded question, showing a small provisional shortlist, or proceeding. Ask only for task-relevant preferences, offer 'not sure', and expose why the answer matters.

Selection stage: estimate whether one answer would change the suitable shortlist enough to justify the shopper's effort. A useful clarification should reduce a material mismatch or comparison burden before the shopper leaves. The edge to test is specific: A policy trained on randomized real-user responses and retained purchases asks fewer irrelevant questions and produces more retained purchasers than either a fixed questionnaire or unrestricted LLM conversation.

Constructor ASA already supports conversational refinement. Research already studies appropriate question selection. This proposal is a narrow policy within those experiences, not a new chatbot category. Hypothesized. question-paper and ecom-bench provide adjacent question-selection and scripted-intent evaluation precedents. Neither measures this policy's incremental human purchases. [Learn about AI Shopping Agent](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/products-ai-shopping-agents-learn-about-ai-shopping-agent); [Learning to Ask Appropriate Questions in Conversational Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04774); [EComAgentBench: Benchmarking Shopping Agents on Long-Horizon Tasks with Distributed Hidden Intent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17698); [Doubly Robust Policy Evaluation and Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.4601)

Test: Randomize eligible shoppers at their first ambiguous request to decision-value questioning, the existing assistant, or a brief fixed questionnaire. Keep product coverage and incentives equal. Use 6-8 weeks of enrollment plus a 45-day return window; power the main comparison for retained purchasers, not response rate. Cap questions and monitor latency, abandonment and unsuitable orders. Retained purchasers within the prespecified 14-day purchase window divided by all assigned ambiguous-intent shoppers, including those who never answer. Report question-answer rate as a diagnostic only. Category-wide purchase measurement catches simple switching between suggested SKUs.

The main failure is concrete: The value model confuses engagement with buying intent, asks an intrusive question, or expects shoppers to know technical details they cannot answer. Drop the approach if it cannot outperform a one-question heuristic on retained purchases at acceptable abandonment, or if any measured benefit disappears when question count and latency are matched.

Economics: Unvalidated value-based test price: GBP400/month for a category pilot, with integration priced separately. No measured baseline. Assume solely for break-even GBP40 contribution per extra retained purchaser and GBP500/month total recurring cost. 500/40 = 12.5, so at least 13 additional retained purchasers per month are needed before setup costs. Required absolute rate gain is 12.5/N. Unhelpful questions can reduce purchases; zero gain leaves the full cost uncovered. Cost control: Precompute answerable questions and shortlist partitions; stop after one useful question unless the shopper requests more help.


**3. Solve the complete purchase mission before recommending a basket**

DIY, hobby, office-setup and consumer-electronics retailers selling complementary components. Individually attractive recommendations can form an unusable or over-budget basket: a missing adapter, duplicate component, incompatible connector or invalid discount prevents completion. Build a graph and constraint solver over component roles, pairwise compatibility, ownership supplied by the shopper, available variants, taxes/fees and valid promotions. Return a complete minimum-cost feasible basket plus optional upgrades separately. Revalidate against authoritative cart totals before checkout.

Selection and cart stages: satisfy a whole task with the minimum complete set of compatible items and a checked total. Removing missing-component and budget uncertainty can convert shoppers who would otherwise abandon a project purchase. The edge to test is specific: For multi-item missions, a verified complete basket yields more retained purchasing shoppers than the incumbent agent or co-purchase recommender while reducing missing-component support and returns.

Constructor already supports goal-based multi-item recommendations, and Algolia has strict co-purchase bundles. Native cart tools already exist. The proposed difference is deterministic mission completion and budget feasibility, not displaying several products or creating a cart. Hypothesized. combo-bench offers a simulated evaluation structure; constructor-asa and algolia-fbt show strong direct overlap. No cited source demonstrates our incremental purchase rate. [ComboShoppingBench: Evaluating LLM Agents for Budget-Constrained Basket Shopping with Coupons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09282); [Learn about AI Shopping Agent](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/products-ai-shopping-agents-learn-about-ai-shopping-agent); [Frequently Bought Together Recommendations - “Strict” Mode](https://changelog.algolia.com/frequently-bought-together-recommendations---strict-mode-3OdQfS); [Get Started](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/guides/get-started)

Test: Randomize mission-bearing shoppers before recommendations between solver-generated baskets and the merchant's current goal-based assistant. Keep promotional eligibility equal. Run 8 weeks or the powered sample target, then follow orders through the category's return window. Primary endpoint is a retained purchaser within 14 days; guardrails include incompatible basket rate, over-budget quotes, refunds and margin. All assigned mission shoppers form the denominator. Compare retained-purchaser probability and separately the proportion with a complete verified mission basket. Do not present increased items per order or AOV as increased purchase conversion.

The main failure is concrete: The system invents a necessary component, omits an adapter, over-constrains the basket or pressures shoppers into buying extras they already own. Kill the conversion claim if the solver improves basket validity but yields no worthwhile retained-purchaser gain, or the incumbent assistant reaches equivalent results at materially lower cost.

Economics: Unvalidated value-based test price: GBP1,000/month plus category modelling and cart integration. No measured traffic/conversion. Chosen values: GBP60 incremental contribution per retained purchasing shopper and GBP1,500/month total recurring cost. 1,500/60 = 25 extra retained purchasing shoppers per month before setup. Absolute break-even rate gain is 25/N. Existing buyers adding components are secondary value, not counted as newly converted purchasers. Zero or negative purchaser effects remain possible. Cost control: Restrict the first release to a few repeatable missions; use integer/constraint solving and cached compatibility rather than repeated open-ended model reasoning.


**4. Recover stockouts with an explicit substitute-versus-wait policy**

Merchants with high variant turnover and AI referrals to products that become unavailable. A generic similar-products carousel may miss the requirement that made a shopper choose an unavailable item, or divert a shopper who would have bought the original after a short wait. Build a conditional substitution graph with shopper-visible differences and protected requirements. Use restock uncertainty, explicit deadline, delivered cost and historic accepted substitutions to choose a small recovery set. Preserve the original item and let the shopper explicitly approve any relaxed preference.

Landing and selection stages: identify which properties are essential, then choose between a verified substitute, a genuinely available alternate variant, or a truthful wait option. The policy seeks additional retained shoppers after a stockout while accounting for purchases merely moved forward in time. The edge to test is specific: On failed AI arrivals, requirement-preserving recovery produces more retained purchasers over 60 days than strong related-product recommendations, rather than merely shifting orders away from the original SKU or a later visit.

Algolia already recommends alternatives to out-of-stock items and supports stock filters. This is a decision policy over substitute, variant and wait options, with longer-horizon cannibalization measurement, not an out-of-stock carousel invention. Hypothesized policy effect; algolia-stockout and algolia-rules establish direct competition. assortment-paper supports modelling substitution but supplies no live AI-origin conversion estimate. [Co-op: Scaling Grocery Search and Improving Basket Sizes With Algolia](https://www.algolia.com/customers/co-op); [Merchandise Recommendations](https://www.algolia.com/ecommerce-merchandising-playbook/recommendations-rules); [Joint assortment and inventory planning for heavy tailed demand](https://www.amazon.science/publications/joint-assortment-and-inventory-planning-for-heavy-tailed-demand)

Test: Persistently randomize shoppers at the first verified stockout between policy recovery and the existing related-products/waitlist experience. Run 6-8 weeks plus 60-day follow-up; size from stockout-cohort volume. Primary endpoint is a retained purchasing shopper over the whole 60-day horizon. Guardrails: unsuitable replacements, higher returns, misleading restock promises and lost margin. Unique retained purchasers across all merchant SKUs divided by every assigned stockout shopper. Report near-term rescue orders separately from 60-day incrementality. An order for the substitute alone does not prove a saved sale.

The main failure is concrete: The substitute violates an unstated hard requirement, inaccurate restock estimates distort the choice, or the system takes credit for a purchase that would have happened later. Reject if the 60-day all-SKU retained-purchaser effect fails the minimum worthwhile threshold despite a higher immediate substitute-order rate.

Economics: Unvalidated value-based test price: GBP500/month; qualify merchants on actual stockout traffic before selling. No observed fault prevalence or baseline. Chosen sensitivity: GBP35 contribution per truly additional retained shopper and GBP700/month total recurring cost. 700/35 = 20 additional retained shoppers in the stockout cohort each month before integration cost. Rate threshold is 20/N_stockout. If substitution only cannibalizes later purchases, incremental value is zero before cost; poor substitutions can make it negative. Cost control: Precompute proven substitute edges and evaluate only currently available options. Do not spend inference on stockouts with no useful recovery action.


**5. Turn room images into a conservative physical-fit decision**

Furniture and storage merchants receiving AI-origin shoppers with a specific space constraint. Visual similarity and attractive room renders do not resolve whether a real item fits through an opening, leaves requested clearance or fits inside a recess. Create a bounded geometry checker for a narrow use case such as shelving inside an alcove. Accept manual dimensions or supported room scans, carry measurement uncertainty, test product/packaging dimensions and requested clearance, and abstain when margin is insufficient. Render the checked measurements beside the product; ask the shopper to confirm critical dimensions.

Selection stage: combine explicitly supplied measurements or a consented scan with product geometry and uncertainty bounds. A verifiable fit decision can resolve hesitation and prevent unsuitable orders, producing more retained purchases. The edge to test is specific: For shoppers with a narrow spatial requirement, uncertainty-aware fit checking beats an incumbent scaled visualization plus dimension table on retained-purchaser rate and fit-related refunds.

IKEA Kreativ already scans or builds rooms and places products at scale. Apple RoomPlan already supplies parametric geometry. The edge must be better decision accuracy and checkout outcomes for a specific fit task across a merchant's assortment, not another room visualization. Hypothesized. apple-roomplan establishes building blocks and ikea-kreativ direct competition. Neither supports our conversion estimate or a universal accuracy guarantee. [RoomPlan Overview - Augmented Reality - Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/augmented-reality/roomplan/); [Can I resize an item or adjust the dimensions of the room in IKEA Kreativ?](https://www.ikea.com/us/en/customer-service/knowledge/articles/3d2f761d-842f-4f1f-aa9b-0e3f91330d59.html)

Test: Randomize eligible AI-origin landing visitors before the fit-tool invitation to checker versus current visualizer/dimension table. Count non-users of the offered tool in treatment. Use 8-12 weeks and a 60-day post-order return window; high-value low-frequency categories may require multiple merchants. Primary endpoint is a retained purchase; guardrails include false-fit decisions, scan burden, abandonment and privacy complaints. Retained purchasers within 30 days divided by all assigned eligible shoppers, with the category's return window matured. Secondary fit-error rate uses independently checked measurements; do not substitute render engagement for purchases.

The main failure is concrete: Photos lack scale, scans miss obstacles, product dimensions exclude protrusions, or the visual confidence of the interface exceeds the evidence. Stop if independent checks find unacceptable false-fit rates, or a powered purchase test cannot beat a simple measurement form plus existing visualization after return maturation.

Economics: Unvalidated value-based test price: GBP1,500/month plus scan/geometry integration, appropriate only for merchants with enough relevant demand. No observed traffic/conversion. Chosen sensitivity: GBP150 contribution per extra retained purchaser and GBP2,000/month total recurring cost. 2,000/150 = 13.34, so at least 14 additional retained purchasers monthly before integration and measurement costs. Absolute rate threshold is 13.34/N. Reduced unsuitable orders may lower gross orders while improving retained orders; scan friction can make both negative. Cost control: Start with manual dimensions and simple bounding geometry; invoke scanning or richer geometry only when it can change a purchase decision. Never present photorealistic rendering as measurement evidence.


**6. Source a small assortment to satisfy unmet AI shopping constraints**

Multi-brand retailers with flexible buying teams, small-lot suppliers and repeated unsatisfied shopping requests. Better metadata cannot create a product the merchant does not carry. Aggregate bestseller lists can miss a smaller but purchase-ready combination of requirements. Build a demand-and-assortment optimizer that selects small pilot buys under inventory, working-capital and return constraints. Use observed merchant AI requests, explicit shopper feedback and historical substitution; separate absence of a suitable SKU from poor retrieval. Recommend concrete purchasable supplier SKUs with uncertain demand, not invented products or synthetic purchase intent.

Offer creation stage: group real unsatisfied requests by compatible constraints, estimate substitution and willingness to buy, and stock a limited set that covers those requests. Incremental purchases arise only if genuinely unmet demand materializes without merely displacing existing sales. The edge to test is specific: A small inventory allocation chosen from repeated unsatisfied constraint sets creates more incremental retained purchasers per pound of inventory risk than the buyer's existing trend/popularity process.

Google Merchant Center already suggests popular products and brands to stock. Assortment and inventory optimization with substitution is established research. The proposed edge is exact unmet constraint combinations from AI-origin demand plus a controlled physical-stock pilot. Hypothesized. google-assortment establishes the native alternative and assortment-paper a substitution-aware modelling precedent. Neither validates a market size or this tool's causal sales effect. [About popular products in Merchant Center Analytics](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13299535?hl=en-IE); [Joint assortment and inventory planning for heavy tailed demand](https://www.amazon.science/publications/joint-assortment-and-inventory-planning-for-heavy-tailed-demand)

Test: Randomize matched, independently stocked region-category cells to an equal working-capital pilot selected by the optimizer or the existing buying process. Keep marketing support equal and predefine eligibility before inventory is added. Run at least one replenishment cycle, typically 12 weeks plus returns; require enough independent cells and account for stock spillovers. Primary endpoint is incremental retained purchasers among all eligible AI-origin category shoppers; guardrails are unsold stock, margin and fulfillment. ITT retained-purchaser probability across the whole category, with randomized cell inference. Report total contribution after carrying cost and cannibalization separately. Sales of the newly sourced SKU alone are not evidence of incremental demand.

The main failure is concrete: Requests express aspiration rather than willingness to pay, seasonality invalidates the estimate, or the new assortment cannibalizes better-margin products. Abandon if repeated small-stock pilots fail to outperform ordinary buying after category-wide cannibalization, return and carrying costs, even if the sourced SKUs sell.

Economics: Unvalidated value-based pilot price: GBP2,000/month; inventory and buying labor are separate merchant costs. Seek a paid pilot only after proving sufficient unmet real demand. No assumed traffic or demand prevalence. Chosen sensitivity: GBP45 incremental contribution per retained shopper, GBP2,500 monthly software/operation cost and GBP1,000 monthly expected carrying/markdown cost. (2,500 + 1,000)/45 = 77.78, so at least 78 incremental retained shoppers monthly before setup are needed. Absolute rate threshold is 77.78/N. A false demand signal can yield zero sales, negative incremental contribution and stranded inventory. Cost control: Begin with small-lot suppliers and a few request clusters; stop buying when posterior demand cannot cover carrying cost. Reuse aggregate demand summaries rather than retaining unnecessary raw conversations.


**7. Buy the missing product evidence with the highest decision value**

Specialist merchants whose shoppers repeatedly hesitate over a fact absent from supplier data. A product assistant can retrieve every available document and still lack the decisive fact, such as sleeve opening width or the clearance needed to open a storage unit. Build a value-of-information queue that ranks concrete evidence-acquisition tasks by affected real demand, probability the answer changes a shortlist, uncertainty and acquisition cost. Issue a precise measurement protocol to merchant staff or a supplier, validate units/provenance, and publish the factual result regardless of whether it is commercially favorable. Initial scope excludes safety-critical claims.

Understanding and selection stages: identify factual uncertainty that blocks otherwise suitable candidates, obtain a real measurement or supplier confirmation, then make the result available during the decision. The enabling algorithm creates value only when acquired evidence changes actual retained purchases. The edge to test is specific: A fixed evidence budget spent on facts that can change a real purchase decision produces more retained purchasers than a catalogue-completeness queue or existing gap scorer.

Constructor Product Insights already answers from product data, reviews and merchant content; Gorgias supports custom product facts; Lily Max scores and tests data gaps. The narrow difference is choosing and funding a new physical measurement or supplier investigation rather than extracting or rewriting existing information. Hypothesized. constructor-pia, gorgias-data and lily-max show substantial overlap in answers and gap management. No source supports a conversion percentage or proves competitors lack this exact acquisition policy. [Learn about Product Insights Agent](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/products-ai-shopping-agents-learn-about-product-insights-agent); [Manage your product information for AI Agent](https://docs.gorgias.com/en-US/manage-your-product-information-for-ai-agent-4036592); [How Lily Max Works | Agentic Product Intelligence](https://www.lily.ai/how-it-works)

Test: Use two equally funded queues: decision-value selection versus the merchant's current missing-field priority. Assign product-family/store clusters before work and keep display treatment consistent. In a second randomized shopper test, compare newly measured factual answers with the current truthful information experience. Enroll for 8-12 weeks plus returns, with enough independent clusters for the queue comparison. Primary endpoint is category-wide retained purchasers, not resolved questions; check incorrect claims and cross-cluster spillover. ITT change in retained-purchaser probability for all eligible shoppers in assigned clusters; measure incremental contribution per pound of acquired evidence as a secondary efficiency result. Count unfavorable evidence that correctly disqualifies a product, and do not count successful measurements as sales.

The main failure is concrete: The algorithm values frequently asked trivia, measurements vary between variants or production batches, or the merchant publishes only favorable results. Reject if targeted evidence does not beat a simple frequency-times-cost queue on retained purchases under equal spend, or if physical verification costs exceed the contribution recovered.

Economics: Unvalidated value-based test price: GBP750/month with an explicit separate evidence-work budget. A paid fixed-budget pilot is the demand test. No observed traffic or blocked-demand proportion. Chosen sensitivity: GBP50 contribution per extra retained purchaser; GBP750 monthly fee and GBP500 monthly measurement labor. (750 + 500)/50 = 25 additional retained purchasers monthly before setup. Required absolute purchase-rate gain is 25/N. A newly measured fact can reduce purchases, and evidence that resolves no material uncertainty has zero conversion value. Cost control: Acquire reusable family-level facts when valid, batch supplier questions, and stop acquiring evidence whose expected decision value is below its measured cost.


**8. Allocate merchant changes by incremental purchase value under a fixed budget**

Multi-store merchants and agencies with many credible improvements but limited engineering, experimentation and traffic budgets. A list of plausible changes can spend the full budget on interventions that improve simulated recommendation scores or clicks without creating additional purchasing shoppers. Build a budget-constrained policy over approved actions, such as compatibility answers, variant routing or checkout error repairs. Log action propensities and costs, use conservative offline estimates only where logged actions have support, retain a fixed randomized control, and promote actions only after mature order/refund outcomes. Treat simulation results as hypothesis features, never rewards.

Experiment allocation stage: choose which deployable change to test next using expected incremental retained purchases, uncertainty, cost and reversibility. The resulting live changes must produce more purchases than an equally funded incumbent optimization workflow. The edge to test is specific: At equal total cost and experiment traffic, this policy finds and deploys more economically worthwhile improvements than a strong human/Lily-style prioritization process without materially worse shopper outcomes during learning.

Dynamic Yield already has bandit traffic allocation and purchase/revenue objectives. Shopify has SimGym and live Rollouts. Lily Max advertises an enrichment/test/deploy loop with holdouts. The proposed edge is resource allocation across distinct merchant actions with mature purchase outcomes and deployability constraints, not 'AI simulation plus A/B'. Hypothesized. dr-policy supports an estimator family under assumptions; dy-allocation, dy-metric, lily-max, shopify-simgym and shopify-rollouts establish strong existing competition. No theoretical guarantee is transferred to sparse, delayed commerce outcomes. [Dynamic Allocation](https://support.dynamicyield.com/hc/en-us/articles/360034513414-Dynamic-Allocation); [Choosing a Primary Metric](https://support.dynamicyield.com/hc/en-us/articles/360026368213-Choosing-a-Primary-Metric); [Doubly Robust Policy Evaluation and Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.4601); [How Lily Max Works | Agentic Product Intelligence](https://www.lily.ai/how-it-works); [Shopify Help Center | SimGym](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym); [Shopify Help Center | Types of rollouts and changes](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/markets/rollouts/rollout-types)

Test: Randomize matched merchant or independent store-category clusters to adaptive allocation versus a prespecified incumbent prioritization process, with equal cash/engineering budgets and a fixed shopper holdout inside each arm. Use at least a 12-week budget cycle plus order maturation; power by independent clusters, not simulation reruns. Primary endpoint is cumulative incremental retained purchasers versus each arm's holdout. Guardrails include returns, gross margin, latency and irreversible actions. Compare policy-level incremental retained purchasers per eligible shopper and total incremental purchasers within the same spend cap. Also report contribution net of implementation cost. A better off-policy value estimate or higher synthetic recommendation share is not a purchase result.

The main failure is concrete: Sparse delayed outcomes make the policy chase noise; poorly logged propensities invalidate offline estimates; interference between SKU changes makes apparent gains noncausal. Drop the policy if it cannot outperform prespecified expert prioritization under equal budgets across two powered budget cycles, or requires unsupported counterfactual extrapolation for most action choices.

Economics: Unvalidated value-based test price: GBP2,000/month for an agency or multi-store pilot; a small merchant is unlikely to provide enough experimental information. No baseline traffic or uplift assumed. Chosen sensitivity: GBP50 contribution per additional retained purchaser and GBP2,500/month allocator plus extra operation cost over the comparator. The allocator must cause at least 2,500/50 = 50 extra retained purchasers per month beyond the equally funded comparator, before setup. Absolute threshold is 50/N. If it merely picks the same actions later, value can be zero or negative. Cost control: Batch compatible actions, reject tests unable to reach a useful decision, and stop expensive replay once it no longer changes the live experiment choice.


**9. Spend extra reasoning only when it can change a purchase decision**

High-volume merchants operating a shopping assistant or approved agent tool with noticeable latency and inference cost. Uniformly expensive reasoning wastes time on easy requests, while a cheap model can fail on the small subset needing compatibility checks or fresh quotes. Build a calibrated decision-stability router using constraint coverage, evidence freshness, model disagreement and price/stock volatility. Actions are a deterministic lookup, a small model, an additional authoritative tool call or a stronger model. Assess whether the final product decision changes, and cap latency. Do not treat model self-confidence as calibrated truth.

Decision and response stages: route easy, verified requests through a fast path and spend computation on ambiguous high-regret cases. Faster useful answers can prevent abandonment; retained savings can fund more valuable verification. Purchase preservation is a required constraint, not an assumed consequence of lower cost. The edge to test is specific: Against both always-expensive reasoning and a simple rule-based router, decision-stability routing maintains or improves retained-purchaser rate while reducing cost and response delay. Savings alone are insufficient if purchases fall.

FrugalGPT and RouteLLM already study cost-aware cascades and routing. Merchant assistants already choose content sources. The edge hypothesis is a commerce-specific objective and guardrails based on real retained purchases and exact decision errors. Hypothesized commerce effect. routellm and frugalgpt establish adjacent routing methods, not current-model savings or shopping conversion lift. [RouteLLM: Learning to Route LLMs with Preference Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665); [FrugalGPT: How to Use Large Language Models While Reducing Cost and Improving Performance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176); [Learn about Product Insights Agent](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/products-ai-shopping-agents-learn-about-product-insights-agent)
