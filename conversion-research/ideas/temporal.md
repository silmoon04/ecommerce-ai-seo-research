# Ten temporal mechanisms for AI-origin purchases

Research snapshot: 29 September 2026. These are hypotheses for product development and merchant experiments. The structured companion, [temporal.json](temporal.json), contains all ten full idea records, including data requirements, comparators, falsifiers, dependencies and price tests.

Time can change whether a product is useful without changing the product itself. A jacket may miss a departure date; a gift may lose its return eligibility before it is opened; a food basket may arrive too early to remain suitable for a party. The product opportunity is to preserve the shopper's dates through selection, payment and fulfillment.

None of the sources reviewed establishes a purchase uplift for these proposed interventions. The source evidence establishes available infrastructure, current competitors and constraints. All ten numerical effect ranges are therefore null. Baseline traffic, fault prevalence, purchase probability and contribution must come from a merchant pilot. The GBP prices in the JSON are unvalidated willingness-to-pay tests, not published market prices.

| ID | Clock | Intervention | Purchase outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| T01 | Latest arrival date | Resolve a delivery or collection route that meets the stated deadline | Retained paid order |
| T02 | Entire period of use | Preserve an available rental interval into checkout | Completed paid rental |
| T03 | Acceptable waiting horizon | Match preorder commitment to the customer's latest acceptable date | Fully paid, fulfilled order |
| T04 | Expiry of the customer's need | Requalify a saved shopping request before each opted-in alert | Qualifying retained purchase |
| T05 | Return eligibility after a gift date | Calculate the actual policy window for the event | Retained gift order |
| T06 | Remaining usable product life | Match use date to batch life or a merchant promise | Retained suitable basket |
| T07 | Selection-to-payment interval | Hold genuinely reserved stock during a checkout handoff | Net retained orders after displacement |
| T08 | Consumption and delivery lead time | Offer a customer-correctable refill reminder | Incremental repeat orders |
| T09 | Channel propagation delay | Coordinate real offer activation with observed readiness | Retained event orders |
| T10 | Category buying window | Schedule proven repairs before demand peaks | More retained orders at a fixed work budget |

**T01. Route each stated arrival deadline to a deliverable offer.** The buyer is a merchant serving gift, travel or event needs. The intervention takes the shopper's stated need-by date, coarse destination, selected variant and current time. It resolves an actual fulfillment service and carries that choice into checkout, where it is checked again. A fallback such as collection matters only if the merchant can provide it.

Google already calculates estimates from handling, transit, business days and cutoffs, and supports more detailed cutoff configuration. The proposed edge is the shopper-specific feasibility calculation and continuity through checkout. It is not a new shipping field. [Google delivery estimates](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/14949917?hl=en), [handling cutoffs](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/16543665?hl=en).

Randomize identifiable AI-origin buyers after the same optional deadline has been collected in both arms. Compare against accurate current estimates. Count retained orders per eligible buyer and check actual on-time arrival. A stricter answer may reduce orders by correctly identifying an impossible deadline. An apparent conversion gain accompanied by more late-delivery refunds kills the claim. This requires carrier and checkout integration.

**T02. Sell availability for the whole usage interval.** A rental merchant sells an interval: equipment for an event, a lens for a shoot or clothing for a trip. The proposed endpoint takes start and end times, quantity and location, checks turnaround buffers, and returns offers that remain available throughout the requested period. Dates survive the handoff to booking checkout.

Booqable already has both calendar availability and quantity availability for an exact interval. The possible advantage is removing the gap between an AI recommendation and a native calendar, including repeated date entry. [Booqable API v4](https://developers.booqable.com/).

Test date-bearing AI-origin requests against the current landing and booking flow. Count completed paid rentals per randomized request after cancellations mature. Double bookings, wrong dates and losses to other booking channels are guardrails. If the native date-aware flow already preserves the context, this idea may have no edge. Rental orders are purchases of a service; keep them separate if the final product scope is strictly ownership purchases.

**T03. Match preorder commitment to the shopper's purchase horizon.** A shopper furnishing a room in several months can accept different supply conditions from someone who needs an item this weekend. The intervention uses that explicitly stated horizon to show an existing preorder option with authoritative delivery estimates, payment milestones and cancellation terms. It avoids treating every unavailable product as either immediately purchasable or irrelevant.

Shopify already supports preorder apps with different initial-payment arrangements. OpenAI accepts availability-date metadata, but that does not imply automatic stock changes or a displayed preorder date. [Shopify preorders](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/purchase-options/pre-orders), [OpenAI product upload specification](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/file-upload/products).

Randomize eligible long-horizon AI-origin buyers between the current truthful page and horizon matching. Deposits and waitlist registrations are intermediate outcomes. The primary endpoint is a fully paid, fulfilled and retained order after the release cycle. This may take months. Supplier reliability and deferred-payment failures can erase an apparent early win.

**T04. Keep a saved shopping need alive only until its own expiry.** A customer who asks for a particular size below a price ceiling before a trip has described a temporary need. A saved-intent record can retain those volunteered constraints, allowed alternatives, notification permission and expiry. Each authorized alert must recheck stock, total price and delivery feasibility. Processing stops after purchase, expiry or withdrawal.

Klaviyo already provides back-in-stock flows; its documented limitation is that the flow cannot recheck inventory before a second message after the initial alert. Shopify already supports long-running exploratory carts. Compound requalification is the hypothesis, rather than reminders or persistence alone. [Klaviyo stock flows](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051612551), [Shopify agent carts](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents/carts-and-checkout).

Compare against the merchant's existing alert workflow with equal contact limits. Count any permitted retained purchase per randomized saved need, including customers who receive no message. The extra consent and constraint form can itself hurt conversion. A customer buying elsewhere is another reason to make reminders easy to stop. Specific channel permission and withdrawal controls are design requirements; the [ICO guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/how-do-we-comply-with-the-pecr-electronic-mail-marketing-rules/) does not make a generic opt-in a universal legal assurance.

**T05. Match gift dates to the actual return window.** This calculator resolves the merchant's actual delivery-based start rule, product exceptions and seasonal policy. Given a gift date and a requested inspection period, it tells the shopper whether the policy covers that period and links the governing terms. It does not extend a policy or replace statutory rights.

Google already supports merchant return data and seasonal overrides, with narrower support for offer-level fields. Shop agents already answer return-window questions. The proposed distinction is reliable event-date arithmetic and exceptions carried into product selection. [Google merchant return policy data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/return-policy), [Shop agent capabilities](https://help.shop.app/en/shop/shopping/personal-agents).

Randomize gift-intent AI-origin shoppers while holding policy generosity constant. Wait through the applicable return period and measure retained orders, returns and disputes. A generic policy summary may already be sufficient. An extension to the merchant's actual return terms would be a separate intervention and cannot be credited to this calculator.

**T06. Match intended use dates to remaining usable life.** For a dated celebration basket, delivery time alone is insufficient. The product would join the customer's use date to batch allocation or an enforceable merchant promise about life remaining at delivery. It could then offer a later delivery slot, another pack or an alternative that the merchant can actually fulfill.

Ocado already provides a minimum-life promise, so freshness assurance is established competition. The new hypothesis is matching the planned date to an allocatable product through selection and fulfillment. UK guidance distinguishes use-by safety dates and best-before quality dates; neither should be inferred from marketing text or silently extended. [Ocado purchase terms](https://www.ocado.com/content/terms-conditions-purchase-consumer), [food-date guidance](https://www.gov.uk/understanding-food-labelling/best-before-and-use-by-dates).

Randomize shoppers who volunteer a use date before assignment. Count retained paid baskets, while monitoring false promises, substitutions and quality refunds. A warehouse that cannot honor the selected batch or minimum life cannot support the feature. It may also cost more in selection effort or waste than it earns in extra orders.

**T07. Reserve a chosen variant for the real checkout handoff.** A persistent conversational cart can outlive its stock. After a customer explicitly selects a scarce variant, the merchant could make a short, genuine inventory reservation bound to the checkout. It expires on the server and is released on payment, cancellation or timeout. The displayed deadline describes that real reservation and does not reset.

Shopify draft orders can already reserve tracked inventory until a specified time. Shopify agent carts already preserve line items and context. The question is whether a reservation at this particular transition earns net orders after accounting for the buyers blocked by the hold. [Shopify draft order reservations](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/create-orders/create-draft), [agent carts and checkout](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents/carts-and-checkout).

Shared stock makes ordinary shopper-level randomization misleading: a treatment hold can remove inventory from control shoppers. Use independent inventory pools or carefully designed switchback periods with adequate washout. Measure all-channel retained contribution as well as AI-origin orders. Higher conversion among held carts is insufficient if total sales fall.

**T08. Ask for a refill when the customer's supply will actually run out.** The merchant offers an optional reminder after an AI-origin purchase. The customer supplies a preferred refill date or correctable usage rate. The system accounts for quantity and delivery lead time, lets the customer snooze or stop, checks the current offer and asks for purchase approval. It uses merchant-authorized data and does not infer health or sensitive household needs.

Klaviyo already has product-cycle replenishment flows and repurchase suppression. Its documented general next-order prediction does not account for which products were ordered. Shop already supports reordering. The edge would have to beat a well-configured existing replenishment flow. [Klaviyo replenishment](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003195232), [prediction limits](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360020919731), [Shop reordering](https://help.shop.app/en/shop/shopping/personal-agents).

Randomize consenting customers and observe at least two consumption cycles with equal contact budgets. Count retained repeat orders over the whole horizon; moving a purchase earlier is not incremental demand. Label the result as repeat revenue in an AI-origin acquisition cohort. Do not present every later refill as a newly AI-discovered purchase. Personal Shop access also has restrictions on unattended background intermediaries; commercial integration needs its own supported route.

**T09. Start an event offer only after its channel state is ready.** This is an operational scheduler for real offers. It records intended activation, submission, acceptance, observed public state and checkout truth. It prepares supported data early, executes actual updates when due and removes expired offers. Public sampling remains an imperfect observation of what individual shoppers see.

Google promotions undergo policy and SKU reviews and have separate display-date fields. A recrawl request does not guarantee inclusion or immediate freshness. OpenAI's file-upload dates are metadata, so setting them is not an activation job. [Google promotion review](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/4588464?hl=en), [display dates](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13861050?hl=en), [recrawl limits](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/ask-google-to-recrawl), [OpenAI product dates](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/file-upload/products).

Randomize comparable merchant-event clusters with matching promotion terms and ad spend. Count retained orders over the full event and an equal postevent window. Faster processing logs alone prove nothing. Delaying a launch can destroy more demand than it recovers. Countdown language must describe a real constraint; [CMA guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/using-urgency-and-price-reduction-claims-online) identifies misleading urgency as a material concern.

**T10. Spend catalog repair capacity before the category's buying window.** A large merchant rarely has capacity to fix every catalog defect immediately. This scheduler places verified purchase blockers into the work queue using upcoming eligible demand, repair effort, propagation delay and the uncertainty around each repair's measured purchase effect. It needs actual repair evidence before it can claim a useful allocation policy.

Google already provides dated category search-interest signals and popular-product insights. These are inputs and adjacent competition, not a measurement of all AI shopping demand. [Google category search interest](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15335651?hl=en), [Merchant Center popular products](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13299535?hl=en).

Compare randomized merchant or independent category clusters over a full season with identical labor and compute budgets. The outcome is retained AI-origin orders and contribution, not forecast accuracy or the number of repaired fields. Preserve minimum service for smaller categories and some exploration of uncertain fixes. This is the frontier idea in the set because the causal repair evidence may not exist yet.

The shared search-proxy proposal is useful for testing time reasoning. Freeze the clock, timezone, calendars, factual product snapshots and policy version, then replay independent shopper intents around meaningful boundaries: just before and after cutoff, holidays, expired offers, delayed supply, a gift after the return deadline, or no product satisfying the requested use date. Tests should detect a false promise as well as a missed recommendation. Repeated generations for one intent measure model variation; they do not create independent shoppers.

A replay can establish that an intervention changes a controlled agent's understanding or choice. A read-only live check can establish that a particular observed surface exposed current facts. Neither establishes incremental purchases. Real consenting shoppers and merchant order outcomes are required for the purchase experiments above, and public catalog interventions need cluster designs that respect propagation and seasonality. A post-click merchant test establishes conversion among identifiable AI-origin arrivals; it cannot claim to have increased external discovery.

Use source labels fixed before assignment wherever possible, with consented first-party journey continuity for delayed purchases. Audit actual paid orders, refunds and cancellations. For a shopper-level experiment, the basic value calculation is eligible customers multiplied by the randomized change in retained purchase probability and contribution, minus incremental costs. Cluster experiments use order-count or contribution differences over fixed cluster-periods. No factor in that calculation has been measured for this project. Zero and negative effects remain live possibilities.

The shortlist for an initial pilot is T01, T04 or T05 if a merchant can document the corresponding failure. T02 and T06 are more differentiated vertical opportunities with heavier operational dependencies. T07 is attractive only with observable stock races and a displacement-aware test. T08 competes with mature lifecycle tooling. T09 needs repeated events; T10 should wait for evidence that specific repairs cause purchases.
