# Starter stores for building AEO software

Checked 29 September 2026. Assumption: a solo founder, initially serving UK customers, wants inexpensive stores that produce useful operating data for future answer-engine optimisation software. The comparisons are practical judgements, not demand or conversion forecasts.

**Start with one coherent gift/stationery and accessory store. Choose a small apparel store instead if fashion merchants are the intended software customers.** Neither requires designing a new physical product or holding sale inventory. An owned store can teach catalogue enrichment, variant handling, publishing, attribution and customer support before it has enough traffic for reliable conversion experiments.

| Category | Route and operating burden | Useful AEO/software data | Commercial consideration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| POD clothing | Existing blanks; medium burden from artwork, samples, sizing and exchanges | Material, cut, measurements, colour/size variants, occasion and outfit intent | Rich relevance to apparel merchants; production, shipping and remakes constrain contribution |
| Accessories | POD bags/cases or supplier-fulfilled existing goods; low to medium burden | Device compatibility, capacity, material, dimensions, intended use and stock | Practical differentiation is possible; inexpensive singles leave little room for acquisition costs |
| Home organisation | Existing supplier goods; medium burden from measurements, damage and supplier coordination | Fit, capacity, mounting, room constraints and comparisons | Useful functional questions; bulky delivery can undermine an apparently cheap product |
| Gifts and stationery | POD or existing goods; low to medium burden, rising with personalization | Recipient, occasion, budget, print format, customization and delivery deadlines | Coherent designs and sets give a selling proposition; individual low-priced items can be shipping-heavy |
| Digital products | Original or commercially licensed files; low fulfilment burden, creation/support still required | File formats, compatibility, licence, delivery, revisions and bundles | No physical unit or postage cost; weak coverage of stock, shipping and physical returns |

Printful documents no-minimum custom clothing and automatic production/shipping from connected stores, including Shopify; its catalogue also covers accessories and home decor. This verifies a fulfilment route, not attractive margins for every item. [Printful UK clothing](https://www.printful.com/uk/personalised-clothing).

For existing goods, **Shopify Collective deserves serious consideration alongside POD**. Eligible retailers can import other merchants' products without upfront inventory commitments; orders pass to suppliers for fulfilment and tracking syncs back. UK eligibility and same-country/same-currency connections apply. Availability of an inexpensive, suitable supplier assortment still needs checking. [Collective retailer overview](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers), [requirements](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/requirements-and-considerations).

Collective permits editing title, description, media and retail price. Inventory stays supplier-controlled; variants cannot be changed, added or selectively published, and duplicating a product breaks its supplier connection. Shopify documents a 100-variant limit per imported product and lists metafields as incompatible. Treat structured enrichment as something to verify, rather than assuming it travels through Collective. Keep supplier syncing off for copy the software owns; policy changes can overwrite edits. [Product management](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/importing-products), [product policies](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/policies/products), [compatibility](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/requirements-and-considerations).

My first store concept is **bookish gifts and reading-desk goods**, with one visual identity and eight initial base products: notebook, mug, tote, art print, wall calendar, greeting-card pack, sticker sheet and downloadable reading planner. These are assortment candidates, subject to the chosen supplier's actual formats. Offer useful design, size, colour and paper options, expanding towards 50–100 variants when justified. That is a manageable starting range, not a hard cap or a claim that every variant is an independent observation.

Start with fixed artwork. Add personalization once name entry, previews and production handoff work. Gelato supports Shopify personalization previews through its paid Gelato+ offering. Its cards and some stationery are sold in sets of ten, so a single-card price must not be mistaken for the purchasable pack. [Personalization Studio](https://support.gelato.com/en/articles/8996331-what-is-gelato-s-personalization-studio), [stationery quantities](https://support.gelato.com/en/articles/12675537-how-stationery-products-are-sold).

The alternative is **walking-club casualwear**, using existing blanks: relaxed tee, fitted tee, long-sleeve tee, sweatshirt, pullover hoodie, zip hoodie, cap, beanie and tote. This gives nine base products with natural size and colour variation. Position them as everyday clothing; any performance or fit claim needs evidence. This store is more representative of fashion clients than a narrow storage shop, but it needs more sampling and size guidance.

Digital goods work as a useful addition or a cheaper initial exercise. Shopify's Digital Products app can automatically email download links after payment and attach assets to individual variants. A digital-only shop would leave important physical-commerce workflows untested. [Shopify digital delivery](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/digital-service-product/digital-downloads).

Use one domain first to concentrate customer acquisition, content and support. Additional owned mini-stores can deliberately introduce different categories, suppliers or platforms once the shared tooling saves work. Their common operator, acquisition methods and young brands still limit what transfers. Collaborating later with established independent merchants adds different catalogues, customers and operational constraints more directly than multiplying empty stores.

Record product facts, edits, publishing failures, shopper questions and fulfilment outcomes as learning data; distinguish those from model-generated examples. Evaluate retail contribution after fulfilment, refunds, fees and acquisition separately from software time saved and reusable failure cases. No research here establishes likely sales or financial return.

A labelled waitlist can test interest before supply is ready; count sign-ups as sign-ups. Real-order learning begins with a working checkout, confirmed fulfilment route, sampled products and honoured delivery promises. Preserve the distinction between real orders, internal test orders and simulated shopping sessions throughout the dataset.
