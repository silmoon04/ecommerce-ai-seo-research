01 / THE REVISED VIEW

There is a market.
The easy gaps are taken.

I would test a narrow operations product. I would be much less confident about launching another general AI SEO app.

02 / WHERE MARGINS TIGHTEN

Useful software can still
be a difficult business.

These are mechanisms supported by product scope and merchant accounts, not diagnoses of individual company finances.

01

The first cleanup can remove the reason to subscribe.

Metadata, image compression and schema fixes can be valuable once. Recurring value is stronger for stores with frequent imports, launches and theme changes. A quiet catalogue may be better served by a paid project.

The distinction appears in mature-app reviews: continuing uploads create work; already optimised images can produce little visible improvement. TinySEO review ↗

02

The buyer is paying for an outcome the tool only partly controls.

An app can make facts accessible and correct. It cannot promise an answer engine will recommend them, or that a credited order would not have happened anyway. A score with no useful next action is easy to cancel.

Burnish explicitly describes its revenue analysis as directional, not a controlled trial. Google says its AI features need no special optimisation file. Burnish ↗ · Google ↗

03

One support conversation can consume the cheap plan.

A merchant asks why their theme and another app publish different product schema. A person must inspect the installation and decide which system should own the field. The cost is diagnosis and responsibility, even if generating the replacement text is cheap.

Smart SEO reviews describe schema conflicts and theme work; DataFeedWatch separately prices coaching and managed service. Smart SEO review ↗ · DataFeedWatch pricing ↗

04

Monitoring costs multiply with the promise.

More prompts, engines, countries and repeats mean more collection, storage, retries and analysis. A high-priced enterprise plan can fund this. Unlimited coverage on a small merchant's budget needs a very different cost structure.

50prompts×3engines×30days=4,500observations

Illustrative scope, not a vendor's usage or cost. Retries and repetitions add work; a collector's advertised unit price is not an all-in delivery cost.

05

Native tools reset what a basic feature is worth.

Shopify already has catalogue mapping and an agentic readiness scanner. Existing feed products have distribution, customer history and support expertise. A new app must save work beyond the tools the merchant already pays for.

Shopify Catalog Mapping ↗ · Readiness scanner ↗

06

The valuable data may be missing at the source.

An image cannot reliably establish a garment's exact measurements. A generated paragraph cannot settle a supplier conflict. Someone must supply, authorise and maintain the underlying facts. Extraction helps only if review costs less than entering the data properly.

The source-data and fashion reviews examine this distinction. See Feedonomics, Lily AI and True Fit in the product explorer below.

A cheap app can still work very well with self-service onboarding and inexpensive distribution. A company can also have healthy customer contribution while spending heavily on growth. Neither possibility is ruled out by the pressures above.

Formula, assumptions and exclusions

Support budget = price × (1 − target margin − processing fee − revenue share) − delivery cost − monthly churn × acquisition cost.

Divide the budget by the hourly labour cost, then multiply by 60. The churn term estimates steady-state replacement acquisition spending. This is contribution after that spending, not conventional accounting gross margin.

The repair preset includes $15 of delivery, equivalent to 30 minutes at the default $30/hour; its output is additional support time. Changing the hourly rate does not automatically reprice that fixed delivery allowance.

Excluded: fixed overhead, shared infrastructure not in delivery, founder pay, product development, tax, refunds, failed payments and initial growth acquisition spending. Onboarding is excluded unless you add it to delivery or acquisition cost. Negative support headroom means the target is missed, not necessarily that contribution is negative.

06 / IDEAS WORTH TAKING

Borrow the discipline.
Earn the difference.

FROM BURNISH

Make a change reviewable.

Show the source, the proposed edit, its dependencies and the way back. A merchant should understand what approving a fix will change.

FROM GEOFFY

Check the actual output.

A successful request is not proof of publication. Verify the expected content in the served page. Leave volatile price and availability to the system that owns them.

FROM DATAFEEDWATCH

Price human work separately.

Its page lists managed service at $750/month and coaching at $450/month. Borrow the separation of labour from software, not those prices as validated rates for your offer.

FROM STORERANK AI

Show the difference before writing.

Compare fields, preserve good existing work and let the merchant select edits. “Improve everything” creates unnecessary review and overwrite risk.

FROM SIMPROSYS

Start with a job already budgeted.

Reliable channel publishing and feed maintenance have a concrete place in store operations. AI can assist that job without becoming the whole sales pitch.

FROM CHANNABLE

Make recovery routine.

Version history and approval matter when a rule affects many products. Track which changes actually reached each destination and who can restore them.

FROM PEEC & PROFOUND

Turn observations into decisions.

Keep the prompt, market, engine and date attached to a result. Help someone decide what to change. A larger dashboard alone is weak differentiation.

FROM FEEDONOMICS

Refuse to invent missing facts.

Separate supplied facts from inferred labels. Ask for the authoritative measurement or material when the source cannot support the answer.

07 / WHAT COULD BE VALUABLE

A smaller promise.
A more useful result.

My first test would be a paid catalogue-conflict investigation for a merchant or agency with frequent changes.

The buyer should already own the operational problem. The offer is to save diagnosis and repair time, with evidence. “More AI traffic” can be measured later if there is enough traffic and a credible comparison.

08 / A TEST WORTH PAYING FOR

Sell a small piece
of the real work.

A free pilot can reveal mistakes in the product. Payment and a second use reveal something different. This is a proposed research design, not a claim that a particular conversion rate or price is achievable.

FIND THE INCIDENT

Ask for a recent, unresolved example.

Recruit catalogue operators and agencies that recently changed products, suppliers, themes or markets. Look at the actual ticket, screenshots and source files with permission. Include stores whose existing tools solved the problem, so we learn where the product is unnecessary.

COMPARE THE CURRENT STACK

Make the incumbent the control.

Run the same case through Shopify Mapping, Merchant Center and the installed feed or SEO app. Include Burnish or Geoffy where their workflow fits. Record any additional useful finding, the time needed to reach it, and false alarms.

BOUND THE FREE WORK

Offer a diagnosis with a clear limit.

For example, one product family and one destination, with no automatic writes. Choose a maximum staff-time budget before recruitment. Explain which repairs and ongoing checks will cost money before doing the free work.

REQUEST PAYMENT

Quote a repair before building a subscription.

Time extraction, investigation, review, implementation and follow-up. Include acquisition and support in the economics. Price the actual work; do not copy a competitor's monthly fee and assume it covers a person doing the job.

LOOK FOR A SECOND USE

Wait for another launch or incident.

A paid second batch or renewal is stronger evidence than a free user saying the audit was helpful. If buyers only need migration cleanup, sell a project. If review costs erase the saved time, narrow the workflow or stop.

CONTINUE IF

A useful difference survives comparison.

The current stack misses material cases; the proposed workflow saves net operator time; the merchant pays; another paid use follows; delivery leaves enough contribution to support the business.

STOP OR CHANGE IF

The value disappears under scrutiny.

Native tools already solve the case, sources cannot support a correction, false alarms create work, or the only repeatable buyer is another free tester.

That is the product opportunity I would fund learning about. A general AI SEO score, unlimited free monitoring and a future “data moat” would not be enough to justify building the business yet.
