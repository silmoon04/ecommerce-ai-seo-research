# Premium Shopify AI-discovery apps: deep dive

**Snapshot: 2026-09-29.** Prices, plan details, and review counts are live-page snapshots and may change. Vendor feature and efficacy statements below are claims unless clearly identified as merchant reports. A review's duration is not proof of a paid subscription or retention; attributed orders do not establish incremental lift. The actual profitability of any app is unknown absent direct disclosure.

## Burnish

Burnish sells a costly, unusually complete “measure → diagnose → change → remeasure” loop: $149/$299/$649 monthly, with plan-dependent coverage of 3–5 AI engines, 1,500–7,500 answer reads, 300–2,000 monthly product optimizations, and articles. Authority adds its “per-action lift + cohort ROI” feature; the vendor says its measurement is directional rather than a controlled trial. This explicit qualification is useful: the homepage and feature pages also promise a projected score lift and a later observed score movement, which are readiness measures and do not prove more profitable orders. [App Store plan and reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/burnish) · [pricing and limits](https://useburnish.com/pricing/) · [method and lift caveat](https://useburnish.com/what-it-does/).

Its clearest strengths are breadth and implementation detail. The public catalog page specifies 41 change types, 29 gated behind approval by default, six dependency checks, before-state records, and one-click rollback; only a subset of deterministic actions can become autonomous. This is a concrete answer to merchant fear of overwriting good copy. Two App Store reviewers (7–8 days) praise visibility/content structure and support. They are positive early-use signals, not proof they paid or renewed. A central limitation is price relative to outcome certainty: $149 is substantial for a small shop before evidence that AI discovery produces enough incremental contribution. Another is measurement noise: repeat reads of model answers, readiness scores, first-party attributed AI sessions, and cohorts help observe movement, but none on its own isolates the effect of edits from seasonality, promotions, or channel mix. There are only two public reviews in the captured listing and no published adverse review evidence in that sample; that says little about broader satisfaction.

Economic pressure is conditional: a merchant may cancel if the recurring answer-monitoring and content workload costs more than realized incremental gross profit, or if the product's many actions create review work. Improve by making a low-cost catalog pilot available, publishing the denominator and uncertainty behind lift estimates, separating operational completion from commercial outcomes, and offering a randomized/holdout design where feasible. Borrow its explicit autonomy levels, approval queue, audit trail, rollback, dependency checks, and honest measurement caveat. Avoid treating “AI-ready” points or attributed revenue as causal ROI, and avoid autonomous rewriting of fields already performing well. Opportunity: controlled, product-level tests joining approved edits to contribution margin and durable query-level visibility. Falsifier: after adequate, preregistered sample sizes, vetted changes fail to beat unchanged controls on incremental contribution or meaningful visibility, while review/rollback burden remains high.

## StoreRank AI

StoreRank is a recurring, low-to-mid-priced Shopify suite: $29/$79/$149/$289 monthly for Starter/Pro/Growth/Scale (the App Store currently exposes full price/limits as $29 Starter and $79 Pro; its own pricing page lists all four). It combines schema/crawler setup, product and FAQ optimization, an article autopilot, mention/citation monitoring and first-touch AI traffic/order/revenue attribution. [Shopify listing, pricing and reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/storerank-ai) · [vendor pricing](https://www.storerank.ai/pricing/) · [product optimizer guide](https://help.storerank.ai/articles/9105371-how-to-use-the-ai-product-optimizer) · [attribution method](https://help.storerank.ai/en/help/articles/0709433-how-to-use-the-ai-traffic-and-revenue-analytics).

A meaningful product strength is that help docs clarify the actual workflow: product changes are drafts, merchants compare before/after fields and previews, and publish only selected fields; by default it skips fields passing its checks. Its FAQ admits recommendations cannot control authority or third-party model behavior. The App Store listing had 28–29 five-star reviews in current search snapshots; among the described experiences, one merchant says the app was useful after five months, while several reviewers describe days or weeks of use. A reviewer who used it for about two months explicitly says revenue impact was too early to measure. Some list support and content quality as helpful, and the vendor replies to reviews. These support usability and service hypotheses, not paid retention or efficacy. Current pricing pages disagree in presentation (dynamic vendor price boxes render as $0 while comparison rows and Shopify show published tiers), so buyers should verify the actual in-app offer.

Profit pressure is conditional: articles and optimizations create recurring content maintenance and review load; merchants may stop paying when the catalog backlog is cleared or if a first-touch channel attribution is mistaken for causal value. The 30-day return-customer attribution window can credit AI discovery to an order without establishing whether AI caused it. Improvements: show source-confidence and referral rules next to revenue, make ongoing value visible through catalog-change alerts and measured tests, state whether tracking cadence is live or sampled, and keep the draft/approval baseline. Borrow the needs-work triage, editable field-level drafts, brand voice controls praised in reviews, plus plain-language limits in docs. Avoid equating schema/llms.txt installation with discoverability or promising AI-driven sales from attributed orders. Opportunity: recurring, trustworthy maintenance plus a holdout workflow tied to gross margin, not just an SEO score. Falsifier: merchants cannot identify a repeatable monthly task or controlled incremental gain after setup, and renewal falls once initial fixes are complete.

## Geoffy

Geoffy focuses on storefront-side product truth: it adds visible buyer-intent answers and matching structured data, checks claims against merchant catalog data, holds unverified claims for confirmation, and aims to keep widget/schema/page information synchronized as price or variant data changes. It also has free GEO scoring and a pathway diagnostic for on-site versus off-site gaps. Public price is inconsistent by channel: Geoffy’s site says bundles from £199/month, while the Shopify listing starts at $249/month; listing tiers currently show $249, $649 and $1,949/month. Monitoring and Influence are described as early access on the vendor site, even as App Store tiers advertise specific monitoring and outreach-pack inclusions. Confirm availability and currency before purchase. [official product/availability](https://geoffy.ai/) · [Shopify plans/reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/geoffy) · [FAQ on data/implementation](https://geoffy.ai/docs/faq).

Strengths are source-grounded claims and a visible page layer rather than invisible metadata alone; its FAQ explains why it deliberately leaves live price/availability to Shopify's commerce node to avoid stale duplicate offers. Reviews include PadelDogs, a small UK padel retailer, reporting five months of use and higher traffic and sales, plus Shakeup's five-month account of making AI discovery feel manageable. Another reviewer says ChatGPT began mentioning its wallet after buying guides were added, but had used the app only three days. Geoffy replies are absent from these three listing reviews, so there is no vendor response to assess. This is the strongest duration and outcome report among these four, but none states plan/payment or gives baseline, order values, margins, or a counterfactual. It remains self-reported association, not proven incremental profit.

Pressure is conditional: £199/$249 entry pricing requires larger expected value than a one-time SEO cleanup, and the buyer must maintain accurate product facts and approve generated surfaces. The long-running monitor and influence features remain early access on the vendor site; this constrains what is independently inspectable. Improvements: harmonize price/feature availability across site and App Store, clarify monitoring sample design and geography, and offer a transparent holdout report with conversion/contribution outcomes and caveats. Borrow its fact verification, refusal to invent vague claims, drift control, server-rendered visible content, and distinction between fixable on-site gaps and off-site authority. Avoid treating “gets products named” as guaranteed outcomes or saying a holdout “proves” lift without adequate randomization, sample size, and margin outcomes. Opportunity: fact-safe catalog enrichment plus external-source acquisition recommendations with controlled measurement. Falsifier: products with verified content changes show no durable improvement in relevant answer inclusion or incremental contribution versus matched/held-out items, or merchants won't sustain price after initial fixes.

## AppearAI

AppearAI is not really a premium recurring suite: it has a free visibility audit (score across eight dimensions, a preview question, one competitor, up to three scans/day) and a $19.90 one-time report with all 20 buyer questions, full competitor snapshots and step-by-step content suggestions. It is therefore a low-cost diagnostic/implementation aid, not a recurring autonomous monitoring product. [Shopify listing, plan and review](https://apps.shopify.com/appearai).

The merchant-facing value is specific and accessible: it tells a nontechnical store owner what content to add and where, with no coding. The only English-language App Store review (15 July 2026) says the reviewer paid $19.90, found the tips actionable, and reports 23 AI-attributed orders. The app page does not show a vendor reply in the extracted review. This is unusually direct evidence of one paid purchase, and a merchant outcome claim, but 15 days and the one-time charge give no evidence of renewal. Attribution, baseline traffic, margin, order verification and counterfactual are unavailable. Treat it as a promising low-friction purchase signal, not an ROI proof. One review is too little to judge the broader user experience, and no negative merchant evidence appeared in the sampled listing; neither fact demonstrates product failure or success.

Profit pressure is conditional and structurally clear: a one-time report needs new stores or a follow-on paid service to produce repeat revenue; buyers may use recommendations once and leave, while low price caps revenue per customer. If recommendations are generic, hard to implement, or do not improve AI exposure, the report's apparent affordability won't prevent dissatisfaction. Improve by adding an optional change log and re-scan after implementation, explicit source/rationale per recommendation, and an appropriately priced recurring monitor only if it shows meaningful temporal change. Borrow the free preview → paid full report ladder, low price, and copy-paste instructions. Avoid overstating “real questions” or “AI traffic orders” as representative queries or causal sales, and don't force a subscription before recurring value exists. Opportunity: independent post-fix verification for small merchants, including a simple control comparison and contribution-margin capture. Falsifier: purchasers do not implement recommendations, fail to return for a re-scan, or cannot distinguish meaningful signal from model-answer variation.

## Cross-product read

These four products show different economic bets: Burnish sells continuous action and measurement at a high subscription price; StoreRank bundles recurring production and analytics at several price points; Geoffy sells higher-priced, verified product-page work and is still rolling out measurement/influence; AppearAI charges once for a useful diagnostic. The best evidence to borrow is operational: safe, reversible changes and claims grounded in source data. The biggest shared gap is proof from changes to incremental margin. No public evidence here establishes any vendor’s actual profit, customer acquisition cost, churn, or causal lift. Review counts are snapshots, not market-size measures. Validate willingness to pay and renewal with paid cohorts, exact price exposure, renewal dates, and observed use; measure causal value with randomized or well-matched unchanged catalog groups, sufficient samples, and contribution margin rather than referral attribution alone.
