# Platform and vertical deep dive: Shopify, Google, Lily AI, True Fit

**Checked:** 2026-09-29. Product claims below come from current first-party product/help pages. Vendor outcome claims are attributed to the vendor and are not independent profitability evidence.

## Shopify native commerce and agent readiness

Shopify bundles several layers into its commerce platform: eligible catalog syndication to AI channels, product mapping and grouping, an Agentic storefront search preview with listing-quality signals, Knowledge Base for store FAQs, and Sidekick for guided admin work and content generation. Eligible product data is automatically included in Shopify Catalog, whose documentation explicitly says inclusion does not guarantee an answer, ranking, or use by each connected channel. Sidekick is included with a Shopify plan; it can create descriptions and other content and presents changes for review. The search preview exposes catalog search and actionable listing factors such as descriptions, image coverage, verified reviews, variant/options and policy completeness. The Knowledge Base lets merchants manage FAQs and store information used by AI shopping agents. These are valuable inputs and operational tools, not a reliable measure of external answer visibility.

**Price and financial evidence.** The Catalog and readiness features are part of Shopify’s platform; Sidekick is included in the subscription. No separate charge for these features is stated in the cited docs. That does not mean the tools are economically unprofitable: they can support merchant retention, platform differentiation and GMV/payment-related economics. Public docs do not disclose contribution margin or attributable revenue for Catalog, Knowledge Base, readiness preview, or Sidekick individually. Treat “free to the merchant” as a competitive constraint, not evidence of negative unit economics.

**Merchant work still required.** Shopify automates distribution only for eligible products. Merchants still need to supply accurate titles, descriptions, images, categories, variants, prices, stock and policies; map custom metafields or grouping logic; review suggested fixes; and choose which agentic storefronts can access catalog data. Shopify notes that downstream channels control final wording, rank and experience. The preview can differ from actual AI results because channels re-rank results. Merchant outcome reporting therefore cannot be equated with deterministic answer coverage.

**What to borrow.** Put concrete findings next to each product, explain why a field matters, offer an editable fix, and route it for merchant approval. Use query previews as directional diagnostics and separate data completeness from relevance/popularity. **Avoid** selling a Shopify-only “AI readiness score” or promising rankings: Shopify already gives these merchants a native baseline. A plausible paid wedge is cross-platform evidence, non-Shopify workflow, source-backed validation, or measured incremental outcomes. Test whether merchants pay for those tasks after native recommendations have been applied.

Sources: [Shopify Catalog](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-catalog); [Agentic storefront management and search preview](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/agentic-storefronts/agentic-home); [Sidekick](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/ai-powered-tools/sidekick); [Sidekick pricing](https://www.shopify.com/sidekick); [Knowledge Base app](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/promoting-marketing/knowledge-base).

## Google Merchant Center and Product Studio

Google’s Merchant Center combines product feed management, diagnostics and free listings; Product Studio adds free AI image and video generation. Eligible free listings may appear on Search, Shopping, Maps, Images, Lens, YouTube and Gemini. Google states that eligibility and an enabled free-listing setting do not guarantee display. Merchant Center flags product issues and explains fixes; core work includes providing identifiers, title, link, image and price, plus other category/country-specific attributes, policy compliance, shipping and returns information. Product Studio can change backgrounds, remove backgrounds, upscale images and generate videos, with new assets saved into Merchant Center eligible to appear across Google surfaces that use feed data.

**Price and financial evidence.** Free listings and Product Studio are explicitly offered at no charge; Merchant Center itself can be created free. No independent revenue, cost-to-serve or product-level margin is published in these help pages. Free merchant tooling plausibly supports advertising ecosystem participation, product data coverage and commerce utility, but that is an inference, not a disclosed financial result. Do not describe these tools as unprofitable based on zero merchant subscription price.

**Merchant work and measurement limits.** The merchant must provide and maintain correct product facts, resolve disapprovals, obey destination policies, configure shipping/returns and review generated imagery. Google warns Product Studio can produce inaccurate or unexpected assets, works better for some products, and excludes regulated goods from image/video generation. Generated lifestyle scenes can misrepresent color, material, fit, context or accessories; merchants must quality-check each image against the real SKU. Free listing status grants permission to appear, not impressions or placement. Search Console and Merchant Center performance data do not give a prompt-level test of every AI answer, so don't claim that feed completeness proves recommendation visibility.

**What to borrow.** Make source data issues actionable, support a one-product path for small catalogs, and provide a review step before publishing any generated asset. Product Studio’s value proposition is concrete production time saved, rather than an ungrounded “AI readiness” promise. **Avoid** competing as a generic feed checker or low-cost image generator. A viable wedge needs a job Google does not complete: cross-channel reconciliation, SKU-grounded validation, human approval trails or controlled outcome measurement. Test against Merchant Center’s existing “Needs attention” workflow and measure incremental fixes plus merchant time saved.

Sources: [Free listings](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13889434?hl=en-A); [Product Studio](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13708167?hl=en-IE); [Merchant Center product editor](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/12158381?hl=en); [Google AI features and ecommerce](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).

## Lily AI / Lily Max

Lily positions Lily Max as catalog product intelligence: it identifies attribute gaps, enriches product records in shopper language, syncs outputs into existing feeds/tools and tests changes across paid media, onsite search and AI discovery. The current product page describes a controlled loop—gap scan, enrichment, test and expansion of what wins—and advertises a 30-day trial on 500 products. Lily says tests use matched spend, holdouts or difference-in-differences and confidence intervals. These are stronger measurement claims than a visibility score, but public marketing examples are vendor-reported; sample size, full protocol, category mix and reproducibility need diligence before treating claimed lifts as expected results. The site currently cites a 28% Google Shopping revenue lift and 21.4% Meta ROAS lift for named-but-anonymized examples.

**Price and financial evidence.** Lily does not publish a fixed rate card: official pricing says quotes depend on catalog size, use cases, markets/channels and ad spend. Its pages describe tailored pilots and a scoped trial offer. That confirms willingness to charge for the service but not customer-level gross margin, retention or profitability. Its differentiation is not merely generated copy; it claims ongoing catalog operations and experimentation. This likely entails integration, review and measurement costs that should be assessed in a paid pilot.

**Merchant work and limits.** Merchants must connect/authorize source catalog and destination tools, confirm generated data before activation, choose test products/surfaces, supply a valid control and enough traffic, and coordinate publication. Subjective tags (occasion, style, fit) can be useful query language but must not become invented physical facts. Lily’s claim that enriched data improves AI discovery should be understood as an input-quality claim; its own FAQ says it cannot guarantee placement, ranking or citation. Holdouts and test design matter because seasonality, promotion, feed changes and inventory can confound lift.

**What to borrow.** Sell a narrow business outcome and baseline/control, not a score in isolation. Make source-to-output diffs inspectable and only scale changes that beat a control. **Avoid** mirroring their broad omnichannel promise before you have integrations and reliable test volume; do not turn a model guess into a SKU fact. The opportunity may be a lighter source-verification and remediation product for merchants unable to justify an enterprise enrichment contract. Test willingness to pay against real operator hours and a pre-registered metric.

Sources: [Lily Max](https://www.lily.ai/); [Lily pricing](https://www.lily.ai/pricing); [How Lily is priced](https://www.lily.ai/faqs/how-is-lily-priced); [Lily’s enrichment and controlled-test explanation](https://www.lily.ai/faqs/what-does-lily-do).

## True Fit

True Fit is a fashion-specific fit and sizing system built from product/catalog data and purchase-and-return outcomes across retailers and brands. Its Shopify offer installs into apparel and footwear product pages; it advertises personalized recommendations, directional fit guidance, broad category and international coverage. The Shopify page says pricing starts at $1,000/month; optional font/color customization is listed at $5/month. It excludes some accessories, including belts, hats, gloves and pet clothing. True Fit describes a large cross-brand dataset and outcome-trained recommendations, and its current technical material documents catalog, shopper and historical purchase/return inputs, with widget, Shopify app, API and MCP pathways. Published lift and reduction figures are vendor claims, and should be tested against the retailer’s own control and eligible shopper adoption.

**Financial evidence.** A published starting price is stronger evidence of monetization than a free platform utility, but it does not establish profitability. Enterprise pricing, implementation/support costs, data rights, retention, inference costs and gross margin are not disclosed in public materials. Do not infer that a high starting price means a profitable business. Nor does an advertised reduction in bracketing automatically equal net profit: returns, kept-item contribution, adoption and implementation costs all matter.

**Merchant work, consent and limits.** Merchants need to connect their store/catalog, ensure product and size information maps correctly, and decide how the fit widget is presented and shopper profile inputs collected. True Fit has both population-level claims and features that collect shopper size/profile information; the exact data flow, retention, consent and controller/processor responsibilities should be checked in contract and privacy documentation before implementation. Do not infer exact photo requirements or dimensions from public pages. Product facts such as fabric composition, garment measurements or construction must remain grounded in merchant/manufacturer sources. Fit recommendations are an outcome model, not a substitute for verified SKU attributes.

**What to borrow.** Build around a costly, specific decision and measure actual kept orders and fit returns. Use category-specific scope, clear unsupported-category boundaries, and a visible explanation of what evidence supports a recommendation. **Avoid** claiming fit prediction from product photos or synthetic shoppers, and avoid treating catalog QA as equivalent to True Fit’s proprietary behavior/outcome graph. A small entrant can first validate source-backed fashion data quality without collecting body profiles. Test only with merchant-approved ground truth and separate measurable operational wins from shopper-outcome claims.

Sources: [True Fit for Shopify](https://www.truefit.com/shopify); [True Fit technical specification](https://www.truefit.com/fit-intelligence-spec); [Data and merchant platform details](https://www.truefit.com/resources/calling-all-merchants); [Privacy information](https://www.truefit.com/privacy-policy-and-choices-policy-and-choices).
