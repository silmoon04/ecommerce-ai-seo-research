# Shopify AI app teardown: four budget competitors

Snapshot: 2026-09-29. Prices are USD as shown on vendor or Shopify App Store pages. This is a public-source teardown; I did not install or hands-on test these products. “Limitation” means a published constraint or a gap in what the reviewed pages document, not proof a capability is absent. Public profitability is unknown for all four; the unit-economic points below are conditional pressures, not claims about actual results.

## IndexGPT

IndexGPT combines an AI SEO score/action plan with indexing and content tools. Its public Shopify offer is Free, Essentials at $16/month ($128/year), and Premium at $45/month ($432/year), with a three-day trial on paid tiers. Essentials adds IndexNow-style “instant indexing,” an `llms.txt` builder and robots.txt analysis; Premium adds a prompt tracker, sentiment analysis, schema builder, blog/content generation and priority support. The vendor describes connecting stores to IndexNow, maintaining `llms.txt`, testing robots rules, and using its dashboard to track indexing performance; the App Store calls out AI SEO score, action plan and AI growth tracking. ([vendor product and pricing](https://indexgpt.app/), [Shopify listing](https://apps.shopify.com/index-gpt))

Its advantage is a comparatively mature Shopify presence: the listing carried Built for Shopify and 144 reviews, 4.9/5 at the snapshot. A merchant review cited readable data and a useful view of which content AI systems used as sources; Shopify’s generated review summary says merchants find it intuitive and report visibility, traffic and sales gains. These are merchant reports, not controlled causal evidence. ([listing and reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/index-gpt?surface_detail=store-management-operations-analytics&surface_inter_position=1&surface_intra_position=3&surface_type=category&surface_version=redesign))

The offer spans several jobs (technical indexing, analytics, prompts and generated content), while the public plan descriptions do not specify prompt counts, engine coverage, run frequency, or the exact definition of “AI growth.” Treat those as undisclosed on the reviewed pages, not proof there are no limits. Shopify now makes eligible products discoverable through Catalog automatically and serves baseline discovery files, which weakens the standalone value of selling indexing or `llms.txt` as the outcome. ([Shopify Catalog documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/agentic-storefronts/products)) No company-level financial disclosure was found in reviewed sources.

**Conditional economic pressure:** low subscription prices, a broad free tier, and potentially recurring model/API and support costs could compress contribution if usage is generous; public evidence does not disclose usage costs, conversion, retention or margin. **Improve:** define tracked surfaces, prompt/sample cadence, and attribution boundaries; connect each diagnosed issue to a reversible fix and show a before/after evidence trail. **Borrow:** clear low-friction free entry and an upgrade path from audit to monitoring/content. **Avoid copying:** “instant indexing means AI recommendations” as a promise; crawl/index notification is not ranking or sales proof. **Opportunity:** prove which product facts and edits change inclusion in real, disclosed shopping queries and produce incremental orders. **Falsifier:** merchants do not renew after fixing baseline issues, or a controlled holdout shows no useful downstream lift.

## SKAW

SKAW’s Shopify listing positions it as an AI-agent catalog audit plus first-party bot-visit visibility. It says the score covers product data and structure, identifies missing GTINs/attributes/metafields, tracks agent visits and browsed products, suggests metafield definitions, and exports a Sidekick brief. Free includes the full score, real-time activity tracking via a web pixel, top 12 product issues and the brief. The paid plan is $19.99/month or $199.99/year and lists unlimited-product catalog audits, an Agent Query Simulator for product pages, collection audits and suggested metafield definitions. ([Shopify listing, pricing and merchant reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/skaw))

That workflow has a useful operational angle: observed bot requests reveal which URLs agents actually fetch, and Sidekick briefs lower the effort to act on findings. One merchant review says the free allocation exposed missing product categories and thin specifications, and reports using Sidekick for fixes; another values seeing bot visits. These are user anecdotes, not independently measured effects. The vendor’s agency article reports revenue and AI crawl activity rising on two client sites after fixes, while acknowledging multiple simultaneous changes and the need for careful scheduling; it does not establish causation. ([StewArt Media case commentary](https://stewartmedia.com.au/the-agentic-first-web-is-already-here/))

There is a concrete pricing/trial inconsistency on SKAW’s own App Store page: plan details mention a 14-day free trial, while its pricing footer says 7-day free trial. Verify at checkout before treating either duration as definitive. The free plan limits surfaced issues to 12, according to the listing and a merchant’s report that the allocation was quickly used; listing pages do not state event retention, pixel coverage limits, bot verification method, or simulator query volume. Those details are undisclosed in the reviewed material. Only two reviews appeared. ([listing](https://apps.shopify.com/skaw))

**Conditional economic pressure:** even a sub-$20 monthly plan must cover pixel/event ingestion, bot classification, audit compute and support; event volume and retention policies could drive variable cost, but no actual cost or profitability data is public. **Improve:** label verified crawlers versus spoofable user agents, show event retention/coverage, and convert recommendations into measured, versioned fixes. **Borrow:** first-party crawl telemetry linked directly to affected product records and the Sidekick handoff. **Avoid copying:** equating bot visits or score increases with shopper demand or revenue. **Opportunity:** merchant-grade answerability monitoring tied to trusted product attributes and query-level outcomes. **Falsifier:** bot logs rarely alter what merchants prioritize, or users will not pay once the one-time catalog issues are resolved.

## WPRobo AI Search Visibility

WPRobo sells a staged workflow: scan the catalog, blog and storefront into a 0–100 AI visibility score; show prioritized issues; offer one-click alt-text and metadata fixes plus AI drafts that merchants approve; publish `llms.txt`, `agents.md` and schema; then track Perplexity citations weekly. Its privacy notice says drafts and analysis use Anthropic, OpenAI and/or Google APIs, and Perplexity is used for citation checks. It states drafts are reviewed before publication and data is deleted after uninstall, typically within 48 hours. ([Shopify listing and prices](https://apps.shopify.com/ai-search-visibility), [vendor privacy policy](https://aisearchvisibility.wprobo.com/privacy-policy))

Pricing is Free up to 25 products; Starter $9.99/month up to 500 with one-click fixes, drafts, blog AEO and live verification; Growth $29.99/month up to 2,000 adds five tracked keywords, FAQ generation, daily scans/alerts and weekly reports; Pro $79.99/month up to 5,000 adds 15 keywords, Hallucination Guard, competitor benchmarking and image generation. The listing had one review at snapshot. That reviewer reports schema fixes preceded appearances in category answers within three weeks and says setup took an afternoon; this is a merchant allegation of improvement with no control group or vendor evidence beyond a thank-you reply. ([listing and review](https://apps.shopify.com/ai-search-visibility))

The public workflow is unusually explicit about approval and checks wrong AI claims against catalog facts. But citation tracking is described as Perplexity-only and weekly on the vendor page; “AI visibility” breadth can therefore exceed the documented recurring measurement. Keyword limits are public, while answer sampling, geographic coverage, citation attribution, scan/API quotas and score formula are not explained in the reviewed pages. This is absence of disclosure, not evidence those controls do not exist. The app launched June 2026 and has little public review evidence. ([vendor product page](https://aisearchvisibility.wprobo.com/))

**Conditional economic pressure:** multiple model providers, image generation, recurring scans and email support may create cost pressure, especially if plan pricing is flat; the public record gives no usage-cost, paid conversion, churn or profit data. **Improve:** publish exact scan/citation cadence by plan, show source URLs and repeatability, and quantify which approved changes correlate with outcomes. **Borrow:** approval before publishing, catalog-grounded hallucination checks, and weekly next-action reporting. **Avoid copying:** a proprietary score with no visible method or treating one citation as broad AI discoverability. **Opportunity:** trust-focused validation that distinguishes correct product facts, crawler access, citations, visits and attributable orders. **Falsifier:** merchants do not value the QA/reporting loop enough to retain a subscription after initial cleanup.

## Shop Rank AI (ShopRank AI)

Shop Rank AI, by Zipify Lab, audits product pages against 17+ AI-readiness factors, then offers product title/description fixes, image resizing/alt-text generation, and JSON-LD injection. Its Shopify plan is free to install, syncs up to 50 products and includes readiness reporting, three tracked searches/one competitor, one bulk AI fix and AI-attributed revenue dashboard. Scale is $9.99/month, billed as $9.99 per 100 products, with up to 3,000 synced products, unlimited tracking and bulk fixes, image-quality tools and priority live support. ([Shopify listing and reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/shoprank-ai))

Zipify’s help center clarifies a consequential data flow: product titles, descriptions and types go to OpenAI API when merchants use “Fix with AI”; public product URLs go to Google PageSpeed Insights. Zipify says this data is not used for training under those services’ API terms. ([Zipify Help Center](https://help.zipify.com/en/articles/14754086-is-my-product-data-sent-to-third-parties)) The listing shows 4.8/5 across 29 reviews. Recent brief reviews mostly say it is easy to use; those establish usability feedback, not sales lift. The App Store claims an AI-attributed revenue dashboard but does not publicly explain its attribution window, source detection or incrementality method. The product page advertises fixes and a score; the reviewed sources do not spell out how the 17+ factors are weighted or how rank changes are sampled. ([listing](https://apps.shopify.com/shoprank-ai))

**Conditional economic pressure:** per-100-product pricing can make catalog size a clear price ladder, but inexpensive plans plus image/model calls, PageSpeed analysis and support could pressure contribution at high usage; actual quotas and costs are not public. **Improve:** explain AI revenue attribution, distinguish observed referral orders from incremental impact, and keep an audit log with rollback for bulk catalog changes. **Borrow:** product-level issue lists, one-click fixes and understandable product-count packaging. **Avoid copying:** “AI-attributed revenue” as causal proof or image upscaling as an AI-search outcome. **Opportunity:** independent, repeatable tests across engines with exact queries, timestamps, cited product URLs and randomized/holdout measurement. **Falsifier:** merchants see no decision value beyond conventional SEO cleanup and do not renew after fixing the initial backlog. No public company financial evidence establishes profitability or loss.
