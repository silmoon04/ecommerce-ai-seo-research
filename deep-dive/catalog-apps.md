# Shopify AI catalog apps: competitor deep dive

**Snapshot: 2026-09-29.** I checked current Shopify App Store listings and vendor pages. No financial statements or unit economics for Kedra, Catalyst, or AIVisible were located in the reviewed primary sources; whether any is profitable is **unknown**. Their public reviews are early adoption evidence, not proof of paid retention or effectiveness.

## Direct overlap changes the opportunity

The proposed “catalog repair plus verified AI visibility” idea overlaps both these three apps and established controls. Kedra advertises approved fixes; Catalyst advertises enrichment and feed tooling; AIVisible advertises audits, bot checks and attribution. Beyond them, Burnish advertises a source-attached fact spine, approval-gated fixes, rollback and an exportable audit trail, while Geoffy verifies its bytes in server HTML before counting publication and leaves price/availability to the commerce platform. Provenance, approval, rollback and propagation checks are therefore controls or competitive features, not a claim of whitespace. The only candidate gap this review leaves open is store-specific diagnosis of contradictory facts across canonical Shopify fields, variants, markets and destination feeds, with identification of the authoritative field and confirmation of each affected destination. Public pages checked do not document that precise conflict-resolution workflow for these apps; that is an evidence limit, not proof it is absent. Compare willingness to pay against existing $10–$64 tiers, free offers and any relevant specialist pricing before treating a new paid workflow as viable.

## Kedra AI Index

**Advertised feature.** The App Store offers Free, Grow $25/month, Pro $79/month and Scale $199/month, with usage tiers for scans/questions, markets, fixes and competitors. Kedra says recommendations are drafted and “applied when you approve”; it also advertises agents.md/llms.txt, structured data, an AI product feed, prompt rankings and assistant-referred visits/orders. The listing describes crawler checks and UCP checkout checking. Current reviews report a practical product/variant/schema audit, fixes associated with a score moving from low 70s to 91, and a live crawler check; these are merchant claims, not independent lift evidence. One reviewer called it a monthly routine after about two months; another reported 100/100 after four hours, highlighting that a readiness score can saturate quickly. Kedra replied publicly to a review on July 21, 2026. ([App Store listing and reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/kedra-ai-index), [Kedra product page](https://kedra.io/apps/kedra-ai-index/))

**Explicit constraint / unknown.** Public plan caps constrain monitoring coverage; exact criteria, sampling reproducibility, write diff, rollback, source provenance and propagation guarantees were not documented on the checked public pages. App permission disclosures include editing products and themes plus recent order/analytics access. No public paid-retention or revenue disclosure found. Its App Store aggregate is a snapshot and the cited reviews range from hours to roughly two months; even the repeat-use reviewer does not establish renewal cohorts or willingness to pay. Public descriptions say recommendations open the relevant Shopify screen, while the listing also advertises fixes applied after merchant approval. The boundary between navigational instructions and app-authored catalog writes is not fully explained on the product page. This matters because merchant labor saved and the risk of accidental factual edits depend on whether the app merely drafts, stages or commits changes. Those mechanics need hands-on confirmation before treating approval as a complete safety loop.

**Economic pressure (inference).** Broad feature scope and frequent model/query checks can create compute/support costs, while a free tier and $25 entry price constrain revenue per small shop. Conversely, higher paid limits offer a plausible usage ladder. None of this establishes margins or loss-making.

**Improve / borrow / avoid.** Borrow approval-before-write, effort-ranked recommendations and a real crawler fetch check. Improve with variant/market conflict diagnosis and identify which competing source should be authoritative. Source citations, diffs, rollback and propagation checks are table stakes already advertised elsewhere. Don’t copy broad readiness scores or imply that bots crawling or referral attribution proves incremental sales. **Opportunity:** determine which store-specific product fact is authoritative when Shopify, a variant or market and a destination feed disagree. **Falsifier:** merchants can already resolve cross-field and cross-feed conflicts with Kedra or existing feed tools, and will not pay beyond those plans for a target-specific audit.

## Catalyst: AI SEO & ChatGPT

**Advertised feature.** Catalyst’s current offer is Free diagnostics, Starter $19/month (50 enrichments, auto-enrich new products, structured data/FAQs, Google Merchant Center feed), and Growth $49/month (500 enrichments, AI Visibility Tracker, MCP endpoint, monthly re-enrichment and priority support). Extra enrichments are listed at $0.05 or $0.03 each by tier. It says it audits 17 SEO/AI-readiness criteria, generates descriptions/FAQs/JSON-LD, maintains llms.txt and tracks AI answers/citations. Product screenshots advertise per-product scores, enrichment status/history and usage. The single App Store review, dated July 15, 2026, calls it simple and useful for agent traffic after six minutes of use; this is a very short, unverified reviewer claim, not evidence of lift or retention. ([App Store listing, pricing and review](https://apps.shopify.com/catalyst-public), [Catalyst FAQ](https://retail-solutions-ai.com/))

**Explicit constraint / unknown.** Enrichment counts and overage pricing create a published volume limit; English and Spanish are the listed languages. The listing requests product edit access. Public pages do not specify human approval requirements, rollback semantics, field-level source provenance, exact crawler propagation validation or how tracker samples are selected. “History” in screenshots does not by itself prove rollback. No financial information or reliable renewal evidence found. Catalyst describes auto-enriching new products and monthly re-enrichment, suggesting a recurring maintenance use case, but that is a vendor feature claim rather than evidence of merchant renewal. Its visible history can help diagnose what the app changed, but the public listing does not say whether history stores the original value, model rationale, source citation, approver identity or a restore action. The one public review is positive but only records six minutes of use, so public evidence on paid behavior is especially thin. Because the Google Merchant Center feed sits beside GEO tracking, a merchant may primarily value conventional feed hygiene; Catalyst would need to distinguish which outcomes come from feed compliance versus AI answer behavior.

**Economic pressure (inference).** At $19/$49, catalog-scale enrichment and external visibility sampling could be costly; overage charges may cover marginal work but could make larger catalogs expensive. Free diagnosis may improve acquisition while delaying revenue. Actual cost and conversion remain unknown.

**Improve / borrow / avoid.** Borrow the direct tie between diagnosis, enrichment and channel feed, and the visible enrichment history. Improve by distinguishing conflicting values across canonical records and affected feeds. Approval, source evidence, reversible patches and post-sync checks are already controls advertised by competitors. Don’t copy unsourced generated FAQs or treat a 17-point score as proof AI recommendation changed. **Opportunity:** explain which store-specific field conflict caused downstream disagreement and identify the system that owns the correct value. **Falsifier:** Catalyst or a feed tool already traces the same store-specific conflicts to canonical fields and verifies corrected destination state, with no incremental paid demand.

## AIVisible: GEO/AEO for ChatGPT

**Advertised feature.** Current App Store pricing shows Basic $10/month (20 recommendation checks, 1 product audit/month), Pro $29 (40 checks, 20 audits) and Advanced $64 (150 checks, 50 audits), with $0.25 per extra check/audit. Plans advertise AI traffic/order/revenue analytics, bot access monitoring, llms.txt and email reporting. It also claims query-level answer tracking and daily robots.txt checks with a ready fix. Reviews include a June 8, 2026 claim of increasing AI-attributed sales after five months, plus setup-focused reviews dated March 31 and April 16. The sales claim is self-reported attribution, not causal evidence; the latter reviews praise quick setup and automatic llms.txt generation. ([App Store listing, pricing and reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/aivisible-ai-geo-aeo), [DigitalSuits product site](https://digitalsuits.co/))

**Explicit constraint / unknown.** Check and audit quotas are explicit, with metered overages; English is the listed language. Public documentation does not clarify change approval or writes, rollback, canonical field provenance, feed destination correction, or whether crawler checks fetch the post-fix representation. App permissions include product/order and analytics access. No public financials, cohort retention or independent sales-lift evidence found. The longer-use review states the merchant planned to stay a user, but the review does not disclose its plan, spend, baseline, sales totals or counterfactual. It supports that at least one reviewer perceived continued utility, not paid retention. Product suggestions mention reviews, content, schema and indexing, but public materials do not show whether recommendations are informational or can be applied directly. The distinction affects implementation burden and the permission surface; the App Store permissions list products, orders, store analytics and online-store data. Its attribution product could reduce uncertainty about channel contribution, but without a holdout or comparable baseline the reported trend can reflect seasonality, wider AI adoption, or unrelated SEO changes.

**Economic pressure (inference).** Low entry price can aid trial, but 20 checks and one audit may not diagnose even a modest store; larger usage triggers variable billing that may deter expanded use. Broad analytics and tracking at $10–$64 suggest exposure to model/query cost and support needs. Profitability cannot be inferred.

**Improve / borrow / avoid.** Borrow query-level observations, nightly crawl-access monitoring, by-assistant attribution and explicit metering. Improve by exposing sampled prompt, market, timestamp, observed URL and repeat variance. Source evidence, approval, rollback and destination checks are controls already advertised elsewhere. Don’t call attributed revenue incremental revenue. **Opportunity:** identify and resolve a specific merchant’s conflicting canonical, variant, market and feed values, then verify which destination accepted the authoritative value. **Falsifier:** AIVisible or an existing feed product already resolves the same conflicts across the merchant’s markets and destinations, and merchants will not pay for a separate workflow.

## Sources and evidence limits

Shopify App Store listings and vendor product/documentation pages were reviewed as primary sources on September 29, 2026; see each linked listing and [Burnish workflow](https://useburnish.com/what-it-does/) and [Geoffy FAQ](https://geoffy.ai/docs/faq). Reviews are merchant claims, not verified outcomes. No product was installed or hands-on tested. Absence of public workflow detail is recorded as unknown, not proof that a feature is absent.
