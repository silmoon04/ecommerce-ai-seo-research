# Conversation audit: AI commerce / merchant agent testing

**Scope and date.** This audits the material factual, numerical, market, product, and viability claims in shared-conversation-1.txt and shared-conversation-2.txt. Checked 28 September 2026. The referenced ai_commerce_competitor_dossier.html is absent from this directory, so the purported 39-vendor dossier cannot be audited from the saved chats alone. “Supported” means supported by a cited source within its actual scope, not independently verified product efficacy. “Unsupported” means evidence was not supplied or found. “Speculative” means a hypothesis. “Needs correction” means overbroad, conflated, or contradicted by current documentation. “Outdated” means contradicted by a newer source.

## Decision-useful readout

The second chat materially corrects the earlier optimism. The defensible version is: **a merchant-facing tool measuring defined AI shopping behaviors is a plausible experiment; synthetic shopper scores and a growing database do not establish that the tool improves production recommendations, incremental profit, or retention.**

Platforms are moving: OpenAI says Shopify product data is integrated through Shopify Catalog. Shopify says eligible products need no additional merchant action to be discovered in ChatGPT, subject to eligibility; checkout currently redirects to the merchant's own store. Shopify's growth figures support a fast-growing channel on Shopify's own data, not demand for this app or ROI from optimization.

The category is contested. Current Shopify App Store pages advertise catalog scores, agent query simulation, product enrichment, competitor visibility, repeated measurement and recommendations. Peec's own product update advertises SKU-level shopping visibility. That verifies feature overlap and price anchors, not product efficacy, paid adoption, profitability, or market saturation.

Most critical methodology correction: changing catalog text and rerunning the same synthetic evaluator is a **simulation replay**, not an A/B test and not proof of lift in a production shopping channel. It shows at most that the selected evaluator behaved differently under altered inputs. “Found,” “shortlisted,” “selected,” click, order, revenue attribution, and incremental profit need separate definitions.

The earlier data moat claim is especially incomplete. Public catalog facts and synthetic scenarios are copyable, and Shopify's API terms prohibit using merchant/customer information for competitive benchmarking. The terms also restrict AI/ML training or improvement using API-derived, merchant, or store data without Shopify's written consent or consent applicable to that merchant/store. Merchant consent for its own service should not be assumed to authorize cross-store benchmarking. See [data-moat.md](data-moat.md) and [Shopify API terms](https://www.shopify.com/legal/api-terms); resolve with Shopify before promising a pooled Shopify-data moat.

## Claim-safe product framing

Describe an initial product as **a merchant-controlled test harness for a defined set of shopping intents on a named agent surface**. Report only what the test observed: page/feed access, attributes extracted, product returned, model's stated rationale, supporting evidence, and changes across controlled replay. Label synthetic runs as simulations. Avoid “rank higher,” universal scores, “600% lift,” “we drove revenue,” and “data moat” without production-channel and outcome evidence.

The decisive evidence is a paid pilot where merchants know the native and commercial alternatives; the product finds a consequential issue those tools miss; customers implement the fix; external outcomes improve under a credible design; ongoing value exceeds compute and human support; and customers renew after the initial catalog cleanup. Neither shared chat establishes any of these today.
