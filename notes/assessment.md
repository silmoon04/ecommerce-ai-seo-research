Ecommerce AI SEO: commercial assessment

Research completed 28 September 2026. This is a decision report, with linked specialist research and an editable financial model. It assesses both supplied conversations, including their changes of position.

**My decision: run a small, time-limited free-to-paid pilot. Do not yet build a broad AI-ranking subscription or fund a large free database strategy.** There is evidence of an emerging market and actual spending on AI visibility. There is no public evidence that establishes this proposed product's conversion, acquisition cost, retention, incremental sales, or profit. A useful small business does not require an uncopyable algorithm, but it does require customers who keep paying more than it costs to acquire and serve them.

The first hypothesis I would test is a narrow catalogue checking and repair workflow: find consequential product/variant inconsistencies, cite the original evidence, help a merchant approve corrections, and verify that the corrections reached the intended pages or feeds. AI shopping tests can help diagnose these problems. The recurring value must come from ongoing catalogue work or measured operating savings. This choice prioritises an outcome we can inspect; it is not evidence of unmet demand, and competitors already cover parts of it.

| Product version | Decision today | What would change the decision |
|---|---|---|
| Free readiness score followed by generic AI SEO subscription | Weak standalone opportunity | Demonstrated acquisition advantage and renewals despite native/free alternatives |
| Repeated AI mentions, citations and competitor monitoring | Paid offers and adjacent company revenue exist; small-merchant adoption/profit remains unverified | A buyer segment with an unmet reporting job and affordable acquisition |
| Synthetic shopper testing lab | Useful testing instrument; commercial demand unproven | Merchants pay for defects it finds that other tools miss, and return to use it |
| Catalogue consistency checks, approved repairs and recurring alerts | First hypothesis to test; observable outcome, unproven demand | Measured avoided work/errors, repeat usage, actual payment and renewal |
| Fashion fit prediction or extracting exact missing measurements from photos | Poor initial scope | Reliable supplied ground truth and validated fit/returns outcomes |
| Cross-store database trained on free users | Do not assume an available moat | Valid rights, representative data, external outcome validity and measurable model improvement |

**There is a market, but market growth is different from your product creating value.**

Peec's own May 28, 2026 announcement reports more than $10 million ARR and more than 2,500 customers. This is a company-reported revenue run rate, not audited profit or evidence about small Shopify apps. It does show that AI visibility is more than an imaginary spending category. The release also reports more than 70 staff and $29 million raised: its business is not a template for the costs of a solo founder. [Peec announcement](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/28/3303009/0/en/Peec-AI-hits-10M-ARR-16-months-after-launch-as-brands-race-to-optimize-for-AI-search.html)

Similarweb's 2026 ecommerce report says generative-AI referrals increased 203% year over year while accounting for only 0.4% of retail ecommerce visits. Its public landing page does not disclose enough methodology to apply that percentage to a particular shop. Adobe's June update says May 2026 AI-referred US retail visits converted 54% better and generated 53% more revenue per visit than non-AI visits. Those are observed channel comparisons, not uplift caused by an optimisation app. They can coexist with a small share of total traffic. [Similarweb report](https://www.similarweb.com/corp/reports/state-of-ecommerce-2026/), [Adobe update](https://business.adobe.com/uk/blog/adobe-report-ai-traffic-travel-sites-surges-200-percent)

The earlier chat's 41% higher revenue-per-visit figure does have the cited Reuters support. The article separately reports June consumer adoption; the precise period for its revenue-per-visit comparison is not sufficiently clear to certify it as June. We found the exact Reuters article, but not a public Adobe methodology release reproducing that comparison. Keep it attributed to Reuters' reporting of Adobe data rather than merging it with Adobe's May or Prime Day statistics. [Reuters, August 7, 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/retailers-tap-ai-shopping-traffic-fight-keep-customer-data-2026-08-07/)

An AI recommendation may lead to a later branded search or direct visit rather than a referral click. Similarweb studied such downstream association in finance, travel and beauty, but that does not quantify the effect for our prospective merchant segment. The 0.4% referral figure is therefore not a hard ceiling on influence, and invisible influence must not be filled in with an invented multiplier. The [market-demand research](market-demand.md) records sample, geography, metric and attribution limits rather than converting these studies into a fabricated total addressable market. [Similarweb downstream study](https://aisearch.similarweb.com/blog/ai-visibility-downstream-impact/)

There is no defensible public count of merchants who simultaneously need this exact product, can implement its recommendations, will pay your proposed price, and will renew. The first addressable market should therefore be a named prospect cohort with a clear operating problem, not a percentage of global ecommerce GMV.

Funding is not revenue; revenue is not profit; reviews are not paid subscribers. The merchant evidence is more informative than ratings alone. Conversely, Burnish's cited reviewers were only seven or eight days into use, and Catalyst's cited review followed six minutes. Those early reviews support onboarding interest, not lasting ROI. The most useful positive signal is that some merchants buy help implementing and maintaining work. The risk is that this creates a service business with human delivery costs. Calling that work automated does not remove those costs.

**Shopify is already supplying much of the basic functionality.**

Eligible products are automatically distributed through Shopify Catalog into relevant AI channels. Catalogue inclusion does not guarantee a recommendation. Shopify also offers its free Knowledge Base app, a public readiness scanner, native agentic search previews and guidance, and default agent-oriented files. Its Agentic plan extends distribution to merchants using other ecommerce platforms. Supporting WooCommerce is therefore not, by itself, a defence against platform competition. Geography and channel eligibility still matter; a UK merchant must not be sold a ranking fix for a channel-eligibility restriction. [Shopify catalogue docs](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/agentic-storefronts/products), [Knowledge Base](https://apps.shopify.com/shopify-knowledge-base), [platform-risk research](platform-risk.md)

Google also explicitly rejects the idea that special AI text files or proprietary AI-ranking hacks are required for its search features. Its current Search Console GenAI report provides impressions grouped by page, country, device and date. Native reporting reduces the value of a simple dashboard, while still leaving workflow and interpretation work. [Google optimisation guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), [Search Console report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139)

A viable paid promise should therefore describe a specific failure that persists despite these tools, such as a variant URL showing the wrong selected size/image or a material conflict between source data and a published offer. This remains a hypothesis until real merchants show repeated examples and pay to resolve them. The additional competitor check found that DataFeedWatch, Feedonomics, Channable and Simprosys already offer feed rules, diagnostics, optimisation or managed operations. Broad catalogue QA is another crowded market. The unresolved question is whether your product can trace a consequential AI-shopping failure to a particular source field and verify a correction more effectively than the merchant's existing tools. That must be demonstrated, not assumed. [Adjacent competition in the dossier](competitors.md)

**The free-first plan is sensible for learning; it is incomplete as a growth strategy.**

Give a merchant a bounded sample that shows a genuine problem and a useful correction. Let them experience the proposed paid workflow for a short period. Set the future price from the beginning, then ask for payment before extending the service. Free usage can reveal onboarding friction, false positives, which fixes get applied, and whether merchants return. It cannot establish willingness to pay or renewal.

An appropriate starting offer is one collection or a small sample of products, one scan, a limited number of shopper tests, and a short evidence report. Scheduled monitoring, larger catalogue coverage, approval/rollback history and hands-on implementation can be paid. Avoid unlimited free multi-engine browser runs. Report limits plainly and keep the free result useful rather than withholding the entire diagnosis behind a score.

The following acquisition sensitivity is arithmetic, not a conversion forecast. Suppose 100 free audits cost £0.50 each in compute. Ten minutes of assistance per audit, if needed, is valued at an assumed £25/hour. These figures are researcher-selected inputs, not measured costs. Marketing and sales time are excluded.

| Free-audit-to-paid rate assumed | Payers from 100 audits | Compute cost per payer | Compute plus ten minutes' help per audit, per payer |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1% | 1 | £50.00 | £466.67 |
| 3% | 3 | £16.67 | £155.56 |
| 5% | 5 | £10.00 | £93.33 |
| 10% | 10 | £5.00 | £46.67 |

At 5% conversion, acquiring 100 payers needs 2,000 completed audits before allowing for churn. The cost of getting those 2,000 merchants to try it is missing from this table. Free is a price, not a distribution channel. If these costs are included in your measured CAC, do not subtract them a second time in the profit model.

ChartMogul's 2026 conversion study covers 200 B2B software products and defines conversion over six months. Its broad benchmarks are useful for deciding what to measure, not for predicting this product's conversion. The earlier chat's low-price AI retention figures are supported by ChartMogul's separate report, but they are revenue-retention measures across other AI businesses, not Shopify-app customer churn. [Conversion report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/), [AI retention report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/), [distribution and pilot research](freemium-distribution.md)

Initial distribution should test a short list of catalogue-heavy merchants and agencies with access to them. Compare direct merchants with agencies separately. An agency can supply several accounts but may demand discounted seats, client reports and substantial support. No outreach or recruitment was conducted during this research.

**The proposed database advantage has both an evidence problem and a rights problem.**

Public product pages and synthetic answers can be collected again by competitors. Historical experiments are more useful, but a million runs of your own shopper bot may teach you to optimise for your own evaluator. The scarce information is the relationship between a verified product fact, an implemented change, a defined external test and a real outcome, with suitable controls.

Shopify's API terms restrict merchant-data use to serving the relevant merchant and separately prohibit competitive benchmarking with merchant/customer information. They also constrain AI/ML improvement using Shopify-derived information, including anonymous or derived data. Merchant-specific consent is not a safe assumption of cross-store reuse rights. The practical consequence is to obtain the necessary platform and merchant permissions before building a pooled benchmark. Independent public-web observations have a different provenance to API-derived merchant data and require separate analysis. [Shopify API terms, sections 2 and 6](https://www.shopify.com/legal/api-terms)

The product should work commercially even if all connected-store information remains isolated per merchant. Start by making each merchant's own history valuable. Treat a permissioned cross-store research panel as a later option, not the reason for subsidising unlimited free usage. The [data-moat memo](data-moat.md) covers sampling bias, deletion, retention and provenance in more detail.

**Good profit is possible in the arithmetic, but neither demand nor these operating inputs has been measured.**

Public supplier rates anchor some costs. Tavily PAYG is $0.008 per credit, with basic search costing one credit and advanced search two. Apify's ChatGPT Search actor advertises $3 per 1,000 searches on Business but $5 per 1,000 on Starter, whose $19 plan includes usage credit. Forty-five thousand searches on that one surface would be about $225 at Starter rates before other work, not a quote for five different engines. That is a search-result collector, not the cost of an autonomous shopping trajectory or a guarantee of reliable consumer-interface parity. Supabase Pro and Vercel Pro provide a roughly $45/month starting infrastructure floor before extra usage. Cheap model tokens make modest analysis plausible; they do not tell us the cost of support, retries, catalogue crawling or acquisition. [Tavily pricing](https://docs.tavily.com/documentation/api-credits), [Apify actor pricing](https://apify.com/apify/chatgpt-search-scraper/pricing), [Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing), [Vercel](https://vercel.com/pricing), [cost memo](unit-costs.md)

For a Shopify-billed app, the example below uses its published 2.9% processing fee and assumes eligibility for the 0% revenue-share tier. Shopify's qualifying $1 million threshold and developer exceptions need checking; it is not simply an annual allowance. A separately billed service follows a different fee path. Stripe should not be charged in the model on top of Shopify billing for the same sale. [Shopify revenue share](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share)

These are the explicit baseline planning assumptions:

- £99/month subscription, excluding any VAT collected for the tax authority.
- £10 per payer/month budget for delivery infrastructure, API use and retries. This is an allowance, not a measured bill.
- Thirty minutes of paid support per payer/month at £25/hour, separate from the founder's product/management pay.
- 5% monthly customer churn and £150 acquisition cost for each replacement payer. Both are unmeasured.
- £100 fixed overhead plus £50 of separate monthly validation/research work, excluded from the acquisition cohort counted in CAC.
- £3,000/month founder compensation before tax. Initial acquisition, initial onboarding, growth acquisition, refunds and taxes are excluded from steady-state surplus.

The per-payer arithmetic is £99 minus £2.871 processing, £10 delivery, £12.50 support and £7.50 expected replacement acquisition = **£66.129/month**. This leaves £3,150/month of fixed spending and founder compensation to cover. Gross delivery contribution before acquisition is £73.629, or 74.4% of revenue under this definition.

| Active £99 payers | MRR | Expected replacements/month at 5% churn | Support hours/month | Surplus after the stated costs and £3,000 founder pay |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 30 | £2,970 | 1.5 | 15 | -£1,166 |
| 48 | £4,752 | 2.4 | 24 | £24 |
| 100 | £9,900 | 5 | 50 | £3,463 |
| 300 | £29,700 | 15 | 150 | £16,689 |

These figures are steady-state sensitivities, not a claim that 100 customers can be obtained or retained. At the assumed £150 CAC, acquiring the first 100 costs £15,000 before onboarding. A 300-account operation would need 150 support hours every month before product development, sales and management; the unchanged fixed-overhead assumption becomes increasingly optimistic.

Holding all other inputs constant, the £49 plan requires 180 payers to cover the same fixed costs and founder pay; the £199 plan requires 20. Higher prices normally change the buyer, service burden and acquisition process, so this is price sensitivity rather than three validated business plans.

Support can overturn apparently attractive margins. With 100 £99 payers, increasing support from 30 minutes to two hours per customer lowers the same modeled surplus from £3,463 to about **-£287/month**. This is why an apparently cheap AI product can fail despite low API costs. If you do support yourself, do not book both an outsourced cash wage and the same founder hours; track your actual hours and capacity separately.

The companion [financial model](financial-model.md) and [spreadsheet CSV](model.csv) make the assumptions editable. None of the customer, churn, CAC or support inputs is disguised as a sourced market average.

**The merchant's return matters as much as your margin.**

At £25 contribution per genuinely incremental order, a £99 fee requires four additional orders per month before implementation cost. Alternatively, at an actual loaded staff cost of £25/hour it needs almost four verified hours saved. These unit values are examples; use the merchant's records. Revenue attributed to ChatGPT is not the same as revenue caused by your product.

For scale, take the sourced 0.4% traffic share only as an illustrative input, then assume 4% conversion, 20% causal order lift and £25 contribution per order. None of those latter three assumptions is supported as this product's performance. A 50,000-session store would have 200 AI-referred visits, eight baseline orders and 1.6 extra expected orders, worth £40/month. A £99 AI-sales-only subscription would not pay for itself in that scenario. At 250,000 sessions, the same assumptions produce £200 contribution. Actual channel share, influence beyond referrals, order economics and lift could be very different.

This suggests two reasonable customer filters: sufficient existing AI activity to measure economic change, or enough recurring catalogue work to justify the fee through saved effort and fewer factual errors. Tiny shops with little traffic and stable small catalogues are a poor default target.

**The next six weeks should buy evidence, with later renewals deciding whether to scale.**

All numbers in this plan are proposed decision gates, not industry benchmarks. The objective is to make rejection cheap and recognise genuine customer value early.

| Period | Work | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Interview ten qualified operators and two agencies; inspect their last actual catalogue problem and current tools | Repeated problem, owner, existing spend, consequences and implementation access |
| Week 2 | Give five relevant stores a small free, evidence-based audit; compare findings with native tools and one inexpensive competitor | Material issues missed elsewhere, false positives, actual staff effort, fixes applied |
| Weeks 3-4 | Offer a defined paid pilot; test £99/month with explicit limits, and a separately scoped setup fee when manual repair is needed | Money paid, why paid, onboarding/support minutes, all provider usage, actual use |
| Weeks 5-6 | Recheck after catalogue changes; measure correct fixes and saved task time; use live holdouts where volume permits | Recurring work, external outcomes, willingness to renew; an honest inconclusive result when traffic is sparse |
| Days 60-90 and 180 | Review continued payment and recurring usage after initial cleanup | Cohort retention, support cost, churn reasons and sustained merchant benefit |

My first continuation gate would be at least three of five qualified pilots paying the stated fee after being shown free/cheap alternatives, at least two independently demonstrating a recurring benefit above that fee, and delivery/support compatible with a stated margin target. This is intentionally a small commercial signal, not statistical proof of product-market fit. Three of five paying once is insufficient to scale paid acquisition. Look for at least three of the first five still actively using and paying at day 90, then test a second cohort before assuming retention.

For operational value, a reasonable initial gate is around four hours/month of independently checked savings at a merchant-specific £25/hour cost, or another demonstrated benefit above the fee. Time comparable real tasks before and with the product, independently check factual accuracy, and count completed corrections. Use the merchant's actual loaded labour cost. If the value only exists during cleanup, sell a one-off cleanup. If recurring work requires two hours per store, price it as a managed service or improve delivery before selling a £99 self-service plan.

For sales claims, freeze the buyer questions, primary outcome and break-even effect before editing. Randomise eligible product families or SKU clusters to treatment and holdout, balanced on baseline performance, category, price and stock. Analyse outcomes by original assignment and calculate uncertainty at that level. Account for promotions and spillover; if randomisation is impractical, label the result observational or quasi-experimental. Verify that the channel received the change and allow for crawl/feed lag. The six-week commercial readout can arrive before adequate search exposure: mark a sales result inconclusive if too few treated products became observable. Repeating a synthetic prompt hundreds of times does not substitute for independent customers or product-level controls. Measured work savings and renewal can validate a different value proposition when a sales test is underpowered. [Measurement plan](measurement.md)

Reject or change direction if qualified merchants only want a free score; native/cheap tools catch the same consequential errors; findings mainly require facts the merchant cannot obtain; monthly checks find nothing new; false positives prevent trust; or customers cancel after cleanup. An agency tool, a one-off repair service, and a recurring merchant SaaS are different outcomes. Choose based on observed buying and delivery behaviour.

My recommended commitment is a bounded pilot with a written cash/time cap, then a decision at six weeks and again after 90-day renewals. The evidence justifies testing a recurring repair workflow. It does not justify spending months building a broad observatory, promising better ChatGPT rank, or assuming free-user data will eventually rescue weak economics.
