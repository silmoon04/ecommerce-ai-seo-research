# Competitors: ecommerce AI SEO / GEO

**Snapshot date:** 2026-09-28. Prices are USD as shown on vendor or Shopify App Store pages on the snapshot date. App Store prices are generally billed every 30 days unless annual prices are stated. Public pricing and review counts change; where App Store and vendor pages disagree, the table uses the merchant-facing App Store offer and flags the conflict. “Review count” is a platform snapshot, not evidence of installs, revenue, retention, or product efficacy.

## Readout

Competition splits into three groups:

1. **AI visibility analytics** (Otterly, Peec, Profound, Scrunch) repeatedly sample prompts and report citations, mentions, competitors, and share of voice. They are usually not Shopify-native and often do not implement catalog fixes. Their spend starts around $29–$250/month, then rises with prompt volume, engines, and enterprise support.
2. **Shopify GEO/AEO apps** increasingly combine AI answer tracking with product audits, approved edits, crawler checks, schema, and revenue/referral analytics. Several are very new and priced from free to $10–$30/month. The closest direct products include Kedra, WPRobo, AIVisible, Catalyst, and the more expensive Burnish. At this point, “score the store, fix metadata/schema, publish llms.txt, track ChatGPT” is an established app-store template.
3. **Established Shopify SEO suites** already own merchant trust and distribution. SearchPie, StoreSEO, TinySEO, and Avada bundle titles, descriptions, alt text, schema, speed, and now LLM files or AI copy, from free or ~$14/month. They can add GEO features to an existing installed workflow.

The main strategic threat is that Shopify now says eligible products are automatically discoverable through Shopify Catalog, which supplies structured titles, descriptions, variants, images, prices, and availability to AI channels and keeps them current. Shopify also serves `/agents.md`, `/llms.txt`, and `/llms-full.txt` by default; its documentation explicitly says a third-party app is not required to generate those files. OpenAI says Shopify product data is already integrated with ChatGPT through Catalog. So “make the store crawlable” and “add an llms.txt” are weak standalone paid outcomes. A more defensible product would solve difficult catalog truth / attribute mapping and operational approval at product scale, then validate changes against representative live-channel outcomes and incremental contribution—not just readiness scores or referral attribution. ([Shopify Catalog documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/agentic-storefronts/products), [OpenAI shopping help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search))

**Bull case:** New Shopify-native tools have made AI shopping visibility salient enough for merchants to search for and install apps. A Shopify-native workflow can connect a report directly to catalog edits, avoid agency-style setup, and make an emerging channel understandable to small merchants. The category remains young: some close competitors have only a handful of public reviews, leaving room to win on evidence quality, usability, and trusted implementation.

**Bear case:** The surface feature set is rapidly commoditizing, with free audits, $9.99 plans, and $14/month apps beside mature free SEO tiers. Native distribution weakens crawler-readiness claims. A mention or visit does not show that a software change caused an incremental profitable order, and customer-specific answers vary by prompt, model, region, context, and time. A product that diagnoses but cannot safely resolve missing/uncertain source data risks handing merchants a work queue and losing renewal. Review counts cannot establish demand, retention, or ROI.

[Lily AI](https://www.lily.ai/) is an enterprise-adjacent catalog intelligence vendor rather than a low-priced Shopify App Store app. Its current Lily Max site offers a free 30-day audit on 500 products and pitches catalog attribute enrichment plus controlled tests across Google/Meta ads, AI shopping assistants and onsite search. It says results are measured against controls using holdouts, matched-spend experiments, and difference-in-differences with confidence intervals; pricing is demo-led. This is strategically important because it attacks product signal and proof of lift, rather than selling an AI-readiness score. Its claims are vendor-reported. ([Product platform announcement](https://www.lily.ai/blog/lily-ai-unveils-its-no-code-product-attributes-platform), [FAQs](https://www.lily.ai/faqs))

It is also worth separating True Fit: it is fit intelligence / product data infrastructure and not a direct Shopify GEO monitoring app. Its existence matters for a fashion-specific thesis, but does not substitute for validating what the merchant can accurately source about each size/fit attribute.

Their center of gravity is feed correctness and distribution, rather than whether an AI shopping surface recommends a product for a sampled buyer intent.

**Assessment:** A broad “find and fix catalog errors” product is not an open category. DataFeedWatch and Channable already validate required attributes, propose or apply rule-based remediation, and optionally generate missing values; Feedonomics sells a higher-touch QA/enrichment operation; Simprosys makes feed rules, variant sync, and errors accessible at a very low monthly price. A merchant could reasonably say the feed layer already fixes incomplete, malformed, or inconsistent fields. An unqualified promise to improve variant completeness, mappings, product titles, or feed health would be easy to copy and difficult to defend.

The narrower gap that remains **unproven** is a cross-layer answerability/commerce audit: reconcile the canonical product and variant facts against Shopify Catalog and destination feeds; identify conflicts at the variant/market level; test a disclosed set of real buyer queries on actual agentic surfaces; attribute each failure to source data, channel eligibility, retrieval, or answer/ranking behavior; then map the approved fix back to the exact source field and verify propagation. The legacy feed tools optimize compliance with documented endpoint schemas and campaign needs. The earlier audit suggested that they do not present a merchant-facing, query-level proof loop for AI recommendations. This is an inference about their product emphasis, not proof they lack every such feature. Shopify itself now offers agentic search previews and listing-quality signals, so the remaining differentiation would need to be cross-channel diagnosis, variant-specific root-cause linkage, reliable change history, and an external outcome test—not another readiness score. ([Shopify Agentic Storefronts admin guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/agentic-storefronts/agentic-home))

## Implications for product positioning

- **Avoid readiness-only positioning.** Shopify's native Catalog and discovery files cover baseline distribution and bot-readable files; apps can still add value in mapping, content quality, monitoring, and workflow, but can't claim those basic enablers are absent by default.
- **Differentiate on trusted product facts, not generated prose.** The hard job is identifying where product facts are missing or contradictory, mapping merchant metafields to channel requirements, proposing sourced attribute fixes, and requiring approval when source evidence is weak. Synthetic copy can be generated cheaply by incumbents.
- **Make monitoring auditable.** Show exact prompt, model/surface, region, timestamp, repetitions, observed cited URL/product, and scoring method. Separate crawl access, feed eligibility, AI mention, store visit, order attribution, and causal lift. A dashboard score cannot be represented as universal rank or guaranteed sales.
- **Make the recurring loop operational.** Version changes, approvals, rollbacks, stock/price freshness and bulk catalog actions address ongoing merchant work better than a one-time audit. Catalyst, Kedra, WPRobo and Burnish already advertise parts of this loop, so execution and evidence matter more than naming a fuller feature set.
- **Prove buyer willingness with an informed offer.** Merchants can compare free Shopify functionality, cheap Shopify apps and $100+ analytics platforms. A paid pilot should establish willingness to pay, implementation burden, meaningful external lift and renewal after the initial cleanup.
