# IP and rights for a quiet fandom clothing brand

Research checked 29 September 2026. UK-first planning note; individual designs and names have not been legally cleared.

My recommendation is to launch with original artwork that expresses the atmosphere you like: solitude, ruined architecture, difficult journeys, quiet rituals and recovery. Build recognisable symbols of your own. Treat clothing tied to a named anime or game as a separate licensing project. This gives the brand something it can develop across collections without depending on permission for every franchise reference.

UK copyright automatically protects original illustrations, photographs and other qualifying works. Copying artwork onto a garment, selling copies or using it online can engage those rights. Buying a print, finding an image online or paying someone to redraw it does not itself provide merchandise rights. These principles also apply to independent competitors' original graphics and photography. [IPO copyright overview](https://www.gov.uk/copyright), [using someone else's copyright](https://www.gov.uk/using-somebody-elses-intellectual-property/copyright).

The useful creative distinction is between a general idea or style and its particular expression. The IPO explicitly distinguishes these. The examples below are practical design guidance inferred from that distinction, not clearance decisions. [IPO guidance on clothing patterns and artwork](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/copyright-notice-knitting-and-sewing-patterns/copyright-notice-knitting-and-sewing-patterns).

| Starting point | Practical approach |
| --- | --- |
| Weathered swords, moons, ruins, medieval armour, samurai or historical runes | Develop your own drawing, composition and story. These subjects are not automatically owned by a franchise. Check the particular image and any source material. |
| Muted colours, tiny embroidery, distressed texture or a dark fantasy mood | Use these as art direction. Avoid reconstructing a competitor's complete graphic or distinctive branding. |
| A franchise emblem, character portrait, recognisable weapon design, screenshot or manga panel | Treat it as material requiring rights assessment and usually permission before commercial production. |
| A subtle redraw of an iconic character, symbol or scene | Small size and abstraction do not establish permission. Assess what protected material remains in the design. |

Minimalist designs still need rights checks. A small but important part can still be substantial. "Inspired by", "unofficial" and crediting the creator do not grant a licence. UK parody, caricature and pastiche exceptions require fair dealing; an affectionate reference on a commercial hoodie is not automatically covered. Assess the actual purpose and design before relying on an exception. [IPO guidance on substantial parts](https://www.gov.uk/using-somebody-elses-intellectual-property/copyright), [copyright exceptions](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/exceptions-to-copyright).

Names need a separate check. Franchise titles, logos and some character or item names may raise trade mark issues when used on clothing, labels, product titles or advertising. That does not make every single word, rune or genre term unavailable. The mark, its protection and the proposed use matter. Use original collection names and descriptions unless the reference has been assessed. [IPO IP basics](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ip-basics/ip-basics), [defending intellectual property](https://www.gov.uk/defend-your-intellectual-property).

FromSoftware's English FAQ permits publication of fan creations for personal enjoyment but expressly excludes commercial use, including sales. It describes possible one-day permission for amateur events through organisers, obtained in advance. That is not a standing permission for an online clothing shop. Bandai Namco Europe's video policy covers gameplay videos; it should not be read as an apparel licence. [FromSoftware FAQ](https://www.fromsoftware.jp/ww/faq-other.html), [Bandai Namco video policy](https://static.bandainamcoent.eu/content/BANDAI_NAMCO_Entertainment_Video_Policy_%2820220124%29.pdf).

For a franchise capsule, Bandai Namco Entertainment America has a merchandise-partner enquiry form listing Dark Souls and Elden Ring among its options. The form explicitly says submitting it grants no IP rights. For a UK launch, establish who can authorise the particular franchise, apparel category and territory; the American form is an enquiry route, not proof of UK authority. Prepare product concepts, proposed territories, quantities, channels and pricing, then establish the approval process and commercial terms before manufacture. [Bandai Namco merchandise licensing](https://www.bandainamcoent.com/licensing).

An independent artist collaboration is a different arrangement. In the UK, a commissioned artist normally owns their work unless otherwise agreed in writing. My suggested brief is original artwork, with a signed commercial licence or assignment covering garments, advertising, territories, duration, edits for embroidery, and permission for your manufacturer or print supplier to reproduce it. Agree credit, fees, exclusivity, source files and treatment of moral rights. Ask the artist to identify third-party material. Their permission covers rights they hold; commissioning fan art does not buy the underlying franchise rights. [IPO ownership guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/ownership-of-copyright-works), [licensing and moral rights](https://www.gov.uk/using-somebody-elses-intellectual-property/copyright).

A live listing is not evidence that another seller has permission. Etsy puts responsibility for necessary rights on sellers and can remove reported listings or disable accounts. Competitor research can establish demand and presentation choices, but it cannot establish the rights available to your business. [Etsy intellectual property policy](https://www.etsy.com/legal/ip/).

Treat any proposed brand names as creative candidates. Before choosing one, search exact and similar words and logos in the UK register, including clothing in class 25 and relevant retail services in class 35. Check wider trading use, Companies House, domains and social handles. A free domain or accepted company name does not clear a trade mark. Have the strongest candidate assessed before ordering labels or packaging. [UK trade mark search](https://www.gov.uk/search-for-trademark), [IPO classification guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-classify-trade-marks), [company names and domains](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/unacceptable-trade-marks).

US sales add another jurisdiction. UK registration does not protect a brand worldwide; check US marks and existing commercial use as well. US fair use is a case-specific assessment, not an automatic defence for fan merchandise or a substitute for a licence. [UK registration scope](https://www.gov.uk/how-to-register-a-trade-mark), [USPTO clearance guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks), [US Copyright Office fair use guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/fls/fl102.html).

For each launch design, keep a small rights file: sketches, reference sources, artist agreement, font or asset licences, relevant permissions and the final artwork. Review the garment and its listing together before ordering stock.
