# A lean validation plan for quiet fandom clothing

Checked 29 September 2026 against primary documentation. This is a proposed 90-day plan. Research sizes and decision rules are practical choices for this brand, not industry benchmarks. No accounts, purchases or outreach were made.

Start with two or three original designs on one sampled garment, with consistent photography and a public price. Consider a heavyweight tee if its actual cut and fabric support the quality promise. Use understated anime or Soulslike references that work as clothes someone would wear regularly. The first commercial question is whether strangers want this garment enough to pay a viable price.

Run two audience tracks. Fashion-first shoppers should judge the silhouette, artwork and styling before receiving an explanation of the reference. Fandom-first shoppers should judge whether the reference feels meaningful and wearable. Record overlap between groups. Start with roughly eight conversations in each track, recruited beyond friends and family. These can uncover specific objections, but cannot estimate market size.

Show identical mockups in varied order, labelled as concepts, with the proposed fabric, fit, price and delivery terms. Ask which they would wear, where, what they currently buy instead, and what would stop them buying. Include "none." Record whether they recognised the reference unprompted. Then reveal the inspiration and ask whether that changes their choice. "Looks cool" and "I would buy it" are useful interview material, but remain stated preference.

Move the strongest ideas into real samples. Check measurements, collar, seams, print feel and appearance after washing according to the care instructions. Photograph the garment on a wearer and close up. Publish composition, garment and model measurements, print method, care and delivery estimate. Calculate contribution from net revenue after discounts and applicable tax, less production, payment fees, shipping subsidy, packaging and expected returns. Include content and acquisition costs when judging whether another batch pays.

Sell only an offer that can be fulfilled. Use a clearly labelled notification signup while supply is unready. If preorders make sense, disclose payment timing, expected dispatch, cancellation/refund terms and how delays will be handled before payment. Shopify's preorder route requires an app and supported payment methods, so it is optional setup rather than a launch requirement. Never use a fake checkout, invented reviews or artificial scarcity. [Shopify preorder documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/purchase-options/pre-orders).

Keep content and product tests distinct. For a content comparison, hold the garment, price, destination page and offer fixed. Compare an outfit/fit opening with a reference/story opening using similar production effort. Use tagged links for each audience and hook. Organic distribution is uneven, so report reach, clicks, relevant visits and purchases; a post-to-post difference is directional, not a randomized finding about taste. Repeat a promising hook with another execution before commissioning more content.

For design preference, show the options with equal prominence and rotate their order. Keep price, garment, model and image quality constant. Otherwise the "winning design" might have better photography or a more popular creator. If a creator supplies video and distribution, record those costs separately. Once one product attracts buyers, a storefront A/B can compare placement of the same verified fit information. Assign visitors after arrival within the same traffic sources and preserve their assignment. Set the review date beforehand; sparse order differences remain unresolved. Before/after sales cannot separate the page change from different visitors.

Maintain a small weekly sheet: audience/source, hook, relevant visits, product views, carts, checkouts, paid orders, refunded orders, fulfilled orders, contribution and buyer objections. Keep friends, gifts and test orders separate. Shopify's main analytics are available across subscription plans, and marketing reports support UTM analysis. Reconcile orders to actual payments and returns because some report counts include pending, cancelled and unpaid orders. Record missing attribution rather than guessing it. [Shopify analytics](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/shopify-reports), [marketing report definitions](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/shopify-reports/report-types/default-reports/marketing-reports).

| Period | Work and decision |
| --- | --- |
| Days 1–14 | Define the two audiences, compare concepts and obtain samples. Set a cash limit for the whole first batch of learning. Advance when a design has specific appeal and the sample supports the quality promise. |
| Days 15–30 | Wear/wash-check samples, confirm costs and publish the honest offer. Revise the garment or price if quality and margin cannot coexist. |
| Days 31–45 | Run the first content batch and offer real purchases. If relevant visits are scarce, change distribution before concluding that nobody wants the designs. |
| Days 46–60 | Fulfil orders, collect fit/delivery feedback and inspect retained contribution. Repeated objections guide the next correction. Refunds and disappointing quality can outweigh initial sales. |
| Days 61–75 | Repeat the strongest audience/product pairing with fresh content and buyers. Run one focused page comparison only if traffic makes the effort worthwhile. |
| Days 76–90 | Choose another small production cycle, revise the proposition, or stop spending. Expand only when strangers repeatedly buy at a viable price and the delivered garment meets expectations. |

A few paid orders justify learning more; they do not establish repeatable demand. Report the actual visitor and order counts beside percentages. Do not impose a universal conversion target or a formal statistical threshold before fixing an obvious problem. When exposure is too thin to distinguish designs, keep that uncertainty and repeat cheaply. When adequately described products repeatedly fail at a viable price with the intended audience, reconsider design, positioning or supply costs before adding a catalogue.

No paid SEO or testing app is needed for these first decisions. SimGym is an AI Research Preview for public Liquid stores with Shopify Network Intelligence enabled. Shopify warns that real buyers may behave differently. A monthly allowance precedes USD 0.75 single-theme and USD 1.50 comparison charges. Use it optionally for page inspection, not demand evidence. Rollouts offers control/treatment experiments on Grow or higher; its analytics cover online-store/cart/checkout visitors. A tiny launch does not need a plan upgrade solely for A/B testing. [SimGym](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/simgym), [Rollouts requirements](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/markets/rollouts/rollout-types), [measurement scope](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/markets/rollouts/analytics).

Let AEO software learning follow genuine shopping questions: composition by colour, garment versus body measurements, available size/colour combinations and delivery expectations. Keep verified facts and observed wrong answers in a separate issue log. Shopify already has apparel category metafields, Catalog source mapping and free Knowledge Base FAQs. Establish that competent baseline before testing additional software. Knowledge Base explicitly improves answer accuracy without promising more appearances. A better answer in a recorded prompt is a useful technical result; retained purchases and repeatable acquisition determine whether the clothing brand deserves more investment.
