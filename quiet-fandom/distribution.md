Start with one wearable product, actual samples and a visible price. For a UK founder building understated anime or Soulslike clothing, I would spend the first month testing whether people want the garment enough to buy it. The channel priorities and budgets below are proposed experiments, not measured performance forecasts. Sources checked on 29 September 2026.

Test two audiences separately: people who already buy restrained streetwear, knitwear or dark fashion, and fans who want a reference they can wear daily. A fandom audience can recognise a motif without liking the fit or accepting the price. A fashion audience might buy the silhouette without recognising anything. Use the same product, price and destination page, changing only the opening creative and relevant audience context. Build around original work or properly cleared collaborations.

The cheapest useful content makes quality visible. Film a complete outfit outdoors, then a close-up of the texture and stitching. Compare the sample before and after a documented wash; show measurements and any shrinkage honestly. Show two bodies wearing the same size with their measurements. Explain one rejected sample and the correction. Try an original dark-fantasy detail revealed under a sleeve, or a muted knit worn to work and then to a gaming evening. Record with a phone in daylight; buy better samples before elaborate camera equipment. Texture shots without fit, price and delivery information leave the purchase questions unanswered.

Choose one primary video channel and reuse the footage elsewhere. Try four original clips weekly, two outfits photographed during the same shoot, and short replies to real questions. Adjust to the founder's available time.

Currently, r/streetwearstartup requires local activity and karma, limits posts and repeated items, removes blatant advertising, and bans AI-written posts/comments. The founder should write their own contributions. r/Eldenring bans sales, advertising, disguised routes to sales and unlicensed merchandise; its limited self-promotion allowance does not override that sales ban. Discord also prohibits unsolicited bulk messages and artificial engagement. Do not join servers to harvest members or DM a launch link. An empty brand server adds moderation work; an opt-in launch email is enough initially. [Discord community guidelines](https://discord.com/guidelines)

Build a shortlist of 20 relevant small creators, then choose five who already style comparable clothes and explicitly invite collaboration proposals. Check their recent ordinary posts, substantive comments, UK audience share when available, and whether viewers ask about clothes. Follower count alone is a poor buying criterion. A creator with 3,000 relevant followers is a candidate, not a promised cheap acquisition channel.

Separate two purchases. A content commission buys agreed photographs or video for your channels; evaluate the usable deliverables. A distribution agreement buys a post to the creator's audience; evaluate visits and attributable customers. A creator can be excellent at either and weak at the other. Ask for separate quotes. Offer a gifted sample without a posting obligation, or agree a paid deliverable; never imply a gift guarantees coverage.

For invited pitches, mention the specific outfit that made them relevant, show the actual sample, state the budget, and ask whether they prefer a content commission or a posted collaboration. Specify one short try-on video, truthful fit observations, timing and requested usage. Use invitations or opt-in routes rather than scraped contact lists. Public contact details do not themselves constitute marketing consent, and UK electronic-marketing protections include sole traders. [ICO electronic-mail guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/how-do-we-comply-with-the-pecr-electronic-mail-marketing-rules/)

An optional affiliate pilot could offer 10% of net merchandise revenue, excluding VAT, shipping and returned/cancelled orders, with a stated attribution window and monthly payments after returns. These are proposed terms, not a market benchmark. Check the contribution margin first; customer discounts and affiliate commission both consume it. Give each creator an individual link/code, retain a simple order ledger, and reconcile refunds. Commission-only arrangements may attract no suitable creators.

Agree usage rights in writing: which files, site and social channels, duration, editing permission, and whether paid advertising is included. Start with a limited organic-use licence; quote paid usage separately. Paying a freelancer does not automatically transfer copyright. Keep music and other third-party rights within the agreed uses. [Intellectual Property Office ownership guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/ownership-of-copyright-works)

Require a prominent upfront "Ad" disclosure on paid, gifted or affiliate promotional content. A discount code, "gifted" label or ambassador bio is not an adequate substitute; brand and creator share responsibility. Collect honest feedback without requiring praise. Request customer reviews after delivery, identify incentives, and publish genuine negative feedback under the same moderation policy. Never manufacture launch reviews or present paid creator testimonials as independent purchases. [ASA disclosure guidance](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-social-media.html), [CMA reviews guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews/short-guide-for-businesses-publishing-consumer-reviews-and-complying-with-consumer-protection-law)

Budget choices exclude product development, stock, website costs and founder pay. A cash-minimal version caps incidental spend at £75 using existing samples and founder content. A £250 creator version allocates up to £100 to sample costs/postage, £125 to one negotiated content commission and £25 to incidental costs; those are spending ceilings, not supplier quotes. If suitable quotes exceed the ceiling, continue founder content. An optional additional £100 paid-media pilot follows purchase-intent evidence and a working checkout. Its purpose is learning, not proving a sustainable acquisition cost.

Track relevant visits, price-aware sign-ups, checkout starts, paid orders, cancellations and returns by source. Separate friends from unrelated customers. A waitlist is weaker evidence than payment. Set a business gate using the actual minimum viable batch and contribution after fulfilment, fees and expected returns. Many views with few visits suggests a creative/destination problem. Visits without purchase may reflect price, fit, trust or availability. Low traffic leaves demand unresolved. Calculate customer acquisition cost from actual campaign costs and attributable new customers; report founder hours separately. Zero customers leaves CAC unmeasurable. Early results remain provisional until returns settle.
