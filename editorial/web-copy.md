# Is ecommerce AI SEO worth building?

Research snapshot: 28 September 2026. This report reviews the supplied conversations and published evidence. The findings come from desk research: no merchants were interviewed, no outreach was conducted, and no product trial was run. Prices and platform capabilities are dated observations.

## 01 · Test the repair workflow

Run a small free-to-paid pilot. The evidence supports testing a business; it doesn't yet support months of development on a broad AI-ranking subscription.

Start with a catalogue problem a merchant can recognise. A variant URL selects the wrong size. A product page and feed disagree about a material. The product finds the conflict, shows its source, helps the merchant approve a correction, and checks that the correction reached the intended destination. That's an outcome someone can inspect.

AI shopper tests could help diagnose those failures. A monthly fee needs recurring work behind it, such as checking changed products or saving staff time. Competitors already cover parts of this workflow, so the first test is whether yours finds and resolves consequential problems more usefully than the merchant's existing tools.

Public evidence doesn't establish this proposed product's acquisition cost, conversion, retention, incremental sales or profit. A useful small business is possible without an uncopyable algorithm. It needs customers who keep paying enough to cover acquisition and delivery.

## 02 · A growing channel, with a small measured footprint

People do pay for AI visibility. Peec reported more than $10 million ARR and more than 2,500 customers in its May 28, 2026 announcement. Those are company-reported figures, not audited profit or evidence about small Shopify apps. [Peec announcement](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/28/3303009/0/en/Peec-AI-hits-10M-ARR-16-months-after-launch-as-brands-race-to-optimize-for-AI-search.html)

Similarweb reports 203% annual growth in generative-AI referrals, alongside a 0.4% share of retail ecommerce visits. The published methodology doesn't give enough detail to apply that share to an individual shop. Adobe says May 2026 AI-referred US retail visits converted 54% better and produced 53% more revenue per visit than non-AI visits. These comparisons describe visitors arriving through a channel; they don't measure an optimisation app's effect. [Similarweb report](https://www.similarweb.com/corp/reports/state-of-ecommerce-2026/), [Adobe update](https://business.adobe.com/uk/blog/adobe-report-ai-traffic-travel-sites-surges-200-percent)

Some influence may appear later as direct visits or branded searches. That means referral share isn't a ceiling, but the missing influence cannot be filled in with an invented multiplier. Build the first prospect list around an identifiable operating problem. There is no defensible public count of merchants who need this exact offer and will renew.

## 03 · Much of the feature list is already for sale

The cheap end is crowded. IndexGPT lists free, $16 and $45 offers; SKAW lists free and $19.99 offers and advertises an Agent Query Simulator. Kedra's free, $25, $79 and $199 plans cover overlapping audits, approved fixes, buyer prompts and tracking. Simulation or a longer feature list won't establish a difference. These are advertised monthly US-dollar offers, not verified customer outcomes. [IndexGPT](https://apps.shopify.com/index-gpt), [SKAW](https://apps.shopify.com/skaw), [Kedra](https://apps.shopify.com/kedra-ai-index)

Monitoring has larger price points: Otterly lists $29 for 15 prompts, $189 for 100 and $489 for 400. That establishes an offer. It doesn't establish your acquisition costs or the profitability of small competing apps. [Otterly pricing](https://otterly.ai/pricing)

Shopify already distributes eligible products through Catalog and supplies a free readiness scanner, Knowledge Base and native agentic search previews. Inclusion doesn't guarantee recommendation, and channel eligibility still matters. Google says special AI files aren't required for its search features; Search Console now supplies GenAI impressions. A basic scanner or dashboard therefore competes with the platform itself. [Shopify products](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/agentic-storefronts/products), [readiness scanner](https://www.shopify.com/agentic-readiness), [Knowledge Base](https://apps.shopify.com/shopify-knowledge-base), [Google guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), [GenAI reporting](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139)

Catalogue repair is crowded too. DataFeedWatch, Feedonomics, Channable and Simprosys already sell feed rules, checks or managed operations. A narrower possibility is tracing a shopping failure to the exact source field and verifying its repair. That gap remains unproven. [DataFeedWatch](https://www.datafeedwatch.com/pricing), [Feedonomics](https://feedonomics.com/pricing/), [Channable](https://www.channable.com/pricing?currency=usd), [Simprosys](https://apps.shopify.com/google-shopping-feed)

## 04 · What the conversations got ahead of

The first conversation mainly pursued a convincing hackathon demonstration. Persistence, integrations and repeatable tests can make a useful product, but they don't establish that merchants will pay. Its proposed selection improvement from 1/12 to 7/12 was illustrative, not an observed experiment. A synthetic before-and-after result cannot establish live recommendation lift.

The second conversation takes commercial risks more seriously, but its pessimistic conclusions also need testing. Competition raises the burden of proof; it doesn't make entry impossible. Shopify's expanding coverage doesn't establish that every useful third-party workflow will disappear.

The earlier 41% revenue-per-visit figure does have Reuters support, reporting Adobe data. The comparison period is not clear enough to certify it as June or merge it with Adobe's other statistics. Keep the attribution attached. [Reuters, August 7, 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/retailers-tap-ai-shopping-traffic-fight-keep-customer-data-2026-08-07/)

## 05 · Free can teach you; it still needs distribution

Offer a small, useful audit: a product sample, limited shopper tests and evidence for a correction. State the future price up front. Larger coverage, scheduled checks and implementation support can be paid. Free use can reveal false positives and onboarding friction. Only a payment request tests willingness to pay.

Here is an assumed acquisition scenario, not a forecast. Give 100 free audits at £0.50 compute each. Value ten minutes of help per audit at £25/hour. The assumed conversion rates give these costs:

| Assumed conversion | 1% | 3% | 5% | 10% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Compute plus help per payer | £466.67 | £155.56 | £93.33 | £46.67 |

Marketing and sales time are excluded.

At the assumed 5% conversion rate, 100 payers require 2,000 completed audits before churn. Finding those merchants has a cost too. Don't count the same audit expense twice if it already sits inside measured customer acquisition cost.

ChartMogul's conversion research covers 200 B2B software products over six months. It can inform measurement; it cannot predict this product's results. Test catalogue-heavy merchants and agencies as separate groups because their pricing and support needs differ. [ChartMogul conversion report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)

## 06 · More records don't automatically make a moat

Competitors can collect public pages and synthetic answers again. Your own shopper bot can also produce an enormous dataset that mostly rewards agreement with itself. More useful evidence links a verified product fact to an implemented change, a defined external test and a real outcome.

Rights constrain the proposed pooled database. Shopify's API terms restrict merchant-data use to serving the relevant merchant. They also prohibit competitive benchmarking with merchant/customer information and constrain AI/ML improvement using Shopify-derived information, including anonymous or derived data. Merchant consent alone is not a safe assumption of cross-store reuse rights. Information collected from public websites comes from different sources and needs a separate rights assessment. [Shopify API terms, sections 2 and 6](https://www.shopify.com/legal/api-terms)

Make each merchant's own correction history valuable first. The business should work with connected-store information isolated per merchant. A permissioned research panel may become useful later, once rights and measurement quality are established. That possibility is a poor reason to subsidise unlimited free runs today.

## 07 · The margin depends on human work

Some supplier costs are public. Tavily PAYG costs $0.008 per credit. Apify's ChatGPT Search actor advertises $3 per 1,000 searches on Business and $5 per 1,000 on Starter; Starter's $19 plan includes usage credit. That prices search collection, not an autonomous shopping journey or coverage of every engine. Supabase Pro and Vercel Pro give a roughly $45/month starting infrastructure floor before additional usage. [Tavily](https://docs.tavily.com/documentation/api-credits), [Apify](https://apify.com/apify/chatgpt-search-scraper/pricing), [Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing), [Vercel](https://vercel.com/pricing)

The planning model assumes:

- £99/month excluding VAT.
- £10 delivery per payer.
- Thirty minutes of support at £25/hour.
- 5% monthly customer churn.
- £150 acquisition cost per replacement payer.

It adds £100 fixed overhead, £50 separate validation/research and £3,000 founder pay before tax. Delivery, support, churn and acquisition inputs are unmeasured assumptions.

Shopify billing uses its published 2.9% processing fee and assumes eligibility for the 0% revenue-share tier. Its qualifying $1 million threshold and exceptions need checking; it isn't simply an annual allowance. Don't also charge Stripe against that same sale. [Shopify revenue share](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share)

| Assumed active payers | Monthly revenue | Monthly surplus after stated costs and founder pay |
|---|---:|---:|
| 30 | £2,970 | -£1,166 |
| 48 | £4,752 | £24 |
| 100 | £9,900 | £3,463 |
| 300 | £29,700 | £16,689 |

These are steady-state scenarios. They exclude initial acquisition, onboarding, growth acquisition, refunds and taxes. Acquiring the first 100 payers costs £15,000 at the assumed acquisition cost, before onboarding. The 300-payer scenario needs 150 monthly support hours; unchanged overhead becomes optimistic.

Holding other assumptions fixed, a £49 plan needs 180 payers to break even; £199 needs 20. These are price sensitivities. At 100 £99 payers, increasing support to two hours each cuts surplus to about -£287/month. Low API costs cannot absorb unlimited help.

## 08 · The merchant needs a return too

At an assumed £25 contribution per incremental order, a £99 fee needs four extra orders each month before implementation costs. If staff time costs the merchant £25/hour, including employment overheads, the fee needs almost four verified hours saved each month. Use the merchant's own economics.

For illustration, take the sourced 0.4% referral share and assume 4% conversion, 20% causal order lift and £25 contribution per order. Those last three inputs are not established product performance. A 50,000-session shop would receive 200 AI-referred visits, eight baseline orders and 1.6 extra expected orders, worth £40/month. The same assumptions at 250,000 sessions produce £200 contribution. [Traffic-share source](https://www.similarweb.com/corp/reports/state-of-ecommerce-2026/)

A £99 subscription justified only by AI sales fails the smaller scenario. An attribution dashboard doesn't settle this: orders credited to ChatGPT aren't necessarily orders caused by your changes. Target merchants with enough existing AI activity to measure a change, or enough recurring catalogue work to justify the fee through saved effort. Small, stable catalogues are a weak default target.

## 09 · Keep fashion factual, and reuse code carefully

Fashion has a substantial information problem. A missing inseam still needs a trustworthy source: supplier records, a tech pack or a measured sample. An ordinary photo cannot reliably supply an unknown exact dimension. A tool that asks merchants to measure hundreds of variants may add work rather than save it.

Test consistency across supplied charts, variants and source records. Leave unknown values unknown. Compare this with a category where compatibility or dimensions can be checked against manufacturer documentation. True Fit and Lily already operate in fit intelligence or catalogue enrichment, so fashion isn't an empty category. [True Fit inputs](https://www.truefit.com/fit-intelligence-spec), [Lily AI](https://www.lily.ai/)

Open-source code can shorten the build. The chat's exact OpenSEO link is the MIT-licensed afazhriel/openseo fork of every-app/open-seo; it supplies adjacent SEO workflows and still needs paid data providers and hosting. Mention Network's ecommerce engine is a pre-alpha scaffold under FSL-1.1-ALv2, with competing-use restrictions. Those restrictions prevent treating it as a ready-made permissively licensed base for this business. Keep traditional SEO metrics and shopper tests separately labelled. [OpenSEO](https://github.com/afazhriel/openseo), [upstream](https://github.com/every-app/open-seo), [Mention Network engine](https://github.com/MentionNetwork/ecommerce-ai-visibility-engine), [license](https://github.com/MentionNetwork/ecommerce-ai-visibility-engine/blob/main/LICENSE.md)

## 10 · Test demand and delivery over six weeks

The following numbers are proposed decision gates, not industry benchmarks. Set a cash and time cap, and a minimum margin, before starting.

In week 1, interview ten qualified operators and two agencies about their last catalogue problem. In week 2, give five relevant stores a bounded audit and compare it with native tools and an inexpensive competitor. During weeks 3-4, offer a limited £99/month paid pilot, with a separately scoped setup fee where manual repair is needed. In weeks 5-6, recheck after catalogue changes and measure completed corrections, support effort and saved task time.

Continue cautiously if at least three of five qualified pilots pay after seeing alternatives, at least two independently demonstrate recurring benefit above the fee, and delivery meets the margin target set before the pilot. Around four verified hours saved per month at a merchant-specific £25/hour is one proposed value gate. These small samples cannot establish product-market fit.

Check usage and payment at days 60-90 and 180. Look for at least three of the first five still active and paying at day 90, then test a second cohort before scaling acquisition.

For sales claims, define the outcome before edits, use comparable product groups and randomised holdouts where feasible, and verify that changes reached the channel. Sparse traffic or insufficient exposure means an inconclusive result. Repeated synthetic prompts cannot replace real outcomes.

If value ends after cleanup, sell a cleanup. If merchants need two hours of recurring help, price a managed service or improve delivery. Payment, useful work and later renewals should determine which business gets built.
