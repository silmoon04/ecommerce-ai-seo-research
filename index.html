A product page and its feed disagree about the material. A variant link selects the wrong size. Your tool finds the conflict, cites the evidence and checks the repair. That's the first business hypothesis to test.

A broad AI-ranking subscription is harder to justify. Basic scans are free, monitoring is crowded, and an impressive simulation doesn't establish live sales lift. Catalogue repair is also competitive. The opportunity has to survive comparison with what merchants already use.

TEST FIRST

Find, approve and verify a useful repair.

Test whether recurring catalogue checks and repairs save enough staff time to justify the fee.

NEEDS PROOF

A monitoring subscription merchants renew.

Adjacent buyers spend money. This product's acquisition cost, retention and incremental value are still unknown.

DON'T ASSUME

Unlimited free users create a data moat.

Data rights, representativeness and outcome quality matter more than the number of stored answers.

What this research can say

This is desk research, not merchant interviews or a product trial. Public evidence can support a test. Asking merchants to pay will show whether they value this offer enough to buy it.

Six different things that shouldn't share one score.

A pass at one stage doesn't prove the next. Randomise comparable product families where feasible, wait for changes to reach the relevant channel, and preserve an untreated comparison. A six-week sales pilot may be too short to establish incremental sales.

05 / THE COST OF FREE

Free can teach you.
It still needs distribution.

Offer a bounded audit. State the future price before the free work starts.

Free users can expose false positives and onboarding friction. A payment request tests something different: whether the work matters enough to buy. Larger coverage, scheduled checks and implementation help can be paid.

What does a free audit really cost?

ASSUMPTIONS

Excludes marketing and sales time. If these audit costs are inside your measured acquisition cost, don't add them again to the business model.

At the assumed 5% conversion rate, 100 payers need 2,000 completed audits before churn. A generous free allowance doesn't find those merchants for you. Test catalogue-heavy operators and agencies separately, because their needs and support costs differ.

06 / THE DATABASE IDEA

Collecting answers is easy.
Learning from outcomes is harder.

Your shopper bot can generate a large dataset that mostly rewards agreement with itself. Competitors can also collect public pages and synthetic answers again.

01Public pages & synthetic answers

Easy to replenish. Reproducibility alone is a weak advantage.

02Verified merchant facts

Verified facts, with a recorded source and permission to use them.

03Approved changes & propagation

A record of what changed, why, and whether the change arrived.

04Reliable external outcomes

More informative, but expensive to measure and limited by rights.

The pooled-data constraint

Shopify's API terms prohibit competitive benchmarking with merchant/customer information and constrain AI/ML reuse, including anonymous or derived data. Merchant consent alone is not a blanket cross-store reuse right.

Read Shopify's API terms ↗

Make each merchant's own correction history valuable first. The business should work with connected-store information isolated per merchant. A permissioned research panel can be investigated later; it is a poor reason to fund unlimited free runs now.

08 / THE MERCHANT'S RETURN

Your margin isn't enough.
The customer needs a return.

For illustration, a £99 fee needs four extra orders at £25 contribution each, or almost four verified hours saved at £25/hour, before implementation costs.

Referral attribution doesn't show how many orders your changes caused. This calculator deliberately separates channel traffic from assumed causal lift.

Would the value cover the fee?

ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO

The initial 0.4% share comes from Similarweb and is only a scale example. All other starting values are assumptions. Causal lift is unmeasured, and time savings must be independently verified. Excludes implementation costs; fractional orders are expected values.

Small, stable catalogues are a weak default target. Look for enough existing channel activity to measure a change, or enough recurring catalogue work to justify the fee through saved effort.

03

Fashion needs ground truth.

An ordinary photo cannot reliably provide an unknown exact inseam. Use supplied charts, manufacturer records or a measured sample. Leave unknown facts unknown.

Test consistency before fit prediction. Compare fashion with a category whose dimensions or compatibility are easier to verify.

Can open-source projects shorten the build?

Yes, with limits. The exact OpenSEO link in the chat is the MIT-licensed afazhriel/openseo fork of every-app/open-seo. It covers adjacent SEO workflows and still needs paid data providers and hosting.

Mention Network's ecommerce engine is a pre-alpha scaffold under FSL-1.1-ALv2 with competing-use restrictions. It is not a ready-made, permissively licensed commercial foundation.

10 / THE SIX-WEEK TEST

Test demand and delivery
over six weeks.

Set a cash and time cap, and a minimum margin. Find out whether the work saves merchants enough time or money to keep paying.

WEEK 01

Find the actual problem.

Speak with ten qualified operators and two agencies about their last catalogue issue.

WEEK 02

Run five bounded audits.

Compare your findings with native tools and an inexpensive competitor. Show the future price.

WEEKS 03-04

Ask for the payment.

Offer a limited £99/month pilot. Scope manual setup separately. Record objections and delivery time.

WEEKS 05-06

Check recurring value.

Recheck after catalogue changes. Measure completed repairs, support effort and verified saved time.

PROPOSED DECISION GATES

Continue only if the evidence earns it.

3 / 5Qualified pilots pay after seeing alternatives.
2Merchants independently demonstrate recurring benefit above the fee.
90 daysAt least three of the first five pilots remain active and paying.

Delivery and support must also fit the margin target set before the pilot. These are deliberately small pilot gates, not industry benchmarks or proof of product-market fit. Check renewals again at 180 days and repeat with a new cohort.

Stop or change the offer if users only want a one-off score, can't act on the findings, need more help than the price covers, or require facts you cannot source. Keep it small until repeated payments and operating results justify more.
