# Experimental design for an owned Shopify laboratory

Research date: 29 September 2026. Proposed experiments and numerical assumptions below are planning examples; none has been run. This extends the previous [harness analysis](../conversion-research/harness-landscape.md) and [value model](../conversion-research/value-and-power.md).

An owned store is a viable laboratory. It gives you control over product facts, releases, instrumentation and fulfilment, and lets you learn without first persuading another merchant to install unfinished software. The strongest initial question is whether the offline system selects useful edits better than a competent factual audit. A profitable store would establish retail viability; a repeatable improvement across other merchants, with paid adoption, would support a software business. Those are two businesses with different costs and evidence requirements.

Start with a coherent niche whose products you can actually deliver. Fifty unrelated products bring fifty demand and supply problems; fifty descriptions of one product share almost all their underlying demand. Use roughly fifty candidate edits as a discovery budget, then take a few distinct mechanisms into live tests. Establish good conventional SEO and usable product pages before comparing edits, so the baseline represents the alternative a merchant could reasonably adopt.

Keep three experiments separate:

| Experiment | What changes | What the result establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offline replay | Your product's representation inside recorded tool responses; other inputs stay fixed | Selection or comprehension in the named model and fixture distribution, conditional on the supplied candidates |
| Live discovery | Published catalogue facts or descriptions, followed through a named external engine | Observed retrieval/recommendation changes; a causal acquisition claim needs a suitable assignment design |
| Storefront A/B | The page experience assigned after a visitor arrives | An effect on outcomes among eligible arrivals from the tested traffic population |

An approximation of GPT or Claude tooling can reveal sensitivities, but its retrieval, prompts and orchestration remain your experimental system. Replacing a result in that system does not make an external engine retrieve it. Keep untouched live-engine observations as a calibration set. Freeze predicted winners before observing live outcomes, and include safe neutral or low-scoring edits; testing only predicted winners conceals false negatives.

Paid traffic is useful for acquiring visitors quickly enough to test onsite clarity, variant selection and checkout. Randomize after arrival within the same campaign, rather than comparing two campaigns whose auctions and audiences differ. This estimates an onsite effect for that traffic. It supplies no proof that AI-organic discovery increased, and paid shoppers may respond differently from organic shoppers.

For acquisition, define units before the intervention. Group substitutable products and their size/colour variants together, randomize groups within baseline-demand strata, and keep assigned product families in the analysis even when they receive no visits. Compare paid orders and retained contribution across those fixed groups, with observed AI referrals as a secondary breakdown. This can estimate a total effect under its assumptions; isolating an AI-specific causal effect needs channel-side assignment or stronger identification. Comparing conversion only among arrivals selects customers partly caused to arrive by the edit; it can hide useful acquisition or exaggerate quality changes. The total number of eligible external AI shopping opportunities is generally unavailable to a merchant. A prompt panel cannot supply that denominator.

Fifty products on one domain remain exposed to shared brand reputation, links, promotions and engine updates. Products compete with each other; a treated product may take sales from its control neighbour. Keep related products together, inspect whole-category totals, and report possible spillovers. Page groups cannot isolate a storewide branding change. Cluster assignment also means the effective sample is not the number of sessions. For a new store with little baseline traffic, an upstream comparison may remain directional until enough independent families have meaningful outcomes.

More variants increase the chance of selecting noise. Choose mechanisms on development prompts, reserve unseen intent families, and confirm a finalist on fresh data. Synonymous prompts and repeated model draws belong to the same family. Reusing the holdout after every rewrite turns it into training data. Keep a persistent live control across iterations as well, while recording any unavoidable shared fixes. Report all attempted variants, metrics and stopping decisions. Multiple-comparison adjustments or fresh confirmation address false discoveries; a selected winner's estimated effect can still be exaggerated. [Winner's curse research](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/stan.12241).

Daily monitoring can catch breakage. It should not become "stop the first time the ordinary p-value looks good." Use a prespecified horizon and sample target, or an appropriate sequential procedure with explicit decision boundaries. Maintain immediate rollback rules for factual errors and checkout failures regardless of statistical significance. [Johari and colleagues on continuous monitoring](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2021.2135).

For a concrete fixture experiment, assume a fictional desk accessory has a verified clamp range of 20–45 mm. Control copy contains that fact in a specification paragraph; treatment repeats it clearly beside the fit explanation. An intent involving an 18 mm desktop should produce rejection or a suitable alternative. A higher raw recommendation rate would be the wrong goal. Freeze candidate order, competitors, price, stock, page responses, model configuration and scoring rules. Unrecorded tool requests must produce an unsupported-run flag, not an unnoticed live-web fallback.

Assume 60 distinct intent families: 40 for development and 20 locked for one finalist. Five repeats of control and treatment on each held-out family produce 200 runs, but only 20 held-out contexts, not 200 independent customers. Score suitability, factual errors and correct variant links. The useful finding is whether clearer facts improve decisions on unseen contexts. It cannot establish increased purchases or earned search position. These counts are an illustrative fixture design, not a power calculation.

For one real-customer experiment to run, test a fit-summary module against the current specification presentation on the same product family. Both versions contain the same verified facts. Assign eligible visitors equally and persist their assignment across return visits. Keep acquisition-facing catalogue/feed copy, prices, promotions and campaign targeting constant. Define the primary endpoint as at least one paid order within seven days per assigned visitor, counting zero for non-buyers. Measure all linked merchant orders to reveal substitution; reconcile refunds and retained contribution after a declared maturity window, provisionally 30 days if the return policy supports it. These windows are proposed choices to validate against actual buying and return delays.

Shopify's control/treatment Rollouts experiments require Grow or higher. The proposed £25 Basic subscription budget therefore cannot assume native experiments are included; use a separately implemented and validated assignment method or revise the budget. Verify assignment persistence and the order join before relying on either approach. For this experiment, a different theme presentation may be suitable; catalogue-state changes are not a general description-testing API. [Shopify rollout types](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/markets/rollouts/rollout-types).

The following independently checked planning calculations assume equal independent Bernoulli arms, one prespecified comparison, 80% power, two-sided 5% significance and the usual normal approximation without continuity correction:

| Hypothetical purchase-rate change | Relative change | Visitors per arm | Total |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2.5% to 5.0% | +100% | 906 | 1,812 |
| 2.5% to 3.0% | +20% | 16,792 | 33,584 |

Calculation: `n = ceil((1.959964*sqrt(2*pbar*(1-pbar)) + 0.841621*sqrt(p0*(1-p0)+p1*(1-p1)))^2 / (p1-p0)^2)`, with `pbar=(p0+p1)/2`. These totals do not cover clustering, multiple variants, missing outcomes or power for profit. They show why large effects can be practical early and small effects costly. They do not predict either effect. Do not postpone mechanical QA or directional learning until a large trial is affordable; reserve strong causal claims for designs and uncertainty that support them.

Mechanical QA needs reproducible evidence that facts, links, assignment and orders behave correctly. Directional discovery needs enough varied cases to choose the next test, with uncertainty visible. A causal claim needs defensible assignment, prespecified outcomes and stopping, credible exposure measurement, and analysis at the assignment unit. Report an effect interval and its economic meaning; crossing a significance threshold alone neither makes the gain useful nor proves transfer to another store.

Make "heavy tracking" mean a small reconciled measurement system. Record assignment, exposure/version, source when available, product/variant, paid order, refund and costs. Deduplicate events and separate synthetic traffic. Shopify pixels have sandbox and consent-dependent visibility limits; adding more scripts can also impair performance. Reconcile purchase events against the order ledger and retain an unknown-source category. A changed referrer label is not an incremental order. [Shopify pixel capabilities and limits](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/promoting-marketing/pixels/overview).

An eight-to-twelve-week programme can use these learning gates:

| Planning window | Work and decision gate |
| --- | --- |
| Weeks 1–2 | Launch the coherent catalogue, verify factual accuracy, crawlability, purchasing, refunds and assignment joins. Keep a release and promotion log. Proceed when the measurement path reconciles, not when a dashboard looks busy. |
| Weeks 3–4 | Collect baseline demand and run fixture discovery. Choose one mechanism; lock unseen intents and a persistent live control. Estimate attainable visitor volume and the smallest economically useful effect. Reject variants that invent facts or damage suitability. |
| Weeks 5–6 | Start the visitor test if volume and measurement support it. Publish a separately assigned catalogue experiment only if suitable product families exist. Check deployed values, crawler/index status and sampled live responses separately; record failed propagation. |
| Weeks 7–8 | Assess completed follow-up windows, factual reliability, assignment balance and contribution. Classify evidence as mechanical QA, directional discovery or causal measurement. Sparse acquisition data means continue observing or change the research question, not declare victory or failure. |
| Weeks 9–12 | Recheck a selected mechanism on fresh intents, later engine observations and external merchants/categories. Compare against a simple factual audit. Offer a concrete paid pilot to budget owners and measure implementation effort, acceptance and repeat use. |

These are work windows, not a guaranteed SEO timetable. A new domain has no established demand history, and indexing is not guaranteed. Google says crawling can take days to weeks and search effects can take hours to months. A successful deployment or crawl is not evidence that the revised fact reached a particular AI answer. Keep the calendar flexible when that gate fails. [Google recrawl guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/ask-google-to-recrawl), [SEO timing guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).

Record concurrent stockouts, price changes, reviews, ads, seasonal demand and site fixes; do not silently attribute their effects to copy. Early revenue remains provisional until cancellations and returns mature. Judge the retail operation after product, fulfilment, acquisition and operating costs; judge the software after model, support, integration and merchant-review costs. Continue toward SaaS when the method chooses worthwhile edits beyond the owned store, saves scarce testing effort, and earns paid adoption. One store can establish a useful mechanism and an operational workflow. Transferability and merchant willingness to pay require customers outside it.
