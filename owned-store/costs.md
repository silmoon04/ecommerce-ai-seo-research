Costs checked on 29 September 2026. This note assumes a UK-based merchant selling to UK customers in GBP, using Shopify Basic and domestic print-on-demand fulfilment. Country, VAT status and the user's budget are unconfirmed. The proposed spending limits below are choices for a small experiment, not supplier quotes or a forecast of demand.

A shop is affordable to put online; producing reliable profit evidence costs more. Start with one supplier, a free theme and roughly 50 sellable variants drawn from a few related products. Keep physical sampling and customer service manageable. A paid supplier subscription, paid SEO app and custom storefront are unnecessary commitments for this pilot.

The current platform costs are:

| Item | Observed terms | Budget implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shopify Basic, monthly | £25 GBP per monthly billing cycle | Use this for the pilot. |
| Shopify Basic, annual | £19/month equivalent | £228 for the year, before applicable taxes; not £19 cancellable monthly. |
| Introductory promotion | Three days free, then £1/month for three months advertised | Treat eligibility and checkout terms as unconfirmed; budget at the normal rate. |
| Shopify Payments, online standard cards | 2% + £0.25 per transaction | A £30 payment costs £0.85 at this rate. |
| Online Amex/international cards | 3.1% + £0.25 | Do not apply the standard-card rate to every order. |
| Third-party payment provider | Basic lists a 2% third-party transaction fee | Provider fees can be additional. |

These prices come from [Shopify's UK pricing page](https://www.shopify.com/uk/pricing). Monthly billing is every 30 days; annual billing charges the full year upfront. Subscription fees are generally non-refundable. Those details favour monthly billing while the shop is still an experiment. [Shopify billing cycles](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/manage-billing/managing-your-bills/viewing-your-bills/billing-cycles-thresholds)

Shopify's fees exclude applicable taxes. The actual bill depends on the billing address and tax treatment; £25 is not a confirmed VAT-inclusive charge. Allow £30 per cycle for planning, comprising the observed £25 plus a provisional £5 tax buffer. Confirm the invoice treatment before committing. The UK's standard VAT rate is 20%, but that does not establish the user's registration or liability. [Shopify UK terms, section 5](https://www.shopify.com/uk/legal/terms), [GOV.UK VAT rates](https://www.gov.uk/vat-rates)

Basic includes unlimited products, so there is no Shopify per-listing charge for this catalogue. A product can currently have 2,048 variants and three options; supplier integrations may impose their own narrower limits. Fifty variants are also different from fifty products: ten designs with five sizes each produce fifty stock-keeping units, while ten product pages carry the discovery experiment. Count both explicitly. [Shopify pricing](https://www.shopify.com/uk/pricing), [Shopify variant limits](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/variants/add-variants)

For a price anchor, Printful's UK t-shirt page advertises adult shirts at £8.10 to £17.94 with one standard print placement; delivery is extra. Its shipping table, retrieved in USD, lists UK standard shirt shipping at $4.79 for the first item and $1.50 for an additional item. Do not silently treat those dollars as pounds or combine the cheapest product and shipping figures into a guaranteed landed quote. Obtain a GBP quote for the exact size, colour, print, fulfilment origin and postcode, including tax. My proposed 90-day allocation is **£500 of planned experiment spending, plus £250 held separately for fulfilment and refunds**. It excludes payment for the founder's labour. It also excludes an unlimited promise to fund customer orders: order acceptance must remain within available cash.

| Allocation | Amount | Basis |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Three Shopify billing cycles | £90 | Observed £75 subscription total plus provisional £15 tax allowance. |
| Domain | £20 | Chosen allowance for an annual registration; obtain an actual quote. |
| Physical samples | £90 | Chosen cap for checking quality, sizing, packaging and delivery. |
| Optional traffic experiment | £150 | Discretionary cap, released only after tracking and checkout work. |
| Metered AI/API or other experiment tools | £50 | Discretionary cap; start at zero if existing tools suffice. |
| Contingency | £100 | Unallocated headroom, not a spending target. |
| Planned experiment allocation | **£500** | Includes the optional advertising allocation. |
| Separate working cash | **£250** | A reserve, not an expense when merely held. |

This gives an initial cash envelope of £750. Before advertising, the platform, domain and sample allocations total £200. Promotions may reduce actual spending; they should not justify raising another allocation automatically. No account, subscription or purchase has been created by this research.

Working cash matters because the shop receives the customer's payment while the supplier charges the merchant separately. Printify cannot withdraw fulfilment costs from Shopify customer receipts; it charges when an order enters production. Manual approval is available. Shopify lists a UK minimum settlement time of three business days, with payout schedules and possible delays still affecting bank receipt. A sale therefore does not guarantee immediately usable cash. [Printify payment process](https://help.printify.com/hc/en-us/articles/4483601124113-How-does-the-payment-process-work), [Printify charge timing](https://help.printify.com/hc/en-us/articles/4483630446865-When-will-I-be-charged-for-my-Printify-order), [Shopify payout timing](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/payouts/payout-timing)

As a reserve stress check, ten unsettled orders costing £18 each plus two £30 customer refunds require £240. That almost exhausts £250. This is an invented scenario, not an expected order rate. Track cash, unsettled supplier commitments and pending refunds daily; reduce acquisition or stop accepting new orders before that headroom disappears. Already accepted orders remain obligations.

For contribution arithmetic, assume the merchant is lawfully not VAT-registered and cannot reclaim supplier VAT. The following values are deliberately invented except the standard-card fee:

| One illustrative order | GBP |
| --- | ---: |
| Customer payment, including delivery | £30.00 |
| Supplier product/print, excluding VAT | -£12.00 |
| Supplier delivery, excluding VAT | -£3.00 |
| Assumed irrecoverable 20% VAT on those supplier charges | -£3.00 |
| Standard-card fee: 2% of £30 + £0.25 | -£0.85 |
| Provisional allowance for returns/replacements | -£1.50 |
| Contribution before acquisition and fixed costs | **£9.65** |

The £1.50 allowance is a sensitivity input, not a measured return rate. Replace it with observed losses as orders mature. Include any actual import charges, currency fees, discounts, extra prints and supplier credits. If VAT registration applies, rebuild the model using revenue net of output VAT and costs net of recoverable input VAT; do not mix the two accounting bases. Registration depends on the business's circumstances, including existing taxable turnover. [GOV.UK registration guidance](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat)

At £9.65 contribution, four such orders cover a £30 subscription allocation only. Fifty-two cover £500 of experiment spending before labour and income/corporation tax, provided those orders actually achieve the assumed contribution. Neither calculation predicts sales. At an assumed £6 acquisition cost per order, contribution falls to £3.65; at £10 it becomes negative £0.35. There is no evidence here for actual CPC, conversion, acquisition cost or repeat purchases. In period accounts, deduct actual advertising once: do not subtract both the campaign invoice and an allocated per-order advertising cost.

Refund exposure stays with the retailer. Printful excludes buyer's remorse from its supplier refund coverage, and size exchanges can require paying for another item. Shopify does not return the original card processing fee when refunding. UK distance-selling rules normally provide cancellation rights, with exceptions including certain personalised/custom-made items; do not assume every catalogue POD design qualifies for an exception or copy a supplier policy as the retail policy. [Printful return costs](https://www.printful.com/uk/blog/top-5-printful-policies-your-store-should-copy), [Shopify refunds](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/payouts/refunds), [GOV.UK returns](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds)

Keep three results visible: actual retail contribution and operating profit after all attributable costs; experiment spending and founder hours; and evidence relevant to a future software product. Record refunds, samples and owner/test purchases separately from customer demand. A store can produce useful measurement lessons while losing money. A profitable store can still fail to show that other merchants will pay for the software. Preserve the pre-change baseline, change log and order-level costs so AI/SEO experiments can be judged against actual contribution, without assigning an invented share of sales to AI discovery.
