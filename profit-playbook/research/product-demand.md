# Product demand and selection research

Checked 30 September 2026. This note applies to an early UK seller with little inventory capital. It adds a demand-testing method to the existing clothing research, which already establishes plausible alternatives and sample/offer questions. No demand, willingness to pay, or product-market fit has been established for this seller. Three additional product groups below are hypotheses for testing, not proven opportunities.

## Principles for choosing what to test

| # | Principle | Evidence and limitation | Specific next test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Start with an expensive, concrete customer problem or a distinctive object, then ask whether it survives the delivered-price comparison. Category audience size is not a buyer count. | Existing evidence shows recognizable, subtle and premium fandom clothes are already sold by UNIQLO, Steady Hands, OMOCAT, Shihiko and licensed specialists. This establishes alternatives and price anchors; none publishes comparable unit sales. The current clothing note correctly leaves unknown-brand willingness to pay open. [Existing competitor scan](https://silmoon04.github.io/ecommerce-ai-seo-research/quiet-fandom/competitors.md), [existing validation plan](https://silmoon04.github.io/ecommerce-ai-seo-research/quiet-fandom/validation.md). | For clothing and each new category, make a 1-page offer with actual or clearly labelled concept images, target delivered price, material/specification, lead time and returns. Ask the participant to choose between it, a named real alternative and “buy nothing.” Then seek paid orders at the feasible price. |
| 2 | Treat a search trend as a direction or relative comparison, never as unit demand or price acceptance. | Google Trends uses a sampled, anonymized and aggregated set of searches, normalizes values to the time/location and scales them 0–100; low-volume queries may appear as 0 and statistical noise can create apparent spikes. Google says it is not a poll. A 100 is a relative peak in the selected comparison, not 100 searches. [Google Trends data FAQ](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533?hl=en-GB). | Compare 3–5 plain-language candidate phrases, same region, same window, and topic/query settings; repeat across a 5-year view and a recent period. Only advance a candidate when the direction is stable and separate sources show transactions or explicit buyer problems. Save the exact settings/date because another query comparison can rescale the chart. |
| 3 | Use keyword volumes to make a shortlist and map customer language, not calculate a sales forecast. | Google Keyword Planner shows estimated, rounded monthly searches and advertiser competition; low-volume terms may be unavailable. Forecasts depend on bid, budget, seasonality and ad quality, and Google requires billing details to access basic Planner features. Search volume is not purchase rate. [Keyword Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243), [historical metrics and forecast limits](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3022575?hl=en-CA). | Build a 20-query sheet per category: product names, use cases, “for X” needs, fit/compatibility, and problem phrases. Record location, date, ranges, suggested variants and whether the query is commercial or informational. Don’t multiply volume by an assumed conversion rate. |
| 4 | Prefer marketplace data that reflects a marketplace where the seller could actually list, while preserving its scope limits. | Etsy says Marketplace Insights uses Etsy search data, showing searches and listings over the last 30 days; its free tier allows 15 searches weekly, while Etsy Plus ($10 USD/month) unlocks unlimited searches. It reflects Etsy activity only, and Etsy explicitly warns search terms can include prohibited terms. Search/listing imbalance does not show purchases, listing quality or profitability. [Marketplace Insights help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/35122361353239-How-Do-I-Use-Etsy-s-Marketplace-Insights-Tool), [Etsy Plus fee](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001589928-What-is-Etsy-Plus). | Spend the 15 free queries deliberately across candidate, synonym, material, occasion and problem terms. Record the exact phrase, recent search count, listing count and related terms. Use Etsy Plus only if a live Etsy-selling decision justifies the subscription; don’t buy it solely to estimate a Shopify market. |
| 5 | Treat third-party SEO tools as additional estimates, not independent proof if they reuse Google inputs. | Ahrefs says its volume combines Google Keyword Planner, Trends and other sources; its Starter plan is $29/month and its help page says the estimate is roughly accurate for 60% of studied terms against Search Console impressions. That is vendor-reported validation for keyword impressions, not purchase demand or niche-level accuracy. [Ahrefs data method and stated study](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/72571-how-accurate-is-keyword-search-volume-in-ahrefs), [Starter plan](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/9419051-about-ahrefs-starter-plan). | Use free sources first. If a paid month is considered later, prewrite the decision the data could change, collect competitor pages and query clusters in that month, then cancel if the question is answered. A $29 subscription is unjustified if it only repeats the free trend pattern. |
| 6 | Research competitors at product and offer level, not by brand fame or follower counts. | Product pages expose price, construction, variants, availability, lead time, shipping, returns, customization and proof of manufacture. The earlier apparel audit demonstrates material differences: licensed brand recognition and garment construction vary widely, and several pages had sold-out items without sales data. A listing, restock notice or “limited” tag alone provides no sell-through number. [Existing competitor scan](https://silmoon04.github.io/ecommerce-ai-seo-research/quiet-fandom/competitors.md). | For each candidate, compare 10–15 live offers across at least three sellers: price plus delivery, core material, key specification, options, dispatch, return terms, review count/date, stock state, and one visual differentiation. Include a mass retailer, specialist and handmade/small seller where relevant. Note what remains unobservable. |
| 7 | Read reviews as a source of failure modes and desired outcomes, not as a representative survey. | Reviews are self-selected; galleries can be curated, reviews can be aggregated across variants, and a high average hides how many customers never review. Shopify’s Shop review flow is purchase-gated, but it still states it does not warrant review accuracy or reliability; policy requires neutral requests and bans fake or conflicted reviews. [Shop reviews](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shop/product-reviews), [review guidelines](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shop/product-reviews/merchant-guidelines). | Read a small, logged sample: 5 most recent, 5 low-rating, 5 high-rating, and 5 with a useful photo/detail across 3 sellers (up to 60 reviews). Record seller, product/variant, date and whether verified status is visible. Code exact themes (fit, dimensions, quality, setup, shipping, value); count only this sample, not as category rates. |
| 8 | Separate stated liking from a real purchase at a real price. | One field experiment in a different market (172 asthma patients) found 38% hypothetical stated purchase intent against 12% actual enrollment across the tested price conditions; its good, service and population differ, so the gap is not a correction factor for apparel or home goods. It supports caution about “would buy,” not a universal haircut. [Blumenschein, Johannesson, Yokoyama and Freeman field experiment](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-6296(01)00075-3). | Keep interviews for discovering language and objections. Next offer an honest sample-backed product at one viable public price with normal checkout/fulfilment and a clear “none” option. Track paid orders, cancellations, refunds and retained contribution separately from emails, likes and hypothetical choices. |
| 9 | Let unit economics screen out attractive but unviable products before demand tests. | The existing crew and tee costing in the dossier is a useful starting input, not a validated whole-order margin. The crew source cost is already £31.53 plus £3.78 shipping; a £72 test price therefore leaves limited room for payment fees, packaging, returns, taxes where applicable, promotion and founder time. [Dossier brief](https://silmoon04.github.io/ecommerce-ai-seo-research/profit-playbook/assumptions.md). | Calculate a conservative contribution per delivered order for each candidate: customer price less tax treatment, goods, inbound/outbound postage, packaging, payment fees, expected return/refund allowance, marketplace fees and variable acquisition cost. Mark each input measured, quoted, or scenario. Reject or redesign if the viable price is implausible against observed substitutes. |
| 10 | Choose small-inventory tests that can be sold as a narrow, coherent offer and whose buyer question can be answered from reliable product facts. | This is an operating hypothesis. Fit, dimensions, version compatibility, materials and actual stock are facts an AI assistant or guide can compare; that does not establish AI referral volume, checkout conversion or merchant eligibility. Etsy reported U.S. agentic shopping partnerships in Jan 2026 for select Etsy items, but that corporate announcement is not evidence for UK independent-store demand or any specific category. [Etsy SEC filing](https://investors.etsy.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001370637-26-000017/0001370637-26-000017.pdf). | For shortlisted SKUs, answer ten realistic buyer questions using only published/verified facts. If a buyer can’t determine “will this fit my room/game/trip/size?”, improve the offer data and test whether it leads to more qualified product-page actions or sales. Keep AI-origin visits/orders distinct only when the analytics can actually identify them; do not infer channel attribution from a creator or a generic referral. |

## Three contrasting category hypotheses

These are intentionally different buying jobs. All are physical, can begin with a sample and one to three variants, and avoid health, electrical, child-safety or ingestible claims. Their demand is unverified. “AI-assisted buying fit” below means the buyer has answerable constraints that a grounded comparison can help resolve; it is not a claim that AI currently sends meaningful traffic.

| Category to test | Narrow first offer | Why it may suit AI-assisted product comparison | Main disconfirming evidence / risk | Cheap test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premium subtle-fandom apparel | One sampled heavyweight crew or tee, one decoration method, 2–3 original motifs, small colour/size range. | Buyers weigh reference recognition, everyday styling, garment weight/composition, fit and price together. A complete size chart and close-up sample facts could answer real comparison questions. | Licensed substitutes have stronger recognition and can price lower; “subtle” may be too obscure, while original art may not carry enough fandom meaning. Fit returns and apparel inventory fragment by size. Earlier research has not proved an unknown label can achieve £72/£45. | Show the actual sample, model/body measurements, exact price and delivery. Recruit fashion-first and fandom-first buyers separately, include “none” and existing alternatives; take paid orders only when ready to fulfil. |
| Board-game organization and table accessories | One edition-specific insert/token tray or portable component case for one named game, made only after a fit sample. | The choice is constraint-heavy: exact edition/box dimensions, sleeved-card fit, player count, setup time, storage orientation and table footprint. A comparison assistant can ask which edition and sleeves the buyer has, then shortlist compatible designs. | Compatibility errors cause returns; every title/edition creates SKU sprawl; custom prints/branding can raise IP or marketplace-policy issues. Etsy search/listing competition is not sales. Don’t use protected game art or imply official status. | Interview 8–10 owners of the same game about storage/setup pain; measure their boxes/components; prototype one insert/case; test a clearly edition-specific page against “buy nothing” and existing organizers at a real price. Stop if compatibility cannot be verified consistently or margin requires too much machining/shipping. |
| Non-electric home-office cable and desk organization | One small, non-powered cable-routing or under-desk organization kit with measured dimensions and reversible fitting options. | Buyers need to check desk thickness, mounting method, cable count/diameter, desk material and whether the item can be removed without damage. These attributes lend themselves to a guided chooser and comparison table. | Commodity price pressure, cheap substitutes, adhesive failure, and room/desk variation. High apparent category search volume may be general research rather than purchase intent. Avoid powered products or unsupported damage-free claims. | Read low and high reviews for 3–5 comparable organizers, code adhesive/fit/routing complaints, measure a sample against common desk constraints, and interview users with photos/measurements. Test one kit with a compatibility checklist and honest fitting limitations. |
| Travel packing and small-item organization | A compact packing pouch/cable-and-document organizer set (non-electrical, no claims about security or protection), limited to two sizes/materials. | Buyers compare dimensions, capacity, compartment layout, material, weight and use case (carry-on, day bag, work commute). A preference/packing-list assistant can ask what items and bag size they carry, then recommend a measured configuration. | Highly substitutable commodity listings, bundle complexity, low price ceilings, and material/zipper durability concerns. Search spikes can be seasonal or caused by travel news. | Inspect the same 20–30 common carry items across comparable product dimensions; sample and pack them; collect review complaints about real capacity and zipper/material life. Put exact internal dimensions and contents diagram on one offer and seek paid orders before adding variants. |

## How to run a compact product screen

1. **Set the constraints first.** Write the maximum cash tied up in samples, inventory and shipping; target contribution per order; acceptable fulfilment burden; and maximum number of variants. These are founder choices, not market norms. No candidate should advance because a trend chart looks exciting if the math already fails.
2. **Use a three-source evidence sheet.** For every candidate capture (a) search language and direction from Google Trends/Keyword Planner or native marketplace data, (b) competing live offers and public customer feedback, and (c) direct evidence from the target buyer, progressing from observed purchase to refundable/paid commitment. Label each item as measured, seller-claimed, sampled, or analyst hypothesis.
3. **Sample the offer, not the category.** Record the precise product, variant, audience, delivered price, delivery promise, source and unit cost, contribution assumption, sample size, objections, and next test. Keep a “no purchase” response available. Don’t treat email signups, likes, bestseller badges or sold-out notices as paid demand.
4. **Make decisions at the level of evidence collected.** A small sample can expose a bad fit, confusing description or weak price. It cannot size the whole market or prove a repeatable sales channel. Repeat a promising offer with fresh, non-friend buyers before raising inventory. A failed test can mean poor access, weak presentation or wrong price as well as weak demand; diagnose those separately before ordering stock.

## What would change this recommendation

Advance a category if a narrow, accurately specified offer can meet its contribution target, real target customers choose it over named substitutes at the stated delivered price, and a small fulfilment test does not surface an unacceptable product or service failure. Reject or reframe it if the only positive evidence is broad audience size, relative search interest, a viral clip, hypothetical “I’d buy,” competitor sales badges, or listings with no evidence of sell-through. For apparel, a failed motif test should not automatically condemn the garment or brand; repeat with a different original motif only if interviews identify a concrete meaning/wearability mismatch. For the other categories, stop when the compatibility or sizing burden makes accurate fulfilment too costly for the available contribution.
