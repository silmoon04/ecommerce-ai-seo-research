# A profit-focused path from an owned store to ecommerce AEO SaaS

**Research snapshot: 30 September 2026.** This is a set of commercial hypotheses and evidence gates, not a forecast or proof that a market exists. AEO/GEO here means work intended to make product and store facts usable by answer engines and to observe resulting visibility. Public vendor prices and feature descriptions establish what is offered; they do not establish vendor profit, gross margin, merchant ROI or willingness to pay. No merchant interviews, paid pilots or customer sales have yet been conducted for this project. Do not describe demand as validated.

## Recommendation

Start with a paid concierge audit and implementation pilot for a narrow, inspectable job: find consequential product-source conflicts or missing verified attributes across Shopify, rendered product pages, structured data and a merchant's chosen feed/channel; show the source evidence; get a named merchant owner to approve changes; then check that the intended values actually publish. Price the first offer after scoping delivery hours and customer costs, as a fixed fee for an agreed catalog sample and outcome. Do not invent a market-clearing rate. Ask for payment before doing the work. A free, tightly bounded sample can earn access to a budget owner, but free full audits mostly test curiosity and consume the very human time whose economics must be known.

Do not launch a generic free SaaS tier yet. The Shopify App Store already lists several free or low-cost apps that promise catalog scans, AI visibility, fixes, prompt tracking, reports and attribution. A no-cost account may recruit installs while failing to distinguish willingness to pay from novelty. If self-serve later becomes justified, keep the free action cheap, bounded and useful for qualification, such as one read-only sample report, with ongoing monitoring, multi-market history, integrations or approved repair in a paid tier. Measure whether that path produces paid activation and renewal, not installs.

Treat the owned clothing store as a research lab for product facts, page rendering, test workflows and its own orders. Its small original catalog and one operator cannot establish that independent merchants share the problem, will pay, can authorize data reuse, or will renew. A paid external pilot with an independent merchant is the transfer test. Do not claim a moat from the store's own data; a defensible evidence asset would require explicit rights to retain and reuse de-identified corrections, provenance patterns or aggregate failure rates across unrelated stores.

## Commercial levers to test

| # | Lever | Initial operating choice and evidence gate |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Sell a job before a dashboard | Offer a scoped, paid audit plus one approved fix and recheck. Record payment, buyer role, hours, unresolved issues, and whether the merchant asks for a second job. If buyers value only a one-off cleanup, retain project/service economics instead of forcing a subscription. No current customer evidence supports a price or repeat rate. |
| 2 | Target a costly trigger | Qualify on a recent launch, feed mismatch, variant error, catalog migration or failed channel listing. Ask for a concrete recent case and its current workaround. Do not sell a vague promise to “rank in AI.” The competitor teardown finds basic readiness scores and fixes are already offered by Shopify-native mapping and current apps; cross-layer conflict and verified repair is only a gap hypothesis. |
| 3 | Make the audit actionable | Deliver a small list with exact product/variant, observed output, source field, evidence, consequence, owner and proposed correction. Merchant approval precedes write access; follow with a recheck. Compare findings against Shopify Catalog Mapping, Merchant Center diagnostics and the incumbent feed tool. Stop if the value is mostly rephrasing information those tools already expose. |
| 4 | Use a paid pilot, not a free consulting funnel | Ask for a signed scope and payment for a fixed sample before doing full analysis. If necessary, use one short redacted example as a qualification artifact, with a cap on products and labor. Log conversion from qualified conversation to payment and hours before/after access. Free comprehensive audits create selection and cost bias. This is a proposed test, not established best practice for this niche. |
| 5 | Charge by value and workload, not raw AI-check count alone | Pilot fixed scope by catalog complexity and verified work; test later pricing against merchant value, support load, destinations and store count. Competitor tiers are observable anchors, not WTP evidence: Kedra lists Free, $25, $79 and $199 per month; AIVisible lists $10, $29, $64 plus $0.25 per extra audit/check; Peec's official page uses prompt/model/project capacity with self-serve and custom Enterprise. These are asking prices, not realized sales or proof customers accept them. |
| 6 | Keep free audits cheaper than delivery | A single read-only sample should reveal enough to qualify, then stop before open-ended interpretation, implementation or follow-up. Calculate fully loaded time per audit, including access setup, source checking, support and reporting. Free remains only if a measured share converts and its acquisition cost per paid pilot stays below an explicit ceiling. Current public competitors show abundant free scans, so matching them feature-for-feature is unlikely to differentiate. |
| 7 | Prove activation by time-to-first-useful-fix | Define activation as a merchant viewing an evidence-backed issue, approving a correction, and seeing the target surface pass a recheck. Track elapsed time and merchant minutes from install/access to that verified result. Avoid vanity milestones such as connection, a score, generated prose or first scan. If value requires founder interpretation every time, that is a service with a software aid until repeatability improves. |
| 8 | Build retention around a recurring event | Retention needs a continuing task: new-season supplier attributes, repeated catalog changes, new destinations, or regressions that incumbents miss. During a pilot, replay recent changes and ask users to identify which alerts they would act on. Record useful alerts, false positives, time spent and a paid second period. If the work happens only at migration, sell a project or periodic re-audit instead of assuming monthly renewal. |
| 9 | Bound inference and retrieval expense | Start with deterministic checks, cached snapshots and a small, merchant-approved test set. Invoke models only for cases where extraction or classification adds measurable operator time saved; validate outputs against source documents. Record calls, retries, tokens/API charges and human review by merchant and task. Price limits should follow measured per-account variable cost plus delivery/support, not token-cost intuition. Competitor usage limits and overages show vendors package compute, but disclose no unit economics. |
| 10 | Separate score monitoring from merchant outcomes | Report answer snapshots, crawler requests, citations/referrals, sessions, orders and retained contribution as different measures. A repeated prompt result is a synthetic observation, not a shopper impression. Attribute only what the merchant's source supports; use a control or staggered change before causal claims. Sell operational correctness and time saved first, since visibility-to-order proof may be sparse or unavailable. |
| 11 | Secure evidence and data rights early | Collect only the catalog, rendered pages and channel results needed for the agreed job. Record source, timestamp, rights basis, tenant boundary and retention rule for every artifact. Default to tenant-scoped processing; do not train shared models or publish cross-merchant benchmarks without explicit permission. A potential moat is a rights-cleared, correction-labeled corpus of real source-to-output failures and validated repairs. Its existence and value are unproven until multiple independent merchants permit reuse and it improves repair accuracy or speed. |
| 12 | Make support cost visible | Measure onboarding, OAuth/API failures, catalog identity matching, merchant review, billing questions and incident handling per account. Use read-only access first and safe preview/rollback for writes. Include support hours in contribution economics alongside hosting, third-party API/model spend, payment fees and any agency delivery. A low list price cannot support bespoke onboarding unless merchants or agencies pay for it. Public competitor plan features do not reveal their support expense. |
| 13 | Choose a route to buyers that matches the work | Test direct founder-led qualification with a few independent merchants or agencies only when outreach is separately authorized; no outreach is part of this research assignment. Compare Shopify App Store discovery later with agency multi-store workflow only after one repeatable task is proven. Shopify requires Shopify-provided billing for public App Store apps and documents 2.9% processing, 0% revenue share on the first $1M lifetime gross app revenue under the standard eligible program, then 15%; refunds do not reduce the gross-sales share. Include these fees and app distribution/support obligations in pricing. |
| 14 | Set a continuation gate before coding | Continue from concierge toward a narrow internal tool only after multiple independent merchants pay for substantially the same diagnosis, approve a repeatable workflow, and the measured manual cost leaves room for software contribution after support and inference. Seek repeat purchase or a second paid period, not verbal enthusiasm. Stop or change segment if each store needs unique consulting, no one can grant adequate data access, or native tools solve the issue. Exact counts/thresholds should be chosen from attainable buyer access and delivery capacity; there is no evidence-backed universal number here. |
| 15 | Do not infer competitor profitability or market WTP | Official app listings are strong evidence of current published offers, plan boundaries and review counts, but reviews are self-selected and listing claims are vendor-authored. Peec's public 2026 release and private-company ARR/funding claims from the sibling dossier are not audited profit evidence. Do not estimate competitor margin, retention, CAC, customer lifetime value, or the project's acceptable price from these disclosures. |

## Competitor evidence and what it means

The current Shopify app listings show fast commoditization at the feature surface. **Kedra** lists a free tier and $25/$79/$199 monthly plans, including prompt tracking, fixes and catalog features; its App Store page showed 47 reviews and a 4.9 rating when retrieved. **AIVisible** lists $10/$29/$64 plans and $0.25 usage units; its listing showed five reviews and a 5.0 rating. **Geoly** lists free through $199 tiers and reports no reviews; **Cited** lists free, $29 and $79 tiers and no reviews in its July 2026 launch listing. This mixed review count illustrates early category activity, not adoption share, retention or revenue. Do not use review volume as a market-size proxy. [Kedra listing](https://apps.shopify.com/kedra-ai-index) · [AIVisible listing](https://apps.shopify.com/aivisible-ai-geo-aeo) · [Geoly listing](https://apps.shopify.com/geoly-ai-visibility-for-shop) · [Cited listing](https://apps.shopify.com/beacon-5)

The established analytics category signals a more expensive prompt-tracking workload: Peec AI's official pricing page structures plans around tracked prompts, selected models, projects and tracking frequency, with Enterprise custom. The page retrieved in this research did not expose exact prices in its text, so this note does not quote them. That product packaging is evidence of a cost/capacity axis, not a demonstrated margin model. [Peec official pricing](https://peec.ai/pricing)

The sibling competitor teardown recommends a focused cross-system discrepancy workflow rather than another generic “AI readiness” score: compare Shopify source values, rendered page/schema and downstream representation, with approval and verification. Shopify itself offers catalog mapping, while Google provides product diagnostics and automated item updates. A SaaS entrant must show the merchant a recent problem those systems miss, or partner with/complement those native systems. [Sibling competitor opportunity design](https://silmoon04.github.io/ecommerce-ai-seo-research/deep-dive/opportunity-design.md) · [Shopify Catalog Mapping](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-catalog/mapping) · [Google product automations](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/15623993?hl=en)

Shopify's App Store is a possible distribution route, not free economics. Shopify's documentation states a $19 one-time partner registration fee, 2.9% billing processing and, for standard eligible developers, a 0% share on the first $1M gross app revenue earned since 1 January 2025 followed by a 15% share. Revenue share is based on gross sales and ignores refunds; other eligibility tiers can differ. Public apps must use Shopify's billing system. [Revenue-share rules](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share) · [App billing requirements](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/billing)

The competitor research found no primary public disclosure establishing audited gross margin, operating profit, retention cohorts, inference cost or support cost for the named AEO vendors. Vendor ARR, funding, headcount, list prices and testimonials must not be turned into profit or customer ROI claims. See the sibling [profitability mechanisms note](https://silmoon04.github.io/ecommerce-ai-seo-research/deep-dive/profitability-mechanisms.md).

## Evidence gates for the next stage

1. **Problem gate:** an independent merchant shares a recent, inspectable case and names the person who can authorize a correction and pay for resolving it.
2. **Paid-pilot gate:** that merchant pays for a bounded sample and approved recheck. Record the exact task, scope, hours, direct costs, findings already surfaced by current tools, corrections accepted and outcome. Do not count a free audit as payment evidence.
3. **Repeatability gate:** a second independent merchant has the same job and the workflow needs materially less bespoke interpretation. Keep the merchants and evidence separate; one friendly pilot does not establish transfer.
4. **Economics gate:** model contribution per paid account after payment/platform share, infrastructure, retrieval/model spend, onboarding, support, review and refunds/credits. Label every unknown; use observed pilot data instead of invented vendor benchmarks.
5. **Retention gate:** secure a second paid cycle or repeated purchase tied to a real recurring event. If only one-time projects renew, offer periodic service rather than monthly monitoring.
6. **Moat gate:** verify that at least more than one merchant has granted the relevant data rights and that reuse measurably improves detection or repair. Until then, call it a moat hypothesis.

The owned shop can test whether our chosen product facts are accurate, whether changes render as intended and whether our own operational records reconcile. Its original dark-fantasy apparel catalog is too small and distinctive to stand in for an independent merchant's catalog, buyer mix, channel stack, support needs, purchase frequency or software budget. It can generate a method and test fixtures; only external paid use can support transfer and WTP claims.
