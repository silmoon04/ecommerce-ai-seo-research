# Practical measurement for small ecommerce experiments

Date: 30 September 2026. Scope: a small UK ecommerce operator making many low-cost decisions across physical products, AI-assisted discovery and eventual ecommerce software. This is an operating guide, not a claim about the store’s current traffic or conversion rate. No universal CVR, CTR, refund-rate or AI-referral benchmark is asserted. Shopify, GA4 and Clarity counts will differ; preserve their definitions and use order records for commercial outcomes.

## Actionable principles

1. **Start with an auditable outcome ledger, not a dashboard target.** Keep one row per Shopify order (or export) with order ID, created date, product/variant, quantity, discounts, shipping, tax, payment status, cancellation date, refund/reversal amount and reason, fulfilment cost, actual product/packaging cost, payment fee and channel evidence. Treat order/payment reports as different views: Shopify sales reports record sales and reversals on their respective processing dates, include canceled, pending and unpaid orders, exclude test/deleted orders, and are not a cash-received ledger. Reconcile bank/payout and payment finance records separately. [Shopify sales reports](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/shopify-reports/report-types/default-reports/sales-report) [Shopify sales discrepancies](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/discrepancies/sales-discrepancies)

2. **Define contribution before calling a variant a winner.** At order level use `Net merchandise revenue = item revenue - discounts - merchandise reversals` and `Contribution before acquisition = net merchandise revenue + retained shipping revenue - product/decoration cost - fulfilment/postage - packaging - payment fees - refund/return handling`. Add tax/duties as separate pass-through lines according to the business’s accounting treatment; do not silently count them as margin. Then `Contribution per eligible session = total contribution / eligible sessions`, and `Contribution per order = total contribution / paid, non-test orders`. If paid traffic is later used, subtract spend to get contribution after acquisition. The formula is a decision model; fill inputs from actual invoices, Shopify/payment records and observed returns, and label any future refund or cost assumptions as scenarios. Shopify defines net sales as gross sales less discounts and sales reversals, while sales reports include more than money actually received. [Shopify sales reports](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/shopify-reports/report-types/default-reports/sales-report)

3. **Use a small, stable event taxonomy.** Minimum useful funnel events are `view_item`, `add_to_cart`, `begin_checkout`, `add_shipping_info`, `add_payment_info`, `purchase`, and `refund`; include `item_id`/variant, currency, value, quantity and `transaction_id` where relevant. `select_promotion` or `search` is useful only when a decision depends on it. GA4 recommends ecommerce events, but they are not sent automatically; use the prescribed names/parameters so reports work. Shopify’s own online-store conversion report provides a built-in session funnel from landing to cart, checkout and completed checkout. [GA4 recommended events](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9267735?hl=en) [Shopify marketing performance](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/promoting-marketing/analyze-marketing/marketing-performance)

4. **Treat Shopify as the commercial source of truth and GA4 as an instrumented journey view.** Compare weekly counts of paid, non-test orders and net sales between Shopify and GA4; investigate directionally large differences, but do not “correct” one system to the other without tracing causes. Consent choices, blockers, browser restrictions, checkout boundaries and implementation errors can lose browser events. Record the reporting window, timezone, currency, order-status definition, refund cutoff and session definition with every comparison. Shopify itself warns that session and conversion metrics can change after methodology changes while total orders/sales do not. [Shopify session-measurement update](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/discrepancies/session-measurement-update) [GA4 ecommerce](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/14430645?hl=en)

5. **Prevent duplicate and orphan purchase events.** Fire `purchase` only after confirmed checkout completion; pass the unique order identifier as GA4 `transaction_id`, never blank and never shared across users. GA4 deduplicates web purchase events with the same transaction ID, and requires it for correct refund processing. If both browser and server/Measurement Protocol send the purchase, use the same valid transaction ID and verify the implementation in DebugView/exports; don't assume arbitrary events dedupe. A back-office daily comparison catches missing or duplicated records that event QA misses. [GA4 transaction ID deduplication](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12313109?hl=en) [GA4 purchase and refund events](https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/collection/ga4/ecommerce)

6. **Record refunds, cancellations and returns by transaction, then mature the cohort.** A purchase conversion is not the same as retained profitable demand. Send a GA4 `refund` with the original `transaction_id`, item ID and quantity (and value where supported); record full/partial refund, cancellation, return reason, replacement, chargeback and date in the order ledger. Calculate `refund/reversal rate = refunded or reversed paid orders / eligible paid orders` and `retained contribution rate = matured cohort contribution / matured cohort gross contribution before reversals`. For decisions made before a return window has elapsed, mark results provisional and show the observation age. Shopify sales reports include reversals on the date processed, and distinguish sales movement from payments. [GA4 refunds](https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/collection/ga4/ecommerce) [Shopify sales reports](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/shopify-reports/report-types/default-reports/sales-report)

7. **Keep tracking consent-aware; do not assume “analytics” means exempt.** The ICO’s final UK guidance (29 April 2026) applies PECR to cookies and broader storage/access methods including pixels, scripts/tags, web storage and link decoration; exceptions exist, including a statistical-purposes exception, but whether a particular vendor/configuration qualifies depends on the purpose and conditions. Check the current ICO exception and UK GDPR obligations for the actual setup rather than treating GA4 or Clarity as automatically exempt. Configure the consent mechanism before optional tags, test reject/withdraw paths, disclose purposes, and retain a record of the configuration. Microsoft says Clarity Consent Mode waits for a valid signal before setting cookies, and that EEA/UK/Swiss visits require a valid consent signal for full functionality from 31 October 2025. [ICO final guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/) [ICO guidance update](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/04/final-storage-and-access-technologies-guidance-published/) [Microsoft Clarity Consent Mode](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/clarity/setup-and-installation/consent-mode)

8. **Use Clarity to find friction, not estimate conversion impact.** After consent is correctly handled, use a small, privacy-reviewed set of recordings/heatmaps to identify concrete usability problems: size/fit information missed, shipping uncertainty, errors, dead clicks, or a confusing checkout transition. Translate each observation into an issue hypothesis, fix obvious defects, then check whether the issue recurs. A handful of recordings can reveal a failure mode, but they do not estimate how common it is or prove that a redesign raises sales. Mask sensitive fields and avoid collecting payment or personal data; do not use replay as a substitute for order reconciliation. [Microsoft Clarity Consent Mode](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/clarity/setup-and-installation/consent-mode) [Clarity overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/clarity/)

9. **Make acquisition labels reproducible and keep “unknown” honest.** For each owned post, email, creator link or partner placement, use consistent UTMs such as `utm_source=instagram`, `utm_medium=organic_social`, `utm_campaign=design_name_yyyymm`, `utm_content=asset_or_link`. Maintain a campaign sheet with the exact destination URL, publication date and post ID. Test redirects and checkout so UTMs survive. GA4 says missing UTMs, redirects/shorteners, direct entry and blockers can leave traffic as `(direct)/(none)`; Shopify lists proxy/firewall, Do Not Track and shortened URLs as reasons a referrer is unknown. Never relabel unknown/direct as “AI” or “social” to make channel reporting look complete. [GA4 direct traffic](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/15258820?hl=en) [GA4 URL builder](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10917952?hl=en) [Shopify marketing reports](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/shopify-reports/report-types/default-reports/marketing-reports)

10. **Measure AI-assisted discovery as a triangulated, imperfect signal.** Separate (a) observed referrers such as `chatgpt.com` when passed, (b) tagged links you control, and (c) voluntary post-purchase “How did you first hear about us?” answers with an “AI assistant/search” option and optional free text. Report each count and denominator separately; allow multiple influences and “not sure.” Referrer captures the click that arrived, not necessarily the assistant that shaped earlier discovery; copied URLs and screenshots often arrive as direct/unknown. Self-report is subject to recall and response bias. Use identifiable referral plus self-report to form hypotheses or learn language, not to claim total AI-attributed orders or channel ROI. Shopify and GA4 both document how source information can be absent or model-dependent. [Shopify referrer definitions](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/shopify-reports/report-types/default-reports/marketing-reports) [GA4 traffic-source dimensions](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/11080067?hl=en)

11. **Match the method to the decision’s evidence needs.** Use qualitative/operational tests for low-cost, reversible questions: five-person usability sessions, customer interviews, sample garment wash/fit checks, supplier lead-time trials, proof-of-concept AI outputs, checkout QA, or a one-week manual workflow. Predefine what counts as an observed issue, task completion, cost or failure; keep notes by participant/order, not an invented percentage. Use aggregate directional tracking for funnel breakage, customer questions, support load, and order economics. Reserve randomized conversion tests for a high-stakes change where enough eligible traffic exists to resolve a business-relevant effect. A tiny store can run many experiments, but most can be decision probes or operational checks rather than underpowered split tests. Controlled experiments establish causal effects when designed and measured soundly; their sample size and duration depend on baseline variation and effect size. [Kohavi et al., controlled experiments survey](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1) [Kohavi et al., experimentation at scale lessons](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/online-experimentation-at-microsoft/)

12. **Before an A/B test, power it for the smallest effect worth acting on.** Lock the primary outcome, eligible unit (usually unique eligible visitor/session), assignment rule, minimum detectable absolute difference (MDE), analysis window and guardrails before exposure. For a binary outcome, approximate equal-size sample per arm using baseline `p0`, planned absolute MDE `d`, `p1=p0+d`, `pbar=(p0+p1)/2`, two-sided `alpha`, and desired power:

   `n/arm ≈ (NORM.S.INV(1-alpha/2)*SQRT(2*pbar*(1-pbar)) + NORM.S.INV(power)*SQRT(p0*(1-p0)+p1*(1-p1)))^2 / d^2`

   Spreadsheet sketch (Excel/Sheets): put `p0` in B2, `d` in B3, `alpha` in B4 (for example 0.05 as an explicit convention), `power` in B5 (for example 0.80 as a planning convention), then `=ROUNDUP((NORM.S.INV(1-B4/2)*SQRT(2*((B2+B2+B3)/2)*(1-(B2+B2+B3)/2))+NORM.S.INV(B5)*SQRT(B2*(1-B2)+(B2+B3)*(1-B2-B3)))^2/B3^2,0)`. This is an approximation for two independent proportions, not a guarantee; use a stats package for final design, especially for small counts, repeated users, multiple variants or clustered assignment. `weeks ≈ 2*n_per_arm / eligible_sessions_per_week`; if that horizon is commercially pointless or beyond the available traffic, don't quietly lower the MDE after seeing results—use another method or accept that the answer will remain uncertain. [Kohavi et al., open-access experiment survey](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1)

13. **Randomize exposure and analyze by assigned group; avoid time-split “A then B.”** Assign each eligible visitor at first exposure with a persistent, consent-appropriate random assignment and keep that variant across visits. Compare intention-to-treat outcomes for assigned groups; do not compare people who clicked, scrolled or purchased after seeing a version. A before/after switch confounds day-of-week, traffic source, season, stock and outside events. If the platform cannot hold a stable random assignment, do matched operational pilots and label them non-causal. Check that assigned counts match the planned split and that each arm received the intended variant; if assignment or logging is broken, stop and repair before interpreting outcomes. Randomization is what supports causal inference, while trustworthy online experimentation also requires sound measurement and checks. [Kohavi et al., controlled experiments survey](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1) [Kohavi et al., experimentation at scale lessons](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/online-experimentation-at-microsoft/)

14. **Use holdouts only when there is a persistent unit and enough volume.** For a campaign or feature expected to affect repeat customers, assign a small eligible cohort to “not exposed” only if you can retain the assignment and measure outcomes across the same window; use customer/order-level assignment where visitors can return on another device, otherwise describe the limitation. A small holdout reduces the exposed sample and can be more costly than the uncertainty it resolves. For a one-off low-volume launch, compare contribution and qualitative response against a stated forecast/control period, disclose the confounding, and do not call it incremental lift. Consider holdouts later for recurring email, paid media, recommendation features or SaaS onboarding where customer IDs and repeat outcomes exist. The experimentation literature notes that design and power must fit the unit and context; marketplace and repeated-measure settings need special handling. [Kohavi et al., online controlled experiments survey](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1) [Kohavi et al., repeated-measures experimentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.00450)

15. **Pre-register a stop rule and report intervals, not “winner” theater.** Stop for a broken checkout, privacy issue, severe contribution/return guardrail breach or stock/fulfilment failure. Otherwise run to the planned sample and include complete business cycles (at least full weekly patterns where feasible), then report arm counts, conversion, absolute difference, confidence interval, contribution/order, and immature refund exposure. Don't peek daily and stop on whichever version is temporarily ahead; don't run many unplanned metrics/variants and select a lucky result. If the test ends short, mark it inconclusive and combine it with qualitative/operational evidence; do not equate “not significant” with “same.” Use holdout/new tests to confirm only where the expected value justifies the time and traffic. Microsoft’s primary experiment papers discuss power, metric choice, test duration and puzzling outcomes; these risks grow when many metrics are explored. [Kohavi et al., five puzzling outcomes](https://ai.stanford.edu/~ronnyk/puzzlingOutcomesInControlledExperiments.pdf) [Kohavi et al., controlled experiments survey](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1)

## Minimum useful instrumentation and sequence

1. **Commercial ledger:** Shopify order and payment exports; stable order/line-item IDs; actual costs and shipping; cancellation, refund/reversal and reason; test-order marker. Reconcile orders to Shopify and payment payouts weekly.
2. **Privacy gate:** inventory every cookie, pixel, script, tag and link-decoration mechanism; check ICO’s current PECR/UK GDPR guidance and each vendor’s consent mode. Verify a fresh visitor’s reject, accept and withdrawal behavior. Keep optional trackers off until the configuration permits them.
3. **Funnel:** establish GA4 property and consent behavior; validate `view_item → add_to_cart → begin_checkout → purchase` plus transaction ID and a refund path. Check test order once, then exclude test transactions from decision data.
4. **Baseline:** collect a stable post-instrumentation period without changing page, pricing, campaign mix or definitions. Review raw counts and missingness, not just percentages. Shopify’s session-measurement rollout on 21–23 September 2026 changes session-based comparisons; use post-update data for a new session baseline and compare orders/sales separately. [Shopify update](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/discrepancies/session-measurement-update)
5. **Decision queue:** choose one uncertainty; classify it as qualitative, operational, directional or causal; name the metric and guardrail; estimate effort/cost and the minimum evidence needed; run only the suitable method. Log result, limits, next action and whether it changes the decision.

## Spreadsheet metrics to start with

| Metric | Formula | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| Product view to cart | distinct sessions with `add_to_cart` / distinct sessions with `view_item` | Funnel diagnostic, not profit |
| Checkout start to order | paid non-test orders / distinct sessions with `begin_checkout` | Checkout friction signal; reconcile platform definitions |
| Session purchase rate | paid non-test orders / eligible sessions | Directional unless randomized and adequately powered |
| Average order value | net merchandise revenue / paid non-test orders | Exclude tax/shipping unless explicitly shown |
| Return/reversal rate | paid orders with any refund/cancel/reversal / paid non-test orders | Mature cohorts; label partial vs full reversals |
| Contribution per order | total order contribution / paid non-test orders | Commercial outcome before or after acquisition, clearly labelled |
| Contribution per session | total order contribution / eligible sessions | Useful north-star candidate once costs and sessions reconcile |
| Tagged-source order share | orders with retained tagged source / all paid non-test orders | Under-counts unattributed and dark-social influence |
| AI signal share (reported) | orders whose voluntary answer includes AI / orders with a usable answer | Self-selected, not channel attribution |

For every rate show numerator, denominator, time period and definition. At low counts, prefer counts and ranges over decimals with false precision: `1 order / 23 eligible sessions` is more honest and useful than presenting `4.35%` as a stable store conversion rate.

## Evidence limits and what would change this guidance

- The business has no reported sales or validated demand yet, so the guide supplies measurement choices, not expected outcomes or economic forecasts.
- Shopify/GA4 reports are product-specific documentation; exact data availability depends on plan, storefront architecture, consent, tags and report configuration. Verify the actual store’s reports and event stream before using a metric.
- ICO final storage-and-access guidance is new as of April 2026 and describes exceptions whose eligibility depends on conditions. This note is not a legal determination for a specific tag stack; get qualified advice if the setup relies on an exception or sensitive processing.
- AI assistant referral strings and browser behavior can change. Recheck observed referrers and do not infer missing exposure from source labels.
- The sample-size calculation assumes independent binary outcomes and equal allocation. Repeat visits, conversion-rate shifts by source, low counts, multiple looks, novelty, interference and delayed refunds all weaken that approximation. If real traffic is too low, use user interviews, usability, garment/sample checks, operational pilots, price/offer tests with actual commitment when appropriate, and recorded customer questions to reduce uncertainty; don't portray them as causal lift estimates.
- I would revise the sequence if a functioning order ledger, stable consented analytics, meaningful repeated traffic, or a reliable customer identity/holdout capability already exists. I would also revisit ICO handling if guidance changes or a specific analytics statistical-purposes exception is confirmed for the exact deployment.
