# AI commerce: discovery inputs, eligibility and measurement

**Checked:** 30 September 2026. This note uses current first-party Google, Microsoft/Bing, OpenAI and Anthropic documentation, with local research checked through 28 September. Product rollouts and access rules change quickly; confirm them again before implementation. “AI commerce” covers several different surfaces: ordinary web search with generated answers, shopping catalogs, AI web search, and checkout inside an assistant. There is no single universal product index or merchant control panel.

## What a merchant can influence

Merchants can improve whether product facts are accessible, understandable, complete, current, and eligible for a surface. They can also make the human-facing offer more persuasive and reduce purchase uncertainty. Search and shopping systems decide what queries trigger product answers, which sources/products are relevant, how offers are compared, and what is displayed. Eligibility, a crawler visit, a feed acceptance, a citation, or a product mention is not a ranking promise or evidence of sales.

Google says its generative search features draw on Search fundamentals, may use query fan-out, and require no special AI schema. Product data in Merchant Center and useful product-page structured data can help Google understand and present products, but rich results are discretionary and indexing/serving is not guaranteed. Microsoft exposes aggregate AI citation reporting and product-discovery freshness tools, not an answer-position control. OpenAI's shopping results are independently selected against shopper intent/context; feeds supply product facts, while Instant Checkout is a separately enabled and approved integration. Anthropic provides opt-in robots for training, user-directed retrieval, and search; those are access choices, not a merchant shopping-feed or recommendation switch. [Google AI Search guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), [Google Product structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product), [Bing AI Performance](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2026/2/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview/), [OpenAI shopping help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search), [Anthropic crawler controls](https://support.anthropic.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler).

## Practical levers worth testing

These are operational hypotheses, not claimed algorithm weights. Prioritize factual defects with direct customer or operational cost before speculative “AI optimization.”

1. **Make product pages fetchable and indexable.** Check public HTTPS URLs, useful server responses, canonical URLs, crawlable links, sitemap coverage, and accidental robots/noindex blocks. Make essential product facts available in rendered text, not only in a client-side interaction. This supports ordinary search and AI web retrieval; it does not force crawling or inclusion. For a small apparel store, verify each size/color page resolves to the intended variant. For SaaS, keep pricing, plans, limits, and docs pages publicly accessible when they are meant to be discoverable. [Google crawling overview](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing), [Google recrawl guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/ask-google-to-recrawl).

2. **Use correct product and variant structure.** Add truthful `Product` / `Offer` data for purchasable products and stable identifiers for each true size/color or model variant. Keep visible page values in agreement with markup. Google says product markup and Merchant Center can be used together; variants can clarify related selections. Validate with official diagnostics. This is about machine-readable facts and rich-result eligibility, not special AEO schema. [Google Product structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product).

3. **Keep offer facts synchronized across page and feed.** For each item, reconcile title, brand, seller, selected variant URL, image, currency, current price, stock, shipping, and returns. Google can combine page and Merchant Center facts; OpenAI describes prices and merchant data arriving from feeds/providers and warns that updates can lag. A simple scheduled mismatch report may be more valuable than new AI copy. Apparel: feed garment color/size and real stock. Functional goods: model compatibility and pack quantity. SaaS: use the product/plan surface only where the platform supports the product type and terms. [Google product data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/share-your-product-data-with-google), [Google merchant listing data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing), [OpenAI product feed spec](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/file-upload/products).

4. **Treat feed access as channel-specific and eligibility as a gate.** Google Merchant Center is the direct Google product-data route; the current API is Merchant API (the older Content API reached sunset in August 2026). OpenAI direct product-feed onboarding is currently for approved partners and requires an application; Shopify catalog data is already integrated for eligible Shopify merchants. OpenAI's `is_eligible_search` flag means eligibility only, not display; feed format matters because a confirmed Google-compatible feed may not support per-item opt-out semantics. Check onboarding, supported markets and product policy before selling integration work. [Google Merchant API migration](https://developers.google.com/merchant/api/guides/compatibility/overview), [OpenAI feed onboarding](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/guides/get-started), [OpenAI product schema](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/file-upload/products).

5. **Give the crawler access only to intended public content.** Anthropic separates ClaudeBot (model training), Claude-User (user-directed page retrieval), and Claude-SearchBot (search). Site owners can choose different access by robot; blocking Claude-User may reduce user-directed web-search visibility. Decide separately for privacy, rights, server load, and discovery. Do not promise that allowing a bot will result in a citation, and do not assume every AI search uses the same crawler. [Anthropic crawler policy](https://support.anthropic.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler).

6. **Write concrete, verifiable product facts before sales language.** State material, construction, fit, dimensions, weight, care, compatibility, included parts, warranty, and limitations where true. Put the answer next to the relevant product choice. A 100% cotton heavyweight crew with a defined garment measurement chart is more decision-useful than “premium quality”; an adapter list with tested device models is more useful than “wide compatibility.” Google's guidance favors original, people-first, non-commodity information. [Google generative AI guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

7. **Add first-hand evidence that another seller cannot copy.** Show the actual sample, measurement method, material close-up, wash/wear result, assembly or compatibility test, and who performed it. Keep dates, conditions, and limitations. For an eventual SaaS, publish a reproducible test protocol and anonymized error examples only with permission. This can help both human confidence and source distinctiveness; it is a content-quality hypothesis, not evidence that a model prefers “originality.”

8. **Use real images that answer comparison questions.** Provide clear main product images and additional views: scale, texture, fit, included accessories, connector/label details, or before/after setup when accurate. Use descriptive alt text for accessibility and image context. Don't fabricate product attributes with image generation or hide material differences through retouching. For apparel, show the real decoration and garment drape; for functional products, show scale and what is in the box.

9. **Expose fit, compatibility, and variant logic in human language.** Give garment measurements and fit notes with model height/size when consented and accurate; explain whether sizing is garment or body measurement. For functional goods, list exact supported models, excluded models, dimensions, and how to check a match. For SaaS, show supported integrations, required plan, limits, and migration constraints. These are useful answers to buyer questions regardless of which search surface brings the visitor.

10. **Make total cost, delivery and returns easy to verify.** Surface shipping costs, expected dispatch/delivery ranges, return window/fees, warranty, taxes or subscription renewal terms clearly and consistently. Use eligible product/merchant policy markup and channel settings only to reflect the actual policy. OpenAI says price data may lag and the initial shown price may not be the lowest offer; give a clear landing page so shoppers can confirm. Measure support contacts and checkout abandonment around these facts rather than assuming markup alone lifts sales.

11. **Use change-notification tools for freshness, not magic.** Submit accurate Merchant Center updates; use sitemaps/Search Console and supported IndexNow notification where applicable. Bing presents IndexNow plus Product structured data as a way to notify participating engines about changes; a notification is not a guarantee of indexing or placement. Log update-to-observation time and stale-price incidents. [Bing IndexNow for shopping](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2025/5/IndexNow-Enables-Faster-and-More-Reliable-Updates-for-Shopping-and-Ads/), [Google recrawl guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/ask-google-to-recrawl).

12. **Earn credible reviews; never manufacture them.** Ask actual customers for honest feedback using a consistent, policy-compliant process. Do not gate requests by sentiment, fabricate reviews, or mark up reviews that are not present on the page. Review summaries and ratings can influence shopper interpretation, but review presence or markup does not guarantee a rich result or recommendation. For early-stage brands without reviews, use clearly labelled sample testing or founder demonstrations, not fake social proof. [Google Product structured data policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product-snippet).

13. **Make SaaS evidence inspectable.** Publish what the tool checks, the exact source/surface/date for any observed answer, known coverage gaps, and an exportable before/after record. If a local harness supplies a catalogue snapshot or prompt to a chosen model, label it a benchmark of that harness. It is not the live ChatGPT, Google or Copilot retrieval pipeline. A manual snapshot of real public UI can be recorded as a dated observation, not a stable rank. Prefer an operational claim such as “found 7 price/schema mismatches in 20 minutes” when that is what was measured.

14. **Measure a live change against a concurrent holdout.** Change one class of product facts on randomly assigned product families; leave matched controls alone; record feed acceptance, crawl/index observation, stock, promotions, and releases. Compare assigned products' qualified visits, carts, kept orders, and contribution margin over the same period, with uncertainty. If volume cannot answer a sales question, measure verified task time and error rate. Fixed prompt runs are secondary diagnostics. Query monitoring records what appeared in sampled runs; it does not establish revenue lift. [Local measurement protocol](https://silmoon04.github.io/ecommerce-ai-seo-research/notes/measurement.md).

15. **Instrument what each platform actually exposes.** Search Console's Generative AI report provides aggregate search impressions and page/date/country/device dimensions, not orders or margin. Bing AI Performance shows aggregate citation activity and sampled grounding phrases; Microsoft says it does not show a cited page's rank or role in an individual answer. GA4's AI Assistant channel captures recognized referrer sessions and excludes Google AI Overviews/AI Mode; missing referrers and in-chat checkout create gaps. Reconcile tagged referrals with commerce exports and keep unknown/direct traffic as unknown. [Google AI performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139), [Bing AI Performance](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2026/2/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview/), [GA4 channel groups](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9756891), [GA4 direct traffic](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/15258820?hl=en).

## Checkout and what it changes

Shopping discovery and checkout integration are separate jobs. OpenAI's current help describes Instant Checkout for some eligible products/merchants, while developer docs say the feature is available to approved partners and requires separate checkout flows. A feed field does not activate checkout. This can reduce the merchant-site session data available for measurement, so use channel order exports/settlement records and join them to product assignment where permitted. Check the merchant-of-record, customer service, cancellation, return, tax, fulfillment, and payment responsibilities in the applicable current agreement before estimating contribution margin. Do not claim checkout placement causes discovery; OpenAI says results are independently selected and not influenced by partnerships. [OpenAI shopping help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search), [OpenAI ACP concepts](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/guides/key-concepts).

There is no equivalent basis in the sources reviewed for treating Google product feeds or Bing Merchant Center feeds as a guaranteed organic product-answer ranking control. Microsoft Merchant Center product/ad APIs and paid shopping campaigns should not be confused with organic Copilot recommendations. Shopify's Agentic Storefront product preview is its own catalog search diagnostic; its help says downstream channels select products themselves. A merchant should first use built-in free tools and eligible routes, then pay for integration or independent QA only where a real gap remains.

## Claim boundaries and commercial implications

- **Supported:** “We corrected factual catalog mismatches,” “the product was eligible/accepted in this named feed,” “Search Console reported these aggregate impressions,” or “this URL was cited in these dated Bing AI Performance records.” Give denominator, surface, date and limitations.
- **Not supported by a local harness:** “ChatGPT recommends this product,” “we improved ChatGPT rank,” or “the ChatGPT pipeline selected our product.” A harness tests its own prompt/model/catalogue configuration. It cannot reveal a private production retrieval or ranking system.
- **Not supported by query monitoring alone:** “AI visibility work generated revenue” or “monitoring drove sales.” A counted mention/citation is a volatile visibility proxy. Revenue contribution requires merchant order data and a credible experiment; last-click AI referrals alone are incomplete and non-causal.
- **Not supported by schema/feed/crawl eligibility:** “Guaranteed ranking,” “guaranteed AI inclusion,” or “special AEO schema.” These actions make facts accessible or eligible; platforms retain selection control.
- **Defensible SaaS wedge:** cross-channel factual QA, feed/schema/page reconciliation, freshness/eligibility diagnostics, permitted observable-answer sampling, and controlled experiment workflow. Basic copy generation, llms.txt, feed submission for an already-integrated platform, or a proprietary “AI rank” score is weak differentiation unless the merchant can show saved cost or incremental contribution margin.

## What would change this view

Evidence of a stable, documented merchant control over unpaid product selection; a public query-level result interface with defined semantics; a well-powered randomized merchant study showing profitable lift from a specific change; or repeatable willingness to pay for cross-platform QA despite native platform tools would alter the product recommendation. A few successful prompt screenshots, vendor case studies without controls, or a local model benchmark would not.
