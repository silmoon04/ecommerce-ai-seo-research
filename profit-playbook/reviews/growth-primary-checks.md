# Growth decision primary-source checks

Retrieved 2026-09-30. This memo records what the cited first-party sources support and the limits of those claims.

## YouTube Shorts views and engaged views

YouTube Help says that, beginning 24 August 2026, a public view is counted when a video starts playing across formats, including Shorts. YouTube says YPP earnings remain based on “engaged Shorts views,” while eligibility uses “qualified Shorts views.” This means public Shorts views and engaged Shorts views are not interchangeable measures. The current Help page does not, in the captured text, provide a full current operational definition of an engaged Shorts view; avoid assigning a watch-time threshold without checking the current metric documentation in Studio. A TeamYouTube announcement for the March 2025 Shorts-specific change had described the then-new view count as starts/replays without minimum watch time, and the previous Shorts metric as “engaged views,” but the August 2026 update now applies starts-based public views across all formats.

Sources: [YouTube Help: Understand your YouTube content performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop&hl=en) (Aug. 24, 2026 change and YPP distinction); [TeamYouTube announcement: A Change to How We Count Views on Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/333869549?hl=en&msgid=346142420) (historical March 2025 definition).

## TikTok hooks and organic-to-paid prediction

TikTok’s Creative Codes recommends an ad structure of hook, body, and close, and hook techniques such as suspense, surprise, or emotion. The page characterizes this guidance as principles for effective TikTok ads. TikTok’s performance article separately describes campaign testing and measurement, including split tests. These sources support using hook formats as creative hypotheses to test in ads; they do not establish that an organic post’s performance predicts paid sales, nor guarantee that a given hook will convert for this shop, audience, product, or budget. The evidence scope is platform ad guidance/research and campaign testing, not a validated organic-to-paid sales forecasting rule. Treat TikTok-published effect sizes as platform-reported evidence, not independent estimates.

Sources: [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/creative-codes); [TikTok: Performance Fundamentals](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/tiktok-fundamentals-unlock-full-potential-tiktok-advertising).

## UK creator gifts, disclosure, and commissioned creative copyright

The CMA’s creator guidance, updated 3 September 2025, applies to creators receiving free or discounted products or other incentives in connection with social media accounts, regardless of follower count. Its companion brand guidance says promotional content arising from brand marketing activity should be clearly labelled as ads, including where a brand sends gifts hoping for posts and does not request anything in return; it describes gifts as an incentive/payment and recommends a clear, upfront “Ad” or “Advert” label. ASA/CAP’s influencer guide is a best-practice guide and says relevant incentivised promotional content needs upfront disclosure. Scope matters: the sources concern content connected to promotion/brand activity; they do not establish that every independent mention of a product, unrelated to a brand connection or incentive, is advertising.

For commissioned creator assets, UK IPO guidance says the creator is generally the first copyright owner, including where a work is commissioned, unless the parties agree otherwise in writing. A commissioner may in some cases have an implied, limited non-exclusive licence for the commissioned purpose, but that is not necessarily an ownership transfer. Put ownership or the intended licence (including paid-media usage, edits, term, and territory) in the written agreement; obtaining the asset or paying for its creation alone does not establish unrestricted rights.

Sources: [CMA: Social media endorsements—guidance for content creators](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/social-media-endorsements-guidance-for-content-creators); [CMA: Social media endorsements—guidance for brands](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/reviews-and-social-media-endorsements-guidance-for-businesses-and-brands/social-media-endorsements-guidance-for-brands); [ASA/CAP: Influencers’ guide to making clear that ads are ads](https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/influencers-guide.html); [UK IPO: Ownership of copyright works](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/ownership-of-copyright-works).

## Attribution and incrementality

Google Ads distinguishes attributed conversions, allocated under configured tracking/attribution rules and windows, from Conversion Lift incrementality, estimated by comparing conversions in an ad-exposed treatment group with a control group. Thus attributed conversions/ROAS describe credit assigned under a model or window; they do not by themselves show how many sales would not otherwise have occurred. A holdout/lift experiment is designed to estimate that causal difference, subject to its study design and measurement limits. This is Google’s measurement description, not an independent validation of a particular campaign result.

Source: [Google Ads Help: Understand your Conversion Lift based on users measurement data](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14102450?hl=en).
