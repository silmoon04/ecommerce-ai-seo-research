# Strategy review: use the speed, test the economics

Final review, 1 October 2026, incorporating the profit review and centrally verified evidence corrections. Research recommendations only; no purchase, advertising, recruitment, outreach or live business test is authorized.

**Adopt a narrower version of Perry Xie's method.** Cheap AI store assembly is useful if it removes a real cost and lets us investigate an offer sooner. The worthwhile sequence is product screening, exact supply and margin checks, a credible page, then bounded buyer exposure. Keep that sequence. Change the evidence required to call something profitable, and defer the collection of subscriptions, nine-ad campaigns, automatic budget increases and simultaneous products.

For the immediate-profit objective, the earlier recommendation still stands: a small paid merchant catalogue job can test buyer access and payment with less retail cash committed, provided we can reach a qualified operator. That is an unvalidated alternative, with selling and delivery costs of its own. If the chosen objective is building the clothing business, the original crew is the best-prepared physical test. The videos strengthen the case for building its page cheaply; they supply no reason to buy a retail operation merely to validate eventual SaaS.

The four videos are useful instruction. The main tutorial starts with a selected product; the separate selection video supplies the filters; the speedruns add implementation and a brief ad heuristic. Long-running ads and tool rankings can narrow a search. Small parcels, inspectable utility and room for margin are sensible questions. However, runtime contains no denominator of ad spend or purchases, and estimated traffic multiplied by assumed 3% conversion does not reveal sales. The creator's revenue and hit-rate claims have no auditable attempts, costs or refund history. Those limits prevent a success-rate forecast without making the workflow worthless. [Selection tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5slU3QjvzI8), [main build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b43_Gs64mYM).

Choose one original embroidered crew, one colour and one exact supplier route. The inherited £72 delivered offer is still a price hypothesis. Its £35.31 fulfilment input belongs to the Inkthreadable Changer scenario; Printful specifications cannot validate that basket. An attractive mockup is particularly weak evidence for a premium garment. Require a sample and identical reorder, measurements against the chart, stitch/backing/puckering inspection, actual fit photographs and a care-instruction wash check. Two samples do not establish every size or long-term durability, but they can reject an obviously inadequate offer before traffic. Confirm the exact blank, decoration, VAT and delivered quotation. [Inkthreadable shipping](https://www.inkthreadable.co.uk/shipping-costs), [embroidery guidance](https://help.inkthreadable.co.uk/en/articles/598083-embroidery-design-guidelines).

Keep the compact cable/charger organiser as a desk-research challenger. It earns sampling only through a named supplier, documented landed cost, workable returns and a useful demonstrated device fit. A generic item with an invented £9 cost has no commercial advantage established. The crew's original design offers possible distinction; the organiser must earn its place against readily comparable alternatives. Defer the neck-pain pillow: exact-product efficacy, fit, freight and return costs create too many unresolved questions for this first allocation. Do not open another store to make the shortlist feel productive.

One real sample shoot should supply clear full-garment, fit and stitch images plus at most two creative concepts: understated everyday styling and original-world/design appeal. Either video or stills can work as executions; this research establishes no winning format. Show the delivered product faithfully. The page needs the actual total price, size guidance, materials, realistic delivery, returns, seller identity and a working mobile checkout. Competitor structure can suggest shopper questions, while copied artwork, regenerated competitor photography and invented reviews add no trustworthy proof. A sticky button earns inclusion only if it helps the task without obscuring controls. [Copyright guidance](https://www.gov.uk/using-somebody-elses-intellectual-property/copyright), [consumer-review obligations](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews/short-guide-for-businesses-publishing-consumer-reviews-and-complying-with-consumer-protection-law).

Use existing AI access, Shopify Basic monthly and free Dawn. The published Basic price is £25 monthly; the annual-equivalent £19 price requires annual billing. The advertised three-day trial then £1/month for three months could reduce early cash if eligible; keep the conservative allowance until checkout confirms it. A builder can earn its bill later if observed time saved, after setup and factual QA, exceeds its cost. We should welcome that saving when demonstrated. Today, Atlas, ad-intelligence subscriptions and bundle apps do not resolve the largest unknowns. [Shopify pricing](https://www.shopify.com/uk/pricing), [Dawn](https://themes.shopify.com/themes/dawn/presets/dawn).

The canonical £500 example is one shared envelope, replacing the inconsistent application budgets. Nothing is committed now. Conditional sample/reorder arithmetic is £83.22; allow £50 for two monthly shop cycles and £15 for a domain; cap traffic at £75; protect £250 working cash; leave £26.78 uncommitted. Maximum planned zero-order spending is £223.22 before founder time, if all operating allocations are used. Sample and account quotes may change it. Headroom covers surprises; working cash covers customer obligations. Neither is spare advertising money. The alternative £1,000 capacity keeps the same product and sample/shared costs, with £250 cumulative traffic, £500 protected cash and £101.78 headroom. Its first traffic tranche is still £75.

Use twenty shared founder hours: four for supply, quality, rights and economics; five for page, policies, checkout and measurement; three for buyer access/task observations; four for the shoot and creatives; four for campaign checks and analysis. An eight-hour private rehearsal fits inside this allowance. A five-person round is optional and must fit the three-hour allocation; fewer relevant observations can still expose confusion. No paid incentives are funded. If readiness exceeds the envelope, revise the plan before releasing more work. Existing customer service remains an obligation and its actual time enters order economics.

After later authorization and readiness checks, run one platform, one £72 offer and page, with at most two concepts, for a chosen seven-day window or £75 spend, whichever comes first. Confirm the platform can enforce the cap. The question is whether this package reaches relevant buyers and produces reconciled orders at an acquisition cost worth investigating. Record delivered exposure, landing visits, checkouts and paid order IDs. Ad delivery between creatives is not a randomized comparison. Stop immediately for an inaccurate claim, broken checkout/events, unexpected spending or inadequate cash cover. An empty dashboard at the cap can mean failed exposure, failed execution or unresolved demand; it does not prove an entire product category cannot sell.

The day-one/day-two/day-three rule is a calendar heuristic. Purchase lag, uneven delivery and a handful of orders can dominate daily ROAS; delivery and returns take longer still. Preserve platform attribution separately from store orders, including unmatched cases and overlapping credits. The video's USD400 checkpoint is neither a recommended daily budget nor the established total cost of its test. Automatic 20% increases can compound an unusually good day. Use each apparent win to decide whether a separately capped repeat is affordable. [Speedrun part two](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oH2r5KUTRzo).

The crew model leaves £26.03 per initial paid order after chosen refunds, replacements, fees and £2.50 variable labour, before acquisition and fixed costs. Preserve the prior chosen £18 residual target, implying £8.03 maximum acquisition cost; show £5 and £0 as sensitivities. None is user-confirmed. At the video's 3.39 ROAS, comparable arithmetic leaves £4.79, positive before fixed costs but far below £18. At that unchanged residual, recovering £148.22 sample/store/domain costs takes 31 initial orders; including twenty setup hours at £15 takes 94. These are recovery calculations, not sales forecasts. A lower residual can still be worthwhile if repeatable volume repays fixed costs and founder time. CAC must use initial acquired orders when compared with this refund-adjusted model. Count all acquisition costs once, including failed tests.

More spending requires a costed repeat of the same offer, updated delivered/returned cohorts, funded remedies and a credible route to recovering ongoing costs and founder time. Reconciled marginal contribution matters; past setup losses still belong in the portfolio report. Changing the chosen profit floor requires an explicit decision before the next cohort. Two positive small cohorts remain uncertain. Refunds preserve original card costs, and working cover must grow with exposed receipts and unpaid fulfilments rather than assuming £250 is always sufficient. [Shopify refunds](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/payouts/refunds), [UK returns](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds).

Finally, reuse verified product facts for ordinary search and later AI-channel checks. A scripted model reading our page tests factual accuracy under that setup; it does not establish real shopping visibility or sales. An actual AI referral, retained retail order and independent merchant payment answer different questions. Keep the proposed SaaS at the manual paid-service stage until unrelated merchants pay for a recurring problem that native tools leave unresolved. The next useful retail commitment is an exact sample quotation and reviewable offer, followed conditionally by one £75 diagnostic. There is no justification here for building a portfolio of stores first.
