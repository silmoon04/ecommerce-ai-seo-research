# Critical evidence review

Reviewed 1 October 2026, Europe/London. Scope: the four-video evidence index, all seven completed research memos, both research JSON files, targeted private-caption checks, and additional primary-source checks. The source memos were frozen before the replacements in `evidence-overrides.json` were prepared. This is a review of the research, not an audit of the creator's accounts or a completed customer experiment.

The method is useful as a cheap implementation workflow. It makes competitor research, page assembly and offer presentation concrete, and gives a founder something testable sooner. That practical value does not require accepting the revenue headlines. Our useful adaptation is one truthful product page, a checked checkout and a bounded demand experiment. Buying every demonstrated subscription, copying persuasive claims or treating a polished page as validation would add costs without resolving the main unknowns.

## What the four videos support

The main tutorial uses a preselected pillow. The July research tutorial uses GetHookd and Similarweb; the August speedrun explicitly switches to TrendTrack. These are related lessons rather than four independent replications. The screen favours cheap, compact products, multiple units in a basket, a plausible problem and a large market. Multiple-unit potential is not evidence of repeat purchasing. The 20-day and 25-day ad cutoffs are historical recipes, not validated thresholds.

The main title's $200k in 30 days remains unreconciled with the opening's $197k in 50 days and $143k in 30 days. Those might concern different stores or windows. Neither accusing the creator of deception nor accepting the title as measured outcome data follows from the record. The missing denominator, failed attempts, costs and retained profit prevent a success-rate estimate. The linked tools and supplier create commercial incentives, but incentives alone do not refute the instruction. [Main tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b43_Gs64mYM)

The competitor-revenue example starts from estimated traffic, increases it without validation, chooses 3% conversion and uses a $77 product price. The selected arithmetic is $138,600 per month, narrated as about $138,000. Its 1,800 orders are implied, not observed. Similarweb describes modeled traffic from several data sources; its methodology supplies no warrant to increase every estimate. The $400 at the day-three checkpoint and reported 3.39 ROAS are also a selected example, with no verified daily-budget or complete-test-cost interpretation. [Product tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5slU3QjvzI8&t=711s), [Similarweb methodology update](https://support.similarweb.com/hc/en-us/articles/18876356573725-Everything-you-need-to-know-about-Similarweb-s-2024-Data-Version-Update), [day-three example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oH2r5KUTRzo&t=1749s)

The tool signals have provenance, but weak connections to the outcome we need. GetHookd's current score uses within-brand impression rank, runtime and creative reuse, without advertiser ROAS; its documentation says the scoring method changed. Its spend estimate uses EU reach and a chosen CPM. These can prioritize inspection, but cannot supply profit or make an older score comparable with today's. TrendTrack identifies Similarweb as its traffic source and labels EU reach/budget data estimated. [GetHookd score](https://gethookdai.crisp.help/en/article/understand-the-ad-performance-score-tjajzj/), [filter methodology](https://gethookdai.crisp.help/en/article/mastering-filters-1xhf011/), [TrendTrack](https://www.trendtrack.io/pricing)

The 90% scroll assertion has no supplied measurement. NN/g measured attention, not the percentage of this shop's visitors who stop at the offer. Its study cannot replace that missing evidence. Keep key buying information prominent and test whether customers can find the rest. [NN/g original research](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention-original-research/)

## Cost and margin corrections

A lean build remains affordable in principle. Shopify Basic lists £25 monthly or £19/month on annual billing, a £228 annual listed commitment before applicable tax. The current introduction advertises three free days followed by £1/month for three months, subject to eligibility; this can lower early cash cost, but is not the ongoing price. Dawn avoids a theme purchase. [Shopify UK pricing](https://www.shopify.com/uk/pricing)

Atlas lists $39/month plus additional charges. Rapi Starter is $15/month or $119.88/year. GetHookd Starter is $29 monthly or a displayed $19/month billed yearly, equivalent to $228 annually. Claude Pro is $20 monthly or $200 upfront annually. Existing subscriptions may make incremental drafting cheaper. These tools are optional, and annual equivalents must not be presented as a month's cash outlay. [Atlas](https://apps.shopify.com/dropshipt), [Rapi](https://apps.shopify.com/rapi), [GetHookd](https://www.gethookd.ai/), [Claude](https://claude.com/pricing)

TrendTrack's public responses disagree: the rendered text capture and directly retrieved current HTML expose different price sets. The override records both observations and removes its disputed price from the stack total. There is no account-specific checkout quote. Another necessary correction is billing cancellation: uninstalling a Shopify-billed app stops future cycles, while pending charges can remain; separately billed subscriptions require cancellation with their provider. [Shopify app billing](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/manage-billing/billing-charges/types-of-charges/third-party-charges/app-charges)

The economics formulas reproduce the selected scenarios. For the crew, £28.5338 before acquisition and labour becomes £26.0338 after £2.50 variable labour. A £20 CAC therefore leaves £6.0338 before fixed costs, clearing the chosen £5 residual; the paid-testing memo had this backwards. The corresponding CAC ceiling is £21.0338 and target gross ROAS is 3.42. At an assumed 3.39 ROAS, the same crew arithmetic leaves £4.79 before shared costs. None is an observed acquisition result.

The £35.31 inherited fulfilment input is an Inkthreadable catalogue-and-shipping scenario that already includes supplier VAT under the assumed unregistered-seller treatment. It is not a Printful quote. Samples, digitisation where applicable and setup hours are separate test costs; adding them to a portfolio is different from adding supplier VAT again to every order. Validate the selected SKU, decoration and basket. Printful's row giving 3 to 7 business days specifically describes Standard DDP to the UK, not every UK shipment. [Prior model](https://silmoon04.github.io/ecommerce-ai-seo-research/profit-playbook/research/unit-economics.md), [Printful shipping](https://www.printful.com/uk/shipping)

## Product, rights and testing limits

The pillow evidence deserves a fairer account. The 70-person crossover trial, completed by 64, tested a particular polyurethane pillow with 60 springs against education plus participants' own pillows. Four-week neck pain improved by 8.7 points on a 0 to 100 scale relative to the comparator; shoulder pain was not significantly different. The broader review included 35 articles and nine high-quality studies involving 555 people, with positive pain/disability findings for rubber and spring pillows and inconclusive sleep-quality findings. Calling all evidence inconclusive understates it. Transferring those findings to an unspecified memory-foam product would still be unjustified. [Trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30939188/), [full systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33895703/)

Pain-relief wording can bring a product into medical-device advertising and regulation; an ordinary pillow is not automatically a medical device. Intended purpose, exact claims, evidence and destination matter. CAP's pain page contains an outdated marking deadline, so use current MHRA guidance for regulatory requirements and distinguish Great Britain from Northern Ireland. The first experiment can simply avoid unsupported therapeutic positioning. [CAP pain guidance](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/health-pain.html), [MHRA requirements](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/medical-devices-how-to-comply-with-the-legal-requirements)

The conversion memo correctly rejects fabricated reviews, artificial scarcity and an assumed legal safe harbour for regenerated competitor pictures. Learning a generic page structure differs from copying protected expression, and private commercial use alone does not establish permission. Genuine testimonials need substantiation and appropriate permission; review publishers also have proportionate prevention/removal duties. Neither a stock face nor a real review validates an objective efficacy claim. [Copyright guidance](https://www.gov.uk/using-somebody-elses-intellectual-property/copyright), [ASA testimonials](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/testimonials-and-endorsements.html), [CMA reviews](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews/short-guide-for-businesses-publishing-consumer-reviews-and-complying-with-consumer-protection-law)

The paid-testing memo is strongest where it separates diagnostics from profitability and counts failed attempts. It should not declare that any £100 test is incapable of supporting a low-CAC result. Budget alone determines neither sample size nor statistical power. A comparative claim needs an effect size, outcome frequency, allocation and analysis plan; platform learning thresholds answer a different question. Predeclared fixed stopping is a sensible simple choice. Valid sequential methods also exist, so the problem is uncorrected repeated significance checking, not every early decision. [Johari et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.04922)

Finally, the AI-shopping memo preserves the right separation: paid response, organic discovery, attributable referrals, retained orders, synthetic factual checks and independent merchant payment answer different questions. OpenAI says Shopify data already arrives through Shopify Catalog; a bespoke feed is not a prerequisite, nor a visibility guarantee. An internal checker may help operate a shop, but external merchants must buy and continue using it to establish SaaS demand. [OpenAI shopping guidance](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search)

Proceed with a cheap private build rehearsal and the missing-facts ledger. Any later live test should use a verified product, explicit cash and time caps, functioning measurement and a conclusion proportionate to its observations. The proposed £500 portfolio, prices, refund rates and test thresholds remain scenarios. This review authorizes no spending or customer contact.
