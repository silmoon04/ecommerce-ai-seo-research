# A faster store can make testing cheaper. It does not make the product a winner.

Perry Xie's method is worth adapting. It reduces the work between noticing an interesting offer and having a page customers can use. The most useful parts are the structured competitor scan, explicit product questions, AI-assisted drafting, reusable sections and mobile inspection. The missing part is stronger evidence that an apparent win leaves enough money after fulfilment, refunds, acquisition and the cost of unsuccessful attempts.

We reviewed the linked tutorial, the dedicated product-selection lesson and both earlier speedruns. Seven GPT-6 Luna researchers investigated the evidence, tools, products, supply, conversion, economics, advertising and AI-shopping transfer. Three GPT-6.1 Sol agents applied that work, another built the visual report, and three GPT-6 Astra agents reviewed the evidence, profit model and every decision at max effort. Model agreement does not substitute for source records or customer results. No purchases, ads, outreach or real customer experiments were run.

## What the other videos add

The [main tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b43_Gs64mYM) starts with a selected product. It demonstrates competitor-page capture, Claude research and copy, an Atlas template, bundles and custom Liquid sections. Inputs, account setup and product choice precede the advertised build time. A polished page is a useful output, but operating the shop still requires accurate stock, delivery, payments, policies, service and fulfilment.

The [selection lesson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5slU3QjvzI8) supplies the earlier step. At roughly 06:54 it filters GetHookd for US image ads that remain active, have run at least 25 days, rank in its top two performance tiers, and appear no more than five times per brand. It then asks whether the product is cheap to source, compact, useful, suitable for multiple units and attached to a sizeable problem. These questions are useful. The filter's score does not disclose the advertiser's profit, and active long-running ads are a selected sample of survivors.

Its competitor estimate is especially easy to overread: 60,000 assumed monthly visits × 3% assumed purchase conversion × $77 = **$138,600 hypothetical monthly revenue**. The video narrates about $138,000. The traffic uplift, conversion, orders and product-price mix are not verified sales. Use the calculation to identify which facts need checking. It cannot supply our expected conversion or success rate.

The [first speedrun](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IbObinWjfHg) switches to TrendTrack and a 20-day cutoff, with long ad copy and rank ordering. Its grounding-sheet example also shows why an attractive functional product can carry difficult claim and quality questions. Evidence for a mechanism or a different product does not establish that the exact item we sell delivers a health benefit.

The [second speedrun](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oH2r5KUTRzo) adds three desire-based ad sets with three variants each, a day-two purchase expectation and day-three break-even ROAS goal, followed by 20% budget adjustments. It reports a 3.39 ROAS example at a USD400 day-three checkpoint. That is neither an independently audited profit result nor a documented complete-test budget. Calendar days and a small number of attributed orders cannot establish recurring profit. They can still expose a broken checkout, poor delivery of ads or a promising response worth investigating.

## What we should actually take from the method

Keep a large free shortlist, then spend only when a candidate earns the next step. Record the source and uncertainty behind each product fact. Ask what the competitor page helps a buyer understand, then answer that question with our own product and evidence. Let AI draft copy and sections from verified facts; make it flag missing facts rather than invent them. Measure whether a builder saves time after its setup, subscription and factual review costs.

The best first physical offer remains the original embroidered crew, if we choose to build the clothing business. The inherited £72 delivered price is unvalidated. It needs an exact Inkthreadable blank and decoration quote, one sample and an identical reorder, measured fit, stitch inspection, a care-instruction wash check and photographs of the actual garment. Two samples can expose obvious defects; they cannot validate every size or long-term durability. A compact cable/charger organiser stays a research challenger until its named supply route, useful fit and delivered cost are established. A neck-pain pillow adds too many unresolved efficacy and operating questions for this first allocation.

For a profit-first software objective, a scoped paid merchant catalogue job remains a separate, potentially cheaper route if we can reach qualified buyers. Running our own shop can teach us about commerce operations. It cannot establish that unrelated merchants will pay for an AEO product.

## Three numbers that should stay separate

Under the crew scenario, a £72 initial receipt, £35.31 landed fulfilment, 8% full refunds, 2% separate replacements, 2% + £0.25 processing and £2.50 variable labour leave **£26.03 before acquisition and fixed costs**. These are catalogue-derived and chosen inputs, not an exact configured quotation or observed loss rates. Supplier VAT is already included in the inherited cost under the assumed unregistered UK seller treatment.

At **3.39 gross ROAS**, comparable arithmetic implies £21.24 acquisition per initial order and **£4.79 remaining contribution**. That is positive. It could be useful if volume and repeatability recover the remaining business costs. With £148.22 sample/store/domain costs, unchanged £4.79 contribution takes 31 initial orders to recover those costs; adding twenty setup hours valued at £15 takes 94. These are recovery calculations, not attainable-sales forecasts. Further recurring charges and failed tests increase the amount to recover.

The earlier report's chosen **£18 residual target** permits roughly £8.03 acquisition cost, requiring 8.96 gross ROAS. A separate £5 sensitivity permits £21.03 and requires 3.42 ROAS. Neither floor is a user-confirmed requirement or an industry benchmark. A result below £18 needs a commercial judgement about volume, capacity and cost recovery; it is not automatically worthless.

Finally, a unit can look healthy while the whole round loses money. At £75 total advertising, five modeled initial orders give £15 ad CAC and £11.03 contribution each, yet the round loses **£80.55 cash**, or **£393.05 including valued order and setup labour**. Ten modeled orders give £7.50 CAC and £18.53 unit contribution, but the round still loses £262.88 economically. The charts show this distinction. Expected fractional refunds are a sensitivity model; actual small cohorts must use their actual retained receipts and costs.

## A smaller experiment with a clear job

The report's proposed **£500 capacity** holds £83.22 for sample/reorder, £65 for up to two shop cycles and domain, £75 total first traffic, £250 protected working cash and £26.78 uncommitted. It uses one product and twenty shared founder hours. The £83.22 depends on the exact supplier basket and provisional digitisation treatment. Funds and affordable loss are unconfirmed. This is a plan to review, not a commitment to spend.

Begin with an eight-hour private rehearsal inside that twenty-hour allowance. After a satisfactory sample, factual page and verified checkout/events, a later authorized diagnostic can use one platform, one offer and at most two concepts. Stop at £75 or the chosen seven-day window, whichever comes first, with earlier stops for operational faults or insufficient cash. The window is a scheduling choice, not statistical power. Organic purchases are not a prerequisite for a properly prepared paid diagnostic. Allocated ad delivery between concepts is not a randomized A/B test.

Keep initial acquired orders, retained orders and platform-attributed purchases separate. Reconcile spend and order IDs, log unmatched credits, and count unsuccessful tests. Hold cash against exposed customer receipts, remedies and unfunded fulfilment; £250 does not establish a safe concurrent-order count. The alternative £1,000 capacity funds the same lead product, not more stores. It permits a separately reviewed further £175 traffic tranche while increasing protected cash and headroom.

Use a promising first result to choose a bounded repeat, rather than automatically adding 20% every day. A comparison claiming conversion improvement needs adequate outcome frequency, allocation and an analysis plan. Small observations still have value when the conclusion stays small: customers understood the sizing, an event failed, a concept reached the wrong audience, or this specific offer generated reconciled orders worth testing again.

## What this teaches the AEO product

Reuse the verified facts for ordinary search, structured product data and later AI-shopping checks. A synthetic harness can test whether a model correctly understands a page under a recorded setup. Real shopping visibility, attributable AI referrals, retained retail orders and independent merchant payments each require their own evidence. Product-feed eligibility and recommendation are different questions. Native Shopify and platform integrations already solve part of the infrastructure, so our software needs a paid customer problem beyond submitting data.

The useful software experiment is a manual, scoped merchant job: identify an actual catalogue or shopper-information failure, fix it with approval, record the work and charge for the result. Repeated merchant payment can then justify automation. Until that exists, our owned-store findings are research and operational practice.

The report contains 24 individually reviewed decisions, source links, four video references, side-by-side workflow changes and editable order/cash calculators. Read the stage gates as conditional decisions. The evidence supports a cheaper way to learn about one offer; it does not supply a reliable winning-product probability or a promised conversion increase.

# AI shopping, product facts and SaaS validation

Research date: 1 October 2026. Current platform details change quickly; verify feed access and reporting before any implementation.

## What a fast store can and cannot prove

A quickly AI-built Shopify store can be a cheap test surface. It can expose whether a specific audience responds to a product story, price, creative and checkout. It does not by itself validate “AEO” or a product-feed SaaS. Keep these outcomes separate:

- **Paid ad conversion:** a visitor arrived from a paid impression and completed a measurable step. This tests a creative/offer/audience combination under the actual auction and landing page. It says little about unpaid discovery or long-term demand unless the orders survive fulfilment, returns and repeat purchase.
- **Organic SEO:** search impressions and clicks earned from an indexed page. Google says SEO fundamentals also apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode; those features have no special technical requirements. Product structured data and Merchant Center feeds can improve eligibility or understanding, but do not promise display or rank ([Google AI search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), [product data sharing](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/share-your-product-data)).
- **AI recommendation / product discovery:** a product is selected in a shopping response, or a page appears as a cited source in a generative answer. These are different surfaces and selection mechanisms. ChatGPT says shopping results are selected for relevance and product metadata; they are not ads. Google’s generative search guidance describes links drawn from its Search index. A screenshot from one prompt is only an observation, not evidence of stable eligibility or lift ([ChatGPT shopping](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search)).
- **Known AI referral:** a landing session carries an attributable source marker. OpenAI documents `utm_source=chatgpt.com` on ChatGPT search referral links; this can be tracked in analytics. It establishes a visit attributed to ChatGPT search, not necessarily that a generative model recommended the product, nor that the visitor bought or retained it ([OpenAI publisher FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq)).
- **Retained order:** a paid order remains after cancellation, refund, chargeback and replacement windows, with fulfilment cost and contribution recorded. This is stronger demand evidence than clicks or checkout starts, but one retained order is not repeatability.
- **Synthetic model harness:** scripted prompts sent to a chosen model/site-reading setup can check whether controlled product facts are retrieved and represented correctly. It tests your harness and content under those conditions. It cannot establish that real shoppers ask those prompts, that a platform indexes the store, or that recommendations cause purchases.
- **Independent merchant willingness to pay (WTP):** a target merchant buys or renews the SaaS from its own budget for a job it values. The founder’s shop using a tool is product dogfooding, not external WTP. A dropshipping store can validate a consumer offer while SaaS remains unvalidated.

A paid campaign may still be useful if its creative deliberately reaches the same fandom or functional-clothing audience the eventual software would serve. It can expose audience vocabulary, objections, size/material questions and checkout friction. But an ad-interested audience is not necessarily shopping through AI, and low/no organic AI recommendations cannot diagnose why. Report audience fit as a hypothesis, not a channel result. For the understated dark-fantasy clothing concept, test whether the niche responds to original art and garment proof; do not expect this to prove broad commerce-feed demand. For a future functional-merchants product, run separate interviews or a paid pilot with independent shops after there is a concrete workflow to buy.

## Minimum facts and provenance ledger

Keep one source-of-truth record per purchasable variant, with immutable experiment snapshots. Google requires price and availability to match landing page, checkout and structured data, and its product specification distinguishes IDs, title, description, image, variants, material, size, shipping and returns ([Merchant Center specification](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7052112?hl=en), [merchant listing markup](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing)). At minimum record:

- merchant, product and variant IDs; canonical URL; product type/brand; title; original description; image URLs and rights/source; sizes, size system, colour, material and fit measurements;
- current price/currency, compare-at or sale price, stock/preorder state, fulfillment origin and service window, shipping cost/estimate, return window/method/outcome, and any product claim with evidence;
- for each fact: value, source owner (merchant, supplier document, observed page, platform feed), evidence URL/file reference, captured-at timestamp, last-verified date, verifier, confidence/status, market/currency, and next expiry/recheck date;
- feed version/hash, schema/platform target, submission and processing result, page/structured-data snapshot, mismatch and correction history;
- experiment ID/version, channel and campaign/query, creative/page version, spend/impressions/clicks, landing sessions, add-to-cart/checkout/order IDs, paid and retained status, cancellation/refund/replacement, fulfilment cost, fees, net contribution, and retention/repeat window.

Do not turn model guesses into facts. Mark unverified supplier composition, delivery estimates, popularity, “best”, sustainability or health claims as unknown until evidenced. Avoid borrowed competitor descriptions, product photos, reviews, trust badges or sales claims without rights and substantiation. OpenAI’s product-feed documentation also treats Ads eligibility separately and calls for current price/availability; its direct feed onboarding is currently restricted to approved partners ([OpenAI feed getting started](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/guides/get-started), [feed fields](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/file-upload/products)). Shopify merchants’ product data is already integrated into ChatGPT through Shopify Catalog, so a rushed bespoke integration is not a prerequisite for trying the store ([OpenAI shopping help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search)).

“Build a database” is not a moat on its own. A large scrape can be stale, duplicated, legally unusable, biased toward crawlable English-language stores, and missing product truth like garment measurements or delivery performance. The useful asset would be permissioned, representative, time-stamped facts with clear provenance, correction rates, variant resolution, and demonstrated merchant outcomes. The defensible question is whether merchants save enough time or recover enough lost product accuracy to keep paying, not whether the table contains many rows.

## Cheap repeatable research loop

Use an internal loop before writing public copy or changing feeds: (1) collect a competitor’s exact claim and URL, label it promotional/self-reported or independently evidenced; (2) trace factual assertions to the primary owner, preserving capture date and version; (3) write original copy from verified product and merchant facts, with claims linked to evidence; (4) QA the rendered product page, structured data/feed export and checkout against the ledger for price, variant, availability, shipping, returns and image rights; (5) save page/feed/model prompt-output snapshots under a versioned experiment ID; (6) review errors and downstream outcomes, then amend facts or copy with a new version. Keep a fixed prompt set and model/date when doing synthetic checks; score exactness, omissions, contradictions and citation quality. Repeat with a second run or model to see variance, but never label that “AI rank tracking” or a market result.

For a low-cost sequence, first check Search Console indexing and merchant feed diagnostics, then run a few buyer-intent prompts manually and log inclusion and factual errors, without treating omission as failure of the store. Track actual AI referral UTMs separately from Google organic impressions/clicks and paid sessions. Only buy traffic if there is a defined audience/creative hypothesis, spend cap and retained-order threshold; no conversion benchmark can be inferred from the Perry video or platform docs. Finally, show a working workflow to a few unrelated clothing or functional merchants and ask for a paid pilot only after measuring a recurring pain. Record their own words, current workaround, decision maker, price response and whether they actually pay. That step tests SaaS WTP independently of the owned store.

Recommendation: build the lean store only if its consumer-offer experiment is worthwhile on its own economics and creative learning. Add a small, read-only product-fact checker as an internal tool if it reduces mistakes across both clothing and functional merchant examples. Do not build a broad product database or promise AI recommendation gains until provenance, channel-specific outcome data and independent merchant payment support that scope.

# Conversion ideas and rights boundaries

Research date: 1 October 2026. Scope: evaluate the video’s screenshot-to-store workflow as a source of testable UI hypotheses for a UK-facing new shop. The video is a tutorial and self-reported demonstration, not a controlled conversion study. ## What the workflow can teach

At 03:31 to 05:32, Xie captures a competitor’s full product page, asks an AI to explain its sections, and generates a similar store structure. At 06:08 to 06:32, he says not to reuse competitor images one-to-one, but recommends feeding them to image generation to make “very similar” images. At 09:34 to 11:33 he recreates a bundle offer from a screenshot, including a free gift, then adds a sticky add-to-cart (ATC). At 16:19 to 17:26 he adds sample review cards, avatars, and a sale announcement; at 18:01 to 18:32 he claims 90% do not scroll past the offer and says the above-fold area and bundle are what matter.

Reference screenshots help identify components and generate hypotheses: shoppers might need variant selection, delivery information near the price, a clear offer, or a way back to ATC. A competitor page cannot show why a component is present or whether it caused sales; its performance may reflect audience, traffic source, or brand equity. A screenshot-to-code prototype tests build speed. Shopper evidence tests whether it answers real questions.

The “90% don’t scroll” statement is not a sound design rule. Nielsen Norman Group’s broad 2010 eyetracking study says users do scroll, while attention becomes more thinly spread lower on long pages; it does not report that 90% stop at the offer. Baymard’s mobile research likewise examines shoppers browsing, comparing, and using product information across long mobile pages, and highlights page-overview problems rather than a fixed fold boundary. These are directional usability findings, not conversion-lift estimates for this shop. Use analytics and task observation to learn how far this audience scrolls, and make key price, variant, delivery, and CTA information easy to locate without assuming everything below the fold is irrelevant. ([NN/g scrolling study](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention-original-research/); [Baymard mobile e-commerce research](https://baymard.com/research/mcommerce-usability))

## Hypotheses that need customer evidence

**Above the fold and offer focus.** A clear product name, price, useful image, variant controls, delivery/returns summary, and next action are reasonable hypotheses. “Above the fold” changes by device, text scaling, and entry path. Test whether people can explain what the item is, its delivered cost, arrival timing, and how to choose it.

**Bundles and free gifts.** Shopify describes bundles as a way to curate related products and optionally offer a discount, while its official documentation notes inventory, checkout, shipping, and returns behavior that needs configuration. These facts establish product functionality, not demand or uplift. Test a transparent base offer against a bundle only if the customer can understand what each option contains and what they save. A “free gift” must actually be included when its stated conditions are met; show item/value or meaningful description, eligibility, threshold, exclusions, duration/stock limits, and whether discounts affect eligibility. CAP guidance says significant promotional conditions must be clear upfront; ASA has upheld a complaint where a spend threshold’s application after a discount was unclear. Avoid fake countdowns, fabricated scarcity, or an inflated “was” price. CMA’s current price-transparency guidance says prices must be clear, complete and accurate, with unavoidable charges included early; reference pricing must not mislead. ([Shopify bundles](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/bundles); [CMA price transparency summary, updated 7 Jan 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-transparency-cma209/providing-clear-and-accurate-information-about-prices-summary); [CAP mystery gifts and prizes](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/promotional-marketing-mystery-gifts.html); [ASA free-gift ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/the-hut-com-ltd-a21-1121849-the-hut-com-ltd.html))

**Sticky ATC.** Treat it as a navigation hypothesis. It can obscure controls or invite adding the wrong variant if unsynchronized. Compare it with an in-flow button using task success and accessibility checks. It must mirror variant/quantity and availability, work by keyboard, expose state to assistive technology, avoid covering content, and confirm activation.

**Homepage dismissal.** Test traffic may go straight to a product page, but visitors can still arrive at the homepage via logo, search, or social. It should identify the seller and route to products/support. Check actual entry paths before minimizing it.

## Rights, proof, and trust

**Competitor photography and “regenerated” images.** The tutorial’s “similar but not identical” threshold is not a legal safe harbour. UK copyright arises automatically in original photography, illustration, text, and software; copying is defined broadly as reproducing a work, and an adaptation can raise rights issues. Feeding a competitor image into an image generator does not itself establish permission, originality, or non-infringement. Use product photography supplied under a commercial licence, commissioned images with written usage rights, or original images made from the actual product. Retain licences/releases and source files. Analyse generic section structure as a design reference. Private commercial use alone does not establish a copyright exception or permission to copy protected material; publishing the screenshot's text, images, logo or distinctive artwork requires its own rights analysis. The legal status of a broad layout idea differs from copying protected expression and depends on the particulars; avoid declaring either that every similar layout is illegal or that changing a photo makes copying safe. ([GOV.UK copyright overview](https://www.gov.uk/copyright/overview); [Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, s.17](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/17); [IPO on using others’ IP](https://www.gov.uk/using-somebody-elses-intellectual-property/copyright))

**Review text and avatars.** A sample review is acceptable as clearly labelled prototype placeholder content in a private mock-up. It must not appear to shoppers as an actual customer review. Before publication, obtain genuine feedback tied to a real customer/product, permission to use the wording and image, and records supporting attribution and accuracy. The ASA requires documentary evidence that testimonials are genuine and permission to feature them in ads. Since April 2025 the DMCC Act regime also prohibits fake reviews, concealed incentivised reviews, and misleading review information; businesses publishing reviews must take reasonable and proportionate steps to prevent them. A stock avatar beside a real review can imply a real reviewer and should not be used to manufacture that impression. Better early trust cues are verifiable business/contact details, accurate delivery and returns terms, real product photos, and a clear explanation that the brand is new. Do not invent review counts, star averages, “verified” badges, or customer photos. Genuine early buyers may be invited neutrally for honest feedback; don’t make incentives conditional on a positive review, and disclose any permitted incentive. ([CMA short guide to review obligations](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews/short-guide-for-businesses-publishing-consumer-reviews-and-complying-with-consumer-protection-law); [ASA testimonials and endorsements, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/testimonials-and-endorsements.html))

**Sale banners.** A competitor’s seasonal banner is a styling example, not permission to imply a sale or borrow its brand assets. Any “sale,” crossed-out price, percentage reduction, deadline, or scarcity claim needs a real basis and records. Show the current total price and material terms where a purchase decision is invited. Use a neutral announcement (“New collection” or genuine shipping information) until an actual promotion exists.

## Mobile-first acceptance criteria

Before sending meaningful test traffic, a shopper on a small mobile viewport should be able to:

1. Identify the seller and real product; see sharp product images and know which artwork is licensed.
2. Read the current price, mandatory charges (or how to calculate variable delivery), arrival timing, and returns terms.
3. Select an available variant/quantity and add it once. Sticky ATC stays synchronized and gives clear feedback.
4. Understand bundle contents, total, savings basis, and gift conditions. Cart and checkout preserve the offer and totals.
5. Reach support, contact, privacy, delivery, and returns with readable text, contrast, keyboard focus, labelled controls, and screen-reader names.
6. Complete product → variant → cart → checkout on a real phone without broken images, overflow, duplicate adds, hidden fees, or stale stock/discount state. Check performance on a throttled connection.

## Better AI-assisted build loop

Use the screenshot as a component inventory. Prompt with shopper questions, product facts, constraints, and test objective; ask for a simple hierarchy and only the sections needed. Prohibit invented claims, reviews, sales, materials, delivery promises, and trust marks. Ask for assumptions and missing facts before copy, and keep price/variant/inventory logic separate from decoration. Test real tasks (“choose a size,” “find delivered total,” “explain gift conditions,” “change variant in cart”), then inspect mobile accessibility and checkout. AI speeds prototyping and QA; it cannot provide rights, substantiation, or customer evidence. Visual resemblance implies no universal conversion lift.

## Unit model and worked examples

Let `P` be the customer's total payment, `C` supplier product plus outbound shipping, `f` the card percentage, `k` its fixed fee, `r` the full-refund share of initial orders, `q` the separate free-replacement share, `L` variable support/handling labor valued per initial order, and `A` CAC per acquired order. In this deliberately conservative model, a refunded order loses all `P`, its original processing fee and first fulfillment; replacement fulfillment is an additional `q*C`. No returned stock, reimbursement, supplier refund, chargeback, tax, or postage recovery is assumed. Expected contribution before CAC and fixed overhead is:

`M = P*(1-r) - (f*P+k) - C*(1+q) - L`.

Variable-cost break-even ROAS on initial gross receipts is `P/M` when `M > 0`; the CAC ceiling at target profit `T` per initial order is `M-T`. Target-profit ROAS is `P/(M-T)`. ROAS is revenue divided by ad spend, so these are algebraic ceilings, not performance benchmarks. Monthly shop/apps/domain and fixed setup labor still need recovery from orders; where `M-T <= 0`, the target is impossible at that price/cost combination.

Chosen generic scenario: £29.99 delivered sale; £9 landed supplier cost; 2% + £0.25 payment fee; 8% full refunds; 2% separate replacements; £0.75 valued variable labor (three minutes at £15/hour). These assumptions produce £16.81 contribution before acquisition and fixed costs, 1.78 break-even ROAS, and £11.81 maximum CAC if the desired residual is £5/order (2.54 ROAS at that CAC). At £15 CAC it retains £1.81 before fixed costs, so paid traffic at that level fails the £5 floor. Actual CAC and conversion rate are unknown. Compare acquisition spend per initial paid acquired order with this refund-adjusted initial-order contribution. Track retained orders separately; actual profit uses retained receipts and all incurred cohort costs. The calculator JSON exposes each input.

Bundle illustration: two of the same item ordinarily total £59.98 and cost £18 fulfillment. A bundle at £49.99 with the same £18 fulfillment and same refund/replacement/labor assumptions yields about £25.63 contribution per basket. The two-unit full-price same-basket control yields about £34.62. Thus this chosen 16.7% discount sacrifices approximately £8.99 contribution per basket (26.0%) before acquisition and fixed costs. It is not evidence bundles lift conversion, add units, or improve profit. A bundle wins only if the change in retained-order rate, order size, CAC, or handling cost compensates for the lost margin. Compare basket contribution per visitor or randomized eligible sessions against the same-basket control; do not celebrate average order value alone.

# Paid testing: what a small budget can tell us

A small paid test asks whether a particular product, presentation, price, audience, placement and checkout produced enough buying behaviour to justify another test. It cannot establish that “the product is a winner” in isolation. Clicks and add-to-carts are diagnostic, not demand proven at the price; attributed purchases are orders, not profit. Profitable acquisition needs mature, reconciled economics. A diagnostic learning test can be useful earlier if its weaker conclusion is explicit.

## Define the economic hurdle first

Use contribution after variable costs as the acquisition ceiling. The dossier’s illustrative £72 crew yields £28.53 before acquisition/labour and £16.03 after chosen £10 acquisition and £2.50 labour; its £45 tee yields £3.19 after the same assumptions. These are not validated costs, demand or CAC. At £20 ad CAC, the crew would leave £6.0338 after the modeled variable labour, before fixed costs. That passes a separate £5 sensitivity and fails the prior chosen £18 floor; it does not fail the £26.0338 variable-cost break-even ceiling. The £18 floor and approximately £8 target CAC are prior planning choices, not user-confirmed requirements or benchmarks. Include fees, fulfilment, discounts, refunds, replacements, service and labour; update with actuals before claiming profit.

Platform ROAS is attributed revenue divided by spend under platform rules. It omits fulfilment, fees, refunds and labour; view-through or modeled attribution may not mean the ad caused the order. Keep a store ledger with date, SKU, paid amount, discount, fees, fulfilment, cancellations/refunds/replacements and source evidence. Freeze the attribution window and report platform figures separately from observed orders. Recent orders remain provisional until the practical refund/return window passes; show gross and mature net orders side by side.

## What a £100 or £250 cap can establish

A cap limits exposure; it does not promise a sample size. We have no defensible CPC or conversion benchmark. As purely hypothetical arithmetic, at realized CPC £0.50, £100 buys 200 clicks and £250 buys 500; at £1 they buy 100 and 250. At a hypothetical 1% purchase rate, that implies expected orders of 1, 2, 5 or 2.5. These are not forecasts, and actual counts are noisy. Even 0/100 purchases is compatible with a true rate near 3% at the approximate 95% “rule of three”, assuming independent, correctly measured clicks.

A £100 test can expose broken checkout, wrong geography or irrelevant clicks and may produce purchase evidence. The cash cap alone cannot establish or rule out a reliable low-CAC result; that depends on actual orders, uncertainty, attribution, mature costs and repeatability. £250 adds information but guarantees neither orders nor significance. A no-sale result says something about this setup only if delivery, tracking and checkout worked; it cannot isolate product from creative or offer. Log spend and denominators at each step: impressions/reach, outbound clicks, landing views, add-to-carts, checkouts and paid orders. A drop between stages suggests possible loading, message, offer, shipping or payment friction; it does not prove a cause.

## Keep the experiment interpretable

Use one product, one factual page and at most two distinct creative concepts. Fix price, shipping promise, audience, placements, dates and page. If delivery is allocated unevenly, ad comparison is an optimization outcome, not a clean randomized test. A formal split can randomize exposure; Meta describes A/B tests as measuring impact, while TikTok’s tools optimize delivery. Even a randomized split estimates those executions for that audience and period, not inherent product demand or each creative ingredient separately. With sparse orders, avoid many audiences, offers or page variants.

Meta and TikTok describe learning phases as enough optimization events and time for delivery systems to adapt. TikTok’s July 2026 budget page distinguishes average daily willingness-to-spend from a lifetime total cap (which can deliver unevenly); its August auction guidance conditions budget on target CPA and conversion volume. Those are delivery recommendations, not proof that the business can afford that CPA or authorization to spend. Meta’s often-repeated event threshold is likewise a delivery heuristic, not a universal minimum sample for product learning. A small test can diagnose a funnel; a profit claim needs mature purchases compared with the economic ceiling and uncertainty.

## Set a stopping rule before launch

Choose a hard cash cap, an end date, and the decision before seeing results. Do not keep checking daily significance and stop the first time one variant looks ahead. Repeated looks and trying many products, audiences, creatives, prices or metrics make a lucky result more likely; selecting the best observed result also inflates its apparent effect (“winner’s curse”). Kohavi et al.’s review of controlled web experiments and Deng et al.’s work on winner’s curse in online tests discuss trustworthy design and selection bias. For a tiny founder test, the practical controls are simpler: preselect one primary metric (mature paid-order contribution per pound, if enough orders; otherwise a named diagnostic transition), limit to two concepts, preserve all attempts in a log, and treat apparent winners as candidates for replication. Stop immediately for broken tracking, material product/page inaccuracies, unexpected spend or checkout failure. Otherwise spend only to the predeclared cap/date; do not raise it just because early ROAS looks exciting. If results are inconclusive, stop and label the next hypothesis rather than repeatedly extending the same test.

A fixed “day 1/2/3” ladder sometimes used in tutorials (tolerate no order on day one, expect an order on day two, demand above-break-even ROAS on day three, then scale budget by 20%) is a decision rule, not evidence that three days contain enough information. Daily delivery, purchase lag and low order counts make day-to-day ROAS especially noisy; even a high revenue ROAS can lose money after refunds and variable costs. The video's USD400 spend at the day-three checkpoint is not a verified daily budget, full-test cost, GBP budget recommendation or safe threshold for this store. Use a longer, predeclared window where affordable and wait for purchase/refund maturity before profit conclusions. Scaling by 20% after a thin apparent win compounds spend on noise and changes the tested conditions; first replicate within a separately authorized cap.

## Affordable route for this case

First approve the exact crew sample and identical reorder, confirm the configured supplier cost and rights, then validate the factual page and events in test mode. Any later authorized live diagnostic must reconcile its real click, initial order, refund and cost records. For Meta, verify Pixel/Conversions API event receipt and deduplication if both are used; TikTok likewise requires a correctly connected pixel/events setup for web conversions. Capture an order ID so store records, refunds and platform reports can be reconciled. Do not buy traffic until the page truthfully states garment, sizing, materials, delivery, returns and seller identity, and checkout has been walked through on mobile.

Choose a route based on the learning question. The reviewed first paid cap is £75; the £100 and £250 examples above are comparisons:

- **Paid diagnostic:** the final proposed £500 route uses one platform, one SKU, one delivered price and at most two concepts, with £75 total traffic. Verify purchase tracking and the platform's enforceable cash minimum first. Report funnel counts, initial paid acquired orders, retained receipts and actual costs. A further £175 exists only in the alternative £1,000 plan after reviewing what a repeat can resolve and preserving working cash. The £100 and £250 figures above are arithmetic comparisons, not additive allocations. The result tests this ad-and-page package; profitable acquisition needs mature costs and compatible denominators.
- **Organic:** publish factual demonstrations and track unique links/codes, sessions and orders. Cash cost can be low, but reach is uncontrolled and audience/timing confound comparisons. Zero reach is not a demand verdict; views and likes are not purchase evidence.
- **Creator sample or paid post:** use a small, agreed fee/sample budget, unique link/code, and written permission for any paid reuse. It tests that creator’s audience and execution; code use misses uncredited conversions and does not prove incremental sales. Do not describe free product or a paid post as independent endorsement.

For the current unvalidated crew, the best near-term value is a verified factual page plus a capped diagnostic that answers one question. Reserve a claim of “profitable acquisition” for a later, preplanned test with enough mature orders to compare net contribution against spend and the chosen acquisition ceiling. If product economics make that ceiling too low for affordable testing, that is itself useful evidence: investigate price, costs or a cheaper channel before purchasing more traffic.

## Pillow evidence and claims boundary

The neck-pain hook has a burden that product specs cannot answer. A randomized crossover trial enrolled 70 adults with chronic nonspecific neck pain; 64 completed. It compared a particular viscoelastic polyurethane pillow with 60 springs against education while participants used their own pillows, with four-week periods and a four-week washout. Neck pain improved by 8.7 points on a 0 to 100 scale relative to the comparator (95% CI 2.6 to 14.7 points of improvement); shoulder pain did not differ significantly. The authors noted that medication, actual pillow use and adherence to advice were not controlled. These findings do not establish efficacy for an unspecified memory-foam or cloud pillow. [Trial abstract and full-text link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30939188/)

The 2021 systematic review included 35 articles, with nine high-quality studies involving 555 participants. It reported benefits for rubber/spring pillows on pain, disability and satisfaction; the sleep-quality findings were inconclusive, and shape/height effects on cervical alignment remained uncertain. The cited PMC record is a conference abstract of that work; the full paper supports a more specific conclusion than mixed evidence overall. These results do not validate an unspecified memory-foam product. [Full systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33895703/) UK CAP guidance says objective product claims require documentary evidence held before publication, and ASA guidance says claims that a product treats or relieves pain are likely to be treated as medical-device claims. Before any neck-pain proposition, check the exact product, intended purpose, claim wording, evidence and applicable regulatory route. An ordinary pillow is not automatically a medical device. CAP's pain page contains an outdated marking deadline; use current MHRA requirements, which distinguish Great Britain from Northern Ireland. [MHRA medical-device requirements](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/medical-devices-how-to-comply-with-the-legal-requirements). For this first test, avoid pain, injury, posture correction, “orthopaedic” efficacy and sleep-improvement promises. [CAP substantiation rule 3.7](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html) [ASA pain guidance](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/health-pain.html)

## Truth, rights and obligations

AI lifestyle images must not imply construction, contour, dimensions, packaging or outcomes absent from the delivered product. Catalog “bestseller” labels, marketplace stars, sales counters and sales estimates are platform or seller signals, not evidence of our demand. # Tool stack: what the “10-minute store” costs

Research checked 1 October 2026 (Europe/London). Prices are in their displayed currencies; no conversion is applied. Trials and software signals do not establish sales.

## Recommendation

For a first test, use one Shopify store, Shopify’s free Dawn theme and existing AI/chat/browser tools to build and inspect one honest product page. Keep the free Shopify Bundles app available if the offer truly needs a fixed bundle. Do not buy Atlas, Rapi and an upper TrendTrack tier together before defining a question each answers. The software can shorten page assembly and competitor browsing; it cannot supply product rights, verified claims, supplier reliability, customer service, delivery performance or demand. A published page is not a completed operating business.

The relevant paid Shopify floor is Shopify itself: its UK page lists Basic at £25/month billed monthly or £19/month when billed yearly. Dawn is free. A one-month Basic + Dawn setup is therefore £25 before payment charges, domain, fulfilment, advertising and any app. Annual Basic commits £228 upfront for the year (equivalent to £19/month) and is poor test economics if the store might close after one month. Shopify’s introductory offer currently advertises three days free followed by £1/month for three months, subject to account eligibility and offer terms. This may reduce early cash cost; £25/month is the ongoing listed monthly price. Confirm applicable tax and the actual checkout charge before budgeting. [Shopify UK pricing](https://www.shopify.com/uk/pricing) and [Dawn](https://themes.shopify.com/themes/dawn/presets/dawn), checked 1 Oct 2026.

Free to install for development stores, then $39/month, 7-day trial, plus additional charges as the store generates “revenue and impact”; USD billed every 30 days. No annual discount is shown. **GetHookd** is used with Similarweb in the July product-research tutorial. ## Costs and simpler alternatives

At listed ongoing monthly rates, Basic + Atlas + Rapi Starter + GetHookd Starter totals £25 + $83 per month before TrendTrack, Atlas usage charges, applicable tax/FX, payment fees, domain, products, shipping and ads. Add a verified TrendTrack quote separately. These tools are optional, and trials or introductory offers may reduce the first charge; the expression is not a complete startup budget. ## Decision for our cases

For the premium anime/dark-fantasy apparel concept, Atlas and bundles do not resolve licensing, sample quality, the unvalidated £72 price, fulfilment or willingness to pay. Build one page with Dawn and licensed/original imagery. Use GetHookd only if it can answer a defined ad-pattern question; consider paying for TrendTrack only if its available free access and existing research methods cannot answer a defined competitor question. Neither makes competitor activity validated demand.

For an external merchant-facing store-builder product, buying Atlas to validate our own-store revenue is a category error: that tests one merchant’s product economics, not whether merchants pay for store-generation software. Interview/observe merchants or prototype the page workflow before paying for their SaaS subscriptions. A builder may cut layout labour from hours to minutes; the operational work remains: choose legal/available stock, set accurate price/shipping/returns, connect checkout and payments, verify tax and policies, test mobile/accessibility, place and fulfil a test order, handle cancellations/refunds, and provide support. A store that looks complete can still fail at any of those steps.

**Recommendation:** start with Basic monthly + Dawn and one page; keep all add-on apps uninstalled until a specific bottleneck is measured. If we need to compare price or page angles, define the audience, spend/time ceiling, and decision rule first. Add one tool only when its output changes a decision that we cannot make with existing tools. Keep creator screenshots and sales claims separate from vendor capability statements, and never enter competitor assets, testimonials, trust marks or revenue figures as our own.

# Video evidence: Perry Xie’s four-video dropshipping workflow

Research date: 1 October 2026. Evidence reviewed: full auto-caption files for four Perry Xie videos, deduplicated into private working transcripts; video metadata and descriptions. Captions can mishear words and are not proof of displayed dashboards. Timestamps below point to the public videos and are rounded to the nearest second. The source files are not reproduced here.

## What the videos establish

The videos show how the creator wants a novice to move from ad-library research to a Shopify product page and a Meta test. They do not establish a general probability that the method finds a profitable product. The dedicated selection tutorial is the best description of his screening logic; the first speedrun restates that logic while creating a grounding-sheet offer, and the second speedrun adds a short performance heuristic. The main tutorial demonstrates store construction after a product has already been selected. In the main video he explicitly says he has selected the product already and sends viewers to the previous product research video (b43_Gs64mYM, 01:38 to 02:03). Thus it is evidence of a build process, not a measured comparison of AI-built stores against other stores.

## Selection method and weaknesses

In “here’s how i find winning products…” he opens Get Hooked and Similarweb (5slU3QjvzI8, 06:42 onward). The visible and narrated filter is US, the top two performance tiers, no more than five ads per brand, image format, still-running status, and at least 25 days’ runtime (06:54 to 07:42). He manually decides which resulting ads are “native,” clicks through to identify a product, then asks whether sourcing cost is low, shipping is cheap because the item is small/light, buyers might want multiples, the item solves a real problem, and the addressable market is large (03:48 to 06:42; 08:04 to 11:26). These are useful questions, but several are qualitative and no scorecard or cutoff is supplied. The paid research tool’s performance ranking is treated as evidence of an advertiser’s success without a disclosed mapping from rank or time-live to sales or profit.

He then estimates a competitor store’s monthly revenue from Similarweb visits, an assumed 3% conversion rate and displayed product price (11:28 to 13:07). In the demo, he starts with 105,000 visits across a displayed period, speculates the tool undercounts and revises the total to 150,000 to 200,000, chooses 60,000 monthly visits and 3% conversion, infers 1,800 orders, multiplies by $77 and calls the result $138,000/month (11:51 to 13:01). That is arithmetic on assumptions, not observed sales. The stated traffic adjustment has no validation, conversion rate is not sourced to this store, and one product price may not equal order value. He calls the example “untapped” after checking a keyword in Meta Ad Library, finding the selected brand and not recognizing one other result as the same item (13:05 to 14:02). This is a narrow, search-term-dependent competitor count, not a market-wide denominator. He suggests compatible gifts and checking Amazon listings as further offer signals (14:04 to 16:00), but neither validates incremental margin or demand.

Speedrun part one explicitly switches from GetHookd to TrendTrack (01:51 to 02:36) and offers a second filter recipe: active, at least 20 days running, image, US, English, and ad copy length of at least 1,000, described by the creator as words; the filter's actual unit was not independently checked, sorted by ad rank (IbObinWjfHg, 02:46 to 04:01). The creator says this length guarantees native ads; that is his classification rule, not proof every qualifying ad is native or profitable. Its 20-day cutoff differs from the dedicated tutorial’s 25-day cutoff. He repeats cheap/light/small, multiple-purchase, problem-solving and market-size checks (04:11 to 06:15). In this tutorial, he also flags that the grounding-sheet product’s health claims lack enough scientific support, then says to check whether a product mechanism actually works and claims can be supported (05:00 to 06:10). That caution is sound as a research principle; it does not substantiate the specific health or sales claims shown in the example.

## What the paid-test lesson says

Part two (oH2r5KUTRzo) creates three “desire” ad sets, each with three ads, giving nine ads total. The set varies long-form copy/hook by desire and uses three image variants (26:28 to 28:15). The spoken evaluation rule is: day one, a purchase is welcome but not expected; day two, seek at least one purchase even below break-even ROAS; day three, seek purchases above break-even ROAS (28:31 to 29:09). He shows one day-three example at 3.39 ROAS after $400 spend (29:09 to 29:17), says to raise campaign budget by 20% at midnight when above break-even, and suggests a further 20% within 24 hours when ROAS exceeds twice break-even; when performance dips, wait 24 hours and reduce 20% (29:17 to 30:06). For a nonperformer he refers to days three to five, “desire 4” and “desire 5,” and no purchases, but the surrounding wording is caption-uncertain and no detailed threshold is given (30:35 to 30:54). The title/description of part two promises an entire launch and scaling strategy, but its conclusion says performance evaluation and doubling down require a separate comprehensive video (28:15 to 28:31). The result is an illustrative rule of thumb, not a fully specified or independently replicated protocol.

The test recommendation cannot be costed from these videos alone. They do not give a standard daily budget or acquisition-cost ceiling, number of products tried, complete spend and results for failed tests, attribution window, audience/placement controls, or refund, chargeback, fulfilment and fee treatment. ROAS is gross attributed revenue divided by ad spend; break-even ROAS depends on contribution margin and is not a universal threshold. A single $400 day-three example does not tell us what a typical test costs or how often one works.

## Claims, presentation, incentives

The main video’s title says “$200k in 30 days,” while the opening instead says one store did $197,000 in 50 days and another did $143,000 in 30 days (b43_Gs64mYM, 00:00 to 00:22). These statements could describe different windows or stores, but the video does not reconcile the title with the opening. Across the clips the creator also self-reports $3m+ clothing-brand sales, a “100% hit rate” across “many stores,” and multiple six-/seven-figure store results. No store list, number of attempts, gross-versus-net definitions, costs or underlying records are shown in the captions reviewed. They should remain labelled self-reported and unverified, rather than treated as proof of deception or as outcome data.

The videos have commercial links: the main description promotes TrendTrack, Atlas, Rapid Bundles, a private supplier and a paid “Luxury Tools” bundle; the dedicated and speedrun videos promote the paid tools bundle, while speedrun part one mentions Atlas and an eight-day trial. Such links create a promotional incentive to recommend tools and simplify setup; they do not by themselves invalidate instruction. The main video also says prompts are available in the free Discord. This evidence memo does not reproduce those prompts or infer their contents.

## Relevance to our decision

Transfer the low-cost, compact/light, multi-unit-purchase, genuine-problem and market-size questions as a preliminary screen, and use competitor ads as qualitative inspiration. Do not treat ad longevity, a tool rank, estimated competitor revenue, or an AI-built page as proof of our conversion or profitability. For our understated clothing concept, the showcased grounding-device logic transfers only weakly: clothing fit, embroidery quality, delivery time, sizing returns and actual willingness to pay matter more than whether customers need several units. Our current margin scenarios already show acquisition cost and after-sale costs can consume contribution; any spend decision needs our own break-even calculation and a small, pre-capped validation design. This is consistent with the brief’s unconfirmed GBP500 planning example: it is not permission to spend, and the video provides no evidence that its $400 example is an appropriate budget for us.

# An AI workflow we can actually operate

Use Perry Xie's four videos as a sequence to examine: shortlist a product, investigate the offer, build a page, then test acquisition. Replace the implied shortcut from competitor activity to a "winner" with evidence gates. The useful transfer is faster research and assembly; the videos provide no controlled estimate of conversion lift or typical profitability. His product-research lesson uses ad longevity and estimated traffic, the main lesson builds a selected product, and the speedruns demonstrate selection and advertising heuristics. Keep those roles distinct. [Public main tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b43_Gs64mYM), [selection tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5slU3QjvzI8), [first speedrun](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IbObinWjfHg), [second speedrun](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oH2r5KUTRzo).

Our first deliverable should be one documented offer and a reviewable draft page. Use existing Codex agents for bounded research, browser inspection for public evidence, local Markdown/CSV/JSON files for records, and an existing Shopify account with a free theme for implementation when authorized. Dawn is free; Shopify itself has a subscription cost. Native discounts and the free Shopify Bundles app can cover a simple fixed offer. A separate builder, ad-intelligence subscription or bundle app needs a measured reason. [Dawn](https://themes.shopify.com/themes/dawn/presets/dawn), [Shopify UK pricing](https://www.shopify.com/uk/pricing), [Shopify Bundles](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/bundles/shopify-bundles).

## Eight stages, with explicit handoffs

Each stage produces a small file another agent can check. The prompts below are original working prompts for us. They do not reproduce the creator's Discord material; this research has no authenticated access to those private prompts.

**1. Selection signals.** Save `candidates.csv` with `candidate_id, exact_item, market, observed_signal, source_url, observed_at, signal_type, sample_check, supply_gap, claim_burden, margin_gap, next_question, decision`. An active ad is an observation; a vendor's performance rank is a proprietary proxy. Neither belongs in a verified-sales column. Shortlist at most two candidates, including reasons to reject them. Do not estimate revenue by multiplying guessed conversion by traffic.

> Inspect these public ads and pages. Record what is directly visible, the observation date and URL. Separate seller claims from evidence. Compare inspectability, delivered-cost uncertainty, returns and substitution. Recommend the next missing fact for each candidate, without calling any a winner.

**2. Primary facts and provenance.** Save `facts.json` per exact variant: `product_id, variant_id, fact_name, value, units, market, source_owner, source_url_or_file, captured_at, verified_by, status, recheck_date, rights_basis`. Use statuses `verified`, `supplier_claim`, `unknown`, `conflicting`. Attach supplier documents and sample notes when available. Resolve material conflicts before public copy. Price, availability and variant information must agree across page, checkout and product data. [Google product data specification](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7052112?hl=en).

> Extract exact variant facts from these primary documents. Cite each value. Preserve unknowns and conflicts. List the questions a sample or supplier answer must resolve. Do not infer materials, delivery guarantees, image rights or efficacy from a competitor listing.

**3. Customer hypotheses.** Save `hypotheses.md`: `hypothesis_id, audience, buying_situation, proposed_reason_to_buy, likely_objection, evidence_seen, alternative_explanation, measurable_action, disconfirming_result`. Customer language from public reviews can suggest questions, but belongs to those reviewers and products. Keep a proposed objection distinct from an observed one.

> Using only this fact ledger, write two competing reasons someone might buy at the proposed delivered price. For each, state the objection, competing alternative and evidence that would change our decision. Label all inferred buyer beliefs as hypotheses.

**4. Original page and creative.** Save `page-spec.md` and `creative-register.csv`: `asset_id, version, owner, rights, source_facts, claim_ids, actual_photo_or_synthetic, intended_use`. Analyse competitor section order as a layout hypothesis, then write from our facts. Use our own art and accurate sample photography. Leave absent reviews absent. An AI lifestyle image cannot establish embroidery quality, dimensions or performance. Objective claims need substantiation before publication. [CAP substantiation rules](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html).

> Draft a mobile product page from verified facts. Include price, variants, sizing or dimensions, delivery, returns and seller information. Flag missing facts rather than filling them. Suggest two original creative concepts using assets we own; attach every factual claim to its ledger entry.

**5. Mobile checkout QA.** Save `qa.csv`: `case_id, device_viewport, page_version, variant, expected, observed, evidence_file, severity, owner, resolved_at`. Walk product selection, cart quantity, shipping calculation, checkout total, payment errors and confirmation. Check text zoom, keyboard controls, unavailable variants and delivery/returns visibility. Use test mode only when access and implementation are authorized; a screenshot review cannot verify a payment transaction. Reconcile a test order and refund path with events before buying traffic.

> Inspect this draft at narrow mobile width. Compare displayed price, selected variant, stock and delivery with the ledger. Record reproducible failures with screenshots. Distinguish visually inspected steps from actual test transactions. Stop publication for wrong totals, unsupported claims or a broken checkout.

**6. Versioned experiment.** Save `experiment.json`: `id, version, product, audience, channel, page_hash, creative_versions, price, dates, primary_metric, attribution_rule, cash_cap, founder_hour_cap, stop_conditions, interpretation_rules, authorization_status`. Freeze one offer and at most two creative concepts. This research authorizes preparation only. Proposed advertising or customer sessions require their own authorization and a fixed cap; a planning budget is no permission to spend.

> Turn hypothesis H1 into one bounded experiment. Identify what stays fixed, the primary metric and denominators, cash and hour ceilings, failure stops and the weaker conclusion possible with few orders. Do not invent expected CPC, conversion or CAC.

**7. Retained contribution.** Save `orders.csv`: `order_id, experiment_id, paid_amount, discount, tax, payment_fee, fulfilment, refund, replacement, chargeback, variable_labour, acquisition_allocation, status, maturity_date, contribution`. Keep customer identifiers outside research exports. Separate platform-attributed revenue from store orders and mature retained orders. Count failed tests and shared subscriptions in the portfolio ledger.

> Reconcile these anonymised orders with spend. Calculate net receipts minus variable costs and acquisition, marking missing costs and immature orders. Show fixed experiment costs separately. Report gross revenue, retained contribution and uncertainty without describing ROAS as profit.

**8. Learn.** Save `decision.md`: `experiment_id, observed_counts, missing_data, comparison_to_rule, alternative_explanations, stop_or_repeat, next_change, remaining_cash, hours_used`. An inconclusive result is a valid stopping outcome. Repeat only through a separately bounded decision.

> Apply the preregistered rule to the recorded counts and mature economics. Separate evidence from possible explanations. Choose one next action and the smallest missing observation that would justify it. Preserve rejected attempts in the record.

## Ten questions worth resolving

1. Which exact blank or functional SKU are we selling, and which advertised attributes are verified on that variant?
2. What does a physical sample show about stitch quality, fit, capacity or construction that supplier images hide?
3. Which supplier can fulfil UK orders, and what documented handling, shipping and return terms apply?
4. What is the actual landed cost for one order, including the proposed delivery promise?
5. Which comparable alternative makes our delivered price difficult to justify?
6. What specific buying situation could make the original design or functional difference valuable?
7. Which objection requires product proof, and which requires clearer page information?
8. Can a mobile buyer identify the correct variant, total price and expected delivery before paying?
9. Which orders survive refunds and replacements, and what contribution remains after service and acquisition?
10. Does an unrelated merchant have a recurring problem our checker solves better than Shopify's native tools, and will they pay for it?

## Where the method transfers

For our original dark-fantasy clothing, prioritise rights, sample appearance, measurements and the proposed £72 delivered price. AI can organise evidence and draft accurate copy; it cannot feel fabric or validate willingness to pay. For a compact organiser, prioritise exact dimensions, a repeatable real demonstration, supplier delivery and price comparison. A useful-looking commodity can still have poor contribution. Bundles need an actual use case and margin comparison, rather than an assumption that customers want multiples.

A synthetic AI harness is a controlled factual check. Store its prompt set, model/version, browsing configuration, source snapshot, output and scoring rubric. Measure contradictions, omissions and unsupported claims. It is neither live ChatGPT shopping search nor a real-buyer proxy. A model asked to read our page has been given access; that does not demonstrate platform indexing, recommendation visibility or buying behaviour. Synthetic examples throughout this workflow are planning material, never tested results. [ChatGPT shopping guidance](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search).

Data quality is the practical limit: stale prices, wrong variant joins and unowned images propagate faster when automated. Keep primary-source dates and an accountable reviewer. Do not expose customer records, credentials or private merchant exports in prompts without a permitted processing route. Existing account access does not authorize private Discord retrieval or supplier outreach.

## When another tool earns its bill

Use a measured monthly comparison: `(manual hours - tool hours - extra QA hours) × chosen hourly value - subscription - setup cost allocation`. Hypothetically, saving four hours, adding one QA hour, valuing time at £15/hour and paying £30 yields £15/month before setup. Two setup hours valued at £15 erase the first two months' net saving. These are chosen inputs, not measured savings. Adopt only after the same tasks demonstrate savings without worse factual errors; value cash and founder time separately.

An eventual SaaS opportunity is a permissioned product-fact and mismatch workflow if external merchants repeatedly struggle with it. First compare their existing Shopify product administration, feeds, native automation and current apps. Our own store can expose errors but cannot prove external demand. A credible next gate is a working demonstration followed, when separately authorized, by an independent merchant paying for a recurring outcome. Promise fewer errors or measured time saved only when supported. Do not promise AI ranking gains from a local model check or build a broad scraped database before that paid problem survives the native-platform comparison.

# Apply the method without confusing speed with a business

Research application, 1 October 2026. This is a proposed plan. The current authorization covers research only: no purchases, ads, supplier outreach, participant recruitment or customer experiments. All prices, allocations and test thresholds below are chosen planning hypotheses unless attributed to a source. UK seller, UK buyers and no VAT registration remain assumptions.

## Choose a lead and a challenger

Keep the original understated dark-fantasy embroidered crew as the lead. Its strongest potential distinction is a piece someone wants to wear because of the original artwork, restraint and garment quality. The inherited £72 delivered price is an unvalidated hypothesis. It needs evidence that the exact garment feels worth that amount, rather than a page that merely looks premium. Choose one original motif, one garment colour and the smallest workable size range after inspecting the supplier chart. Those are scope choices, not validated demand findings; do not call this a limited edition without a real limit.

Use a compact workday cable/charger organiser as the functional challenger. No exact SKU, selling price, capacity, supplier or landed cost is verified. The [product and supply memo](https://silmoon04.github.io/ecommerce-ai-seo-research/dropshipping-test/research/product-and-supply.md) favours this category for inspectable utility and lower claims burden. A proposed offer is one pouch for a clearly specified everyday device set, with photographs showing what fits. Avoid a launch bundle: the customer needs to understand the single item before we know whether multiples solve anything.

For a commodity organiser, a prettier page is insufficient distinction. The minimum credible value is documented compatibility with a useful device set, accurate dimensions, an honest packing demonstration, dependable delivery and a workable returns service at a total price the buyer accepts relative to alternatives. If we cannot provide that, the challenger should stop. This does not require a patented product, but it requires a reason to choose this seller that survives comparison. Original embroidered artwork offers another route to distinction, provided quality supports the premium.

The eventual clothing brand can build a coherent collection, identity and repeat purchases. A temporary learning store answers a narrower question about an offer and buying process. Do not attach an unrelated commodity pouch to the premium brand just to save a subscription. Prototype the crew privately and keep the organiser as a written comparison. Only the crew receives sample, page, creative and traffic allocations in this proposed round. Reuse tooling, research and accounting across the portfolio; branding should match the actual experiment.

## Adopt, change and defer the four-video method

Adopt the sequence: inspect competitor ads and pages, screen for product usefulness and fulfilment constraints, build quickly with AI, then test a specific offer. The [four-video evidence memo](https://silmoon04.github.io/ecommerce-ai-seo-research/dropshipping-test/research/video-evidence.md) reconstructs the selection tutorial, both speedruns and the main builder tutorial. Its small/light, genuine-problem and margin questions are useful screening prompts. Clothing needs additional fit, stitch, originality and identity checks. # A capped evidence ladder instead of a three-day winner rule

Prepared 1 October 2026. This is a proposed research protocol; no customer recruitment, purchases, advertising or account changes are authorized. Amounts in GBP are chosen planning caps, not forecasts. The video's day-three example is **USD400**, with reported 3.39 revenue ROAS, and is not a GBP budget recommendation. Its day-one/day-two/day-three purchase expectations and 20% budget increases come from the speedrun's short illustrative lesson, rather than a documented sequence of profitable retained-order cohorts. [Speedrun part two](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oH2r5KUTRzo&t=1711s)

## Define the thing being tested

The unit is an offer package: exact SKU and variant, price, shipping terms, page version, creative concept, audience, channel, attribution settings and dates. A poor result cannot identify an inherently bad product when the creative or checkout failed. A good result does not establish that every audience or price will work. Keep one lead SKU and at most two creative concepts. Freeze the offer and page during a paid cohort; fixes create a new version. A platform allocating most spend to one ad has optimized delivery, not randomized a fair comparison. Use an actual split test only when the budget can support its question; otherwise describe each execution and its observed counts. [Controlled web experiments](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1)

The original embroidered crew remains the lead hypothesis because it has inspectable supply and an existing cost model. That does not establish willingness to pay the proposed GBP72. A functional organiser becomes a challenger only after a named supplier's costs and service terms are evidenced. Do not run these as simultaneous nine-ad campaigns on a tiny portfolio budget. A single coherent store can share factual seller information and infrastructure, while each product keeps its own costs, versions and outcomes.

## Resolve cheap uncertainties first

Before paid exposure, spend founder time on the exact sample, supplier quote, materials, dimensions or size chart, delivery, returns and rights. Use the four-hour supplier/sample/rights/economics allocation inside the shared twenty-hour round. If a blocking question cannot be resolved within the remaining time, stop optional activity and revise the same plan rather than adding another allowance. Sample cash needs a quote and later approval. Preserve sample photos and measurements; generated lifestyle scenes must not imply unavailable product details. Defer pain-relief products whose claims require specialist evidence. [CAP substantiation](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html)

A proposed first qualitative round recruits five people who plausibly buy the category, separately from friends who mainly want to encourage the founder. Five is a practical usability allowance, not statistical coverage. Ask participants to choose a variant, explain delivered price and arrival timing, find returns and complete a mock purchase task. Let them speak before showing suggested answers. Record repeated obstacles and conflicting views, without claiming a conversion rate. Use the three-hour shared buyer-access and observations allocation for recruitment, preparation, sessions and synthesis; recruit fewer than five if access takes longer. The cash plan contains no participant incentives. Recruitment and real transactions remain outside this research authorization. Fix factual or task-blocking defects before aesthetic preferences. One later round on the changed page checks whether the original obstacle remains.

Two paid creative cuts can test distinct factual reasons to consider the same item: embroidery and texture versus understated everyday styling, or organiser capacity versus a specific packing demonstration. Recut original footage before buying more production. Keep the destination and delivered offer fixed. Click response helps diagnose a hook; it does not prove buyers will pay. Do not introduce three audiences, three desires and three images together unless there is enough resource to interpret their separate outcomes.

## Verify the measurement path

Create a versioned test card and a ledger before launch. Record spend, impressions, outbound clicks, landing views, checkout starts, paid order IDs, source markers, fees, fulfilment, refunds, replacements and valued support time. Walk the mobile product-to-checkout path, including unavailable variants and changed quantities. Test event receipt and purchase value/currency; if browser and server events are both enabled, check deduplication. Reconcile one controlled order/refund path with store records using a platform test mode where available; any live transaction needs authorization. Never interpret a campaign when a broken event path explains the apparent result.

Freeze the attribution window and report platform-attributed purchases separately from ledger orders. A tracked referral or coupon is source evidence, not proof the channel caused an incremental order. Keep campaign purchase lag visible, and leave the reporting window open after spend ends rather than judging only the last day's dashboard. AI referrals, organic search, paid ads and synthetic model checks each get separate fields. A fixed prompt suite measures fact retrieval under specified conditions; it cannot measure real shopper demand. [OpenAI publisher FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq), [Google AI search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

## Make a small paid test honest

If authorized later, use one platform, one offer, up to two concepts and a £75 total first traffic cap over a chosen seven-calendar-day window. The final £500 example has no additional traffic allocation. The alternative £1,000 example may release a further £175, bringing traffic to £250, only after a reviewed learning question, mature evidence where profit is claimed, and a cash check. Seven days is a scheduling choice, not statistical power, platform learning or profit maturity. If platform minimums cannot fit, revise or defer the test without diverting reserve. Campaign QA, ledger and interpretation use four hours inside the sole twenty-hour round. Cash and hours are portfolio totals; no SKU or application file receives another allowance.

Stop immediately for materially false claims, rights gaps, unusable checkout, wrong geography, failed events or unexpected spend. Otherwise stop at the agreed cash/date boundary. A single day without orders does not trigger an arbitrary kill, and a profitable-looking day does not release more money. Low delivery, uncertain attribution or sparse purchases may leave the result inconclusive. Preserve denominators: zero purchases among 100 independently measured relevant visits still permits a purchase rate near 3% under the approximate 95% rule of three. That illustration depends on assumptions and is not a traffic forecast. Do not convert zero tracked sales into a categorical demand verdict.

## Use mature contribution and repeat the candidate

Choose a maturity rule before observing orders: wait through the applicable delivery, cancellation/return, processing and relevant dispute exposure, label unresolved liabilities, and retain a reserve for later losses. Full chargeback maturity may take longer than a founder can wait, so publish both provisional and updated cohorts rather than calling an early dashboard final. Shopify retains original processing fees on refunded orders, which must remain in the ledger. [Shopify refund treatment](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/payouts/refunds), [UK returns guidance](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds)

Compare retained revenue minus actual fees, fulfilment, refunds, replacements and valued variable labour against acquisition spend. Also show contribution per initial paid order, contribution per eligible visit where denominator quality permits, and total portfolio cash/hours including failed offers. The inherited crew scenario gives GBP26.03 before acquisition and fixed overhead once GBP2.50 labour is included; GBP28.53 excludes that labour. The inherited chosen GBP18 floor allows GBP8.0338 CAC and requires 8.96 gross ROAS. A separate GBP5 sensitivity allows GBP21.0338 CAC and requires 3.42 ROAS; the GBP0 variable-cost break-even requires 2.77 ROAS. None is an expected CAC, validated margin or user-confirmed target. Compare contribution and CAC per initial paid acquired order, with retained receipts and actual cohort costs used for realized results. Shared subscriptions count once; cash float remains a reserve until used.

An apparent profitable candidate earns a separately capped repeat cohort with the same offer and declared source, followed by a deliberate change if needed. Two small cohorts still may be uncertain; require compatible economics and reliable fulfilment before expanding. Record every rejected creative, product and cohort. Selecting the best of many attempts exaggerates its apparent performance, so the full portfolio and replication matter. [Winner's curse research](https://www.kdd.org/kdd2018/accepted-papers/view/winners-curse-bias-estimation-for-total-effects-of-features-in-online-contr)

Finally, owned-store learning can justify an internal product-fact checker. It cannot validate a merchant SaaS. That requires independent merchants with a recurring problem, a concrete workflow and actual paid continuation. The next useful evidence is a sample and a comprehensible truthful page; the next paid decision comes after the unknown budget, economic ceiling and measurement path are settled.

The pillow evidence deserves a fairer account. ## Keep the model, correct its interpretation

The expected contribution per initial paid order is:

`M = P*(1-r) - (f*P+k) - C*(1+q) - L`

Here P is the complete customer payment, C is original delivered fulfilment, r is the fraction of initial orders fully refunded, q is a separate free-replacement frequency, and L is valued variable order labour. Full refunds remove the whole receipt. Original fees and fulfilment stay incurred; replacements add fulfilment. The base assumes no returned-stock recovery, supplier reimbursement, retailer-paid return postage or chargebacks. Partial returns need a different basket model. These assumptions describe losses; an 8% expected refund allowance does not fund the actual cash needed when two customers both require £72.

The denominator must remain initial paid acquired orders when comparing CAC with M. The economic memo's instruction to use retained orders only conflicts with its refund-adjusted initial-order model. Retained-customer CAC is also useful, but requires contribution expressed on that same retained-customer basis. For actual results, use the cohort's retained receipts and incurred costs directly, including refunded customers' acquisition spend. Never deduct ads once through CAC and again as fixed test spend.

The inherited crew cost belongs to the Inkthreadable Changer scenario. Printful examples establish an alternative supplier's capabilities, not this £35.31 cost or its delivery performance. The £72 price, £35.31 fulfilment, 8% refunds, 2% replacements and £2.50 order labour are still planning inputs.

## What the diagnostic can earn

Organic orders are not a prerequisite. Paid exposure can be justified when the exact sample and repeat inspection pass, the offer is fulfilable, cheap usability checks have removed blocking faults, attribution works, and a specific buyer-access question remains. It needs an affordable accepted learning loss and enough working cash for any orders it creates. This research authorizes none of those external actions.

Use one platform, one crew, one delivered price, one page and up to two concepts over a chosen seven-day window, stopping sooner at £75 or any material fault. Confirm the platform can enforce that spend before launch. Calendar days diagnose delivery and purchase friction; they do not mature returns, establish recurrence or validate subscription demand. Do not automatically increase budgets 20% because day three looks good.

At the inherited expected-loss model, spending £75 requires at least three initial orders to cover variable costs and traffic, four to leave £5 per order, or ten to leave £18. For £100 the counts are four, five and thirteen; for £250 they are ten, twelve and thirty-two. These are arithmetic hurdles, not a statistical sample-size calculation. One actual refund in a small cohort can change the result sharply. CPC, purchase rate and reachable audience remain unknown.

For example, after £75 traffic, five initial crew orders imply £15 ad CAC and £11.03 unit contribution after ad cost. Yet with £148.22 sample/store/domain costs and twenty setup hours, the modeled round still loses £80.55 cash or £393.05 after all valued labour. Ten orders imply £7.50 ad CAC and £18.53 unit contribution, while the round shows £62.12 cash profit and a £262.88 economic loss. Those counts are illustrations; they are not plausible-volume claims.

Define portfolio economic profit as retained receipts minus actual fulfilment/remedies, original fees, all acquisition spend, shared costs, samples, variable labour and fixed founder labour. Include stopped products and failed ads. Define economic ROI explicitly as that profit divided by those consumed cash and labour resources, excluding held reserves. The five- and ten-order examples have approximately -54.3% and -28.4% economic ROI. A later product with a positive unit margin cannot erase earlier losses from the report.

A small result can justify one specific repair or a separately capped repeat. It can also leave demand unresolved because few relevant people saw the offer. It cannot warrant scaling without reconciled cohorts, actual costs, funded remedies and evidence that the next spend can recover its own costs. The fast-build method is useful when it saves page-production time for this sequence. Its reported store speed supplies no evidence for the quality, demand or cash gates.
